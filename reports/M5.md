> **Correction (same day).** The numbers in the first version of this report came from a matcher with a line-chaining bug: every later line was placed relative to line 1, not to the line before it. After the fix, a strict chain did worse (a junk band in the middle pulled good matches down), so the final matcher lets any line in the chain be skipped (score floored at 0). The tables below are from that final matcher with the run-2 model (`results_run2_skip`); all variants are compared in the last section. The verdict does not change.

## Setup

- **Held-out set.** 25 manuscripts that the recogniser never saw (see M4). 207 clean one-to-one parchment images: 15 biblical manuscripts and 10 copies of multi-copy compositions.
- **Pipeline, as on 4Q9999.** M2 segmentation → the recogniser reads every detected band (no transcription is used) → per-position letter probabilities → matcher.
- **Reference.** MT (WLC, 23,213 verses, 1,197,042 letters) plus the preserved letters of all 1,001 ETCBC scrolls, **minus the fragment's own manuscript**. Final forms are folded (22 letters). Barriers at lacunae, fragment breaks and document ends stop a match from crossing gaps.
- **Score.** For each line: Σ log((P_i[c] + 0.02) / E_q[P_i + 0.02]), a log-likelihood ratio against random letters with corpus frequencies. Each next line may start 15–140 letters after the previous one. Two modes: `ex` (exact spelling) and `sk` (ו and י dropped).
- **Controls.** For each fragment, 10 shuffled readings (letter positions permuted within each line) give about 2,000 null best scores. The threshold at false-positive rate α is the (1−α) quantile of the null scores, pooled, or taken per band of read letters.
- **Two measures of identification:**
  1. *Passage.* A fragment is identifiable when its own transcription has a preserved run of ≥ 10 letters (≥ 10 skeleton letters in `sk`) that occurs exactly outside its manuscript. That occurrence is the truth, and the top hit must land on the same passage (same chapter, verses within 3; synoptic parallels such as Ps 18 = 2 Sam 22 count). This measure is strict: 42 of the 207 images qualify.
  2. *Own book* (biblical manuscripts only, 121 images). The top hit must land in the manuscript's own book. This works for small fragments too. Chance is the book's share of MT, about 6%.

The pooled threshold is too strict for short readings, because the null scores of long readings dominate it. Per-band thresholds (above) are the ones M7 uses.

Skeleton mode (`sk`) is weaker (13 of 42 at 1% in the first run). Dropping ו/י removes letters that the recogniser reads well.

### False positives

- By construction, 1% of the shuffled controls pass the threshold.
- Real fragments with no ≥10-letter parallel that still pass the pooled threshold: 2–4 of 165, depending on the variant. When inspected, all but one land in the manuscript's own book, at passages consistent with it: 4Q10 → Gen 2:1–3 (the reading `ם אשר / ברך אלי / למלאכתו`), 4Q82 → Amos 5:12, 4Q9 → Gen 42:37–38. The strict ground truth misses these because the transcription has no 10-letter run. The apparently false ones are 4Q368 → Ezek 45:22 (run 1, just above the threshold) and 4Q85 → Lev 22:25 (run 2). **That is about 1 in 165 per run (0.6%).**
- The 4Q9 case also exposed a pairing error. The photo B-366516 shows 4–5 lines, which the recogniser read as Gen 42:37–38 (score 62.9), but its catalogue-linked transcription has one line. The photo shows a different piece of 4Q9 than the link claims.

### Where it fails

- Fragments where under 10 letters are read carry no signal (own-book rate = chance). About half of the test images are in this band.
- Large assembly images (4Q252, 4Q7) fail at segmentation: 3–7 letters are read from pieces whose transcriptions hold hundreds of letters.
- Non-biblical texts identify worse. Their parallels are other fragmentary copies, so the text to hit is short and full of gaps. Only 4Q259 → 1QS-type and Pseudo-Daniel cases have long enough parallels.

## Verdict against the brief's rule

The brief says to stop if identification is poor for fragments with 10 or more letters, or if false positives exceed about 1%.

- **False positives: within the limit.** The rate is 1% by construction on the controls, and about 0.6% on real fragments without a parallel.
- **Identification: not poor from about 20 read letters upward.** In the biblical test, 20 of 21 above-threshold hits with 20+ letters read land in the right book, and most land on the right passage. At 10–19 letters the signal is real but weaker (57% top-1 in the own book against 6% chance; 4 of 4 above-threshold hits correct, 5 of 7 in run 1). Below 10 letters there is no signal.

**Decision: continue to M6 and M7, with narrow expectations.** Only 4Q9999 fragments from which the recogniser reads at least about 15–20 letters are worth matching. From the brief's description (3–5 legible letters per line), that is probably only the top part of the `ink_rank.json` list, and fragments of about three or more lines.

## Changes to the plan

- **M6.** Set thresholds per band of read letters (already done here), turn the null quantiles into an empirical p-value or E-value per fragment, and report the own-book / right-passage precision measured here next to each candidate.
- **M6.** The matcher has no insertions or deletions yet. One missed or extra letter shifts the rest of the line. Add a banded alignment that allows gaps.
- **M7.** Skip targets with fewer than 10 read letters in total (no signal), and rank the rest by read letters.
- **Recogniser.** More data should matter more than cleaning (run 2 ≈ run 1). The `shared` text fragments are the obvious source.
