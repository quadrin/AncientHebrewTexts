# M4 Train

Date: 2026-09-24. Code: `src/split.py`, `src/train.py`, `src/recognise.py` (inference with per-position probabilities), `src/ctc_clean.py` (the M3 cleaning step). Models and logs stay in `data/m4/` (not committed). The split is committed as `reports/M4_split.json`.

## Split (by whole manuscript)

- **Test: 25 manuscripts, 4,902 letters, held out for M5.** Every test manuscript needs a parallel text outside itself: 15 biblical manuscripts (60–800 letters each), plus one copy each of 10 compositions that survive in several manuscripts (Mysteries 4Q301, Community Rule 4Q259, Damascus Document 4Q270, Pseudo-Daniel 4Q243, Apocryphal Pentateuch 4Q368, and others). Some Leon Levy composition names are loose ("Hymn", "Sapiential-Didactic Work"), so M5 takes its ground truth from the text itself.
- **Validation:** 27 manuscripts (8% of the rest), 3,510 letters.
- **Train:** 309 manuscripts, 59,000 letters. Lines marked `has_gap` are not used, which leaves 5,255 lines and 46,884 letters.

## Model

- **Network:** a CRNN. Seven 3×3 conv blocks with batch norm reduce the height from 64 to 1 and the width by ¼. Then a 2-layer BiLSTM (192 per direction) and a softmax over 27 letter forms, space and the CTC blank.
- **Input:** line crops scaled to 64 px high (width at most 1,200 px). Pixels outside the mask are filled with the line's parchment tone.
- **Labels:** reversed for training (Hebrew runs right to left; the network reads left to right) and reversed back when decoding.
- **Augmentation:** contrast and fading, stroke thinning or thickening (dilate/erode), blur, noise, dark and bright blobs ("holes" and flaking), shear, small rotation, vertical shift, horizontal scale.
- **Training:** AdamW, one-cycle learning rate up to 1e-3, batch 16 bucketed by width. CPU only (4 threads): about 115 s per epoch.
- **Output (`recognise.py`):** per band, the CTC best path. For each emitted letter, the mean softmax over its frames (blank removed, renormalised), top 5 (probabilities of at least 0.01). This is the per-position distribution the brief asks for.

## Results

### Run 1 (50 epochs, tiers A + B)

The validation CER is **0.386** at best (0.408 without spaces). It stops improving after about 25 epochs, while the training loss keeps falling. On the training lines without augmentation the CER is 0.261.

The validation labels are weak labels, so the CER includes label errors. On 14 random validation lines, the misreadings fall into three kinds:

- letters dropped at the ends of lines, where the fragment edge cuts a letter and the editors mark it uncertain;
- confusions between similar forms (ב/כ, ו/י, ה/ח, מ/פ);
- one crop/label mismatch (4Q62, where the model reads one letter against a 20-letter label).

When the model reads a letter, it usually reads it correctly (`והיו עמי ה`, `בעולה מרחם`, `ראל שנתי`), and its confidence is low where it is wrong.

### CTC cleaning (the brief's M3 step)

The run-1 model gave a loss per symbol for each training line, and the worst 15% were dropped: 788 lines, 6,948 letters (loss ≥ 1.81). The dropped lines are the ones expected to carry bad labels:

| | Lines dropped |
|---|---|
| Tier A | 6% |
| Tier B | 20% |
| Width misfit above 0.3 | 21% (13% below) |
| More than 30% editor-uncertain letters | 20% (13% otherwise) |

Surviving training data: **4,467 lines, 39,936 letters.** Run 2 trains on it (results below when finished).

## Changes to the plan

- The training set is small (47k letters) and the model overfits after about 25 epochs. The next data sources, in order: the 106k letters in `shared` text fragments (they need the aligner at the piece level), papyrus (8.6k letters, a separate domain), then synthetic lines built from letter crops of confident readings.
- Kraken was not used. The small CRNN already gives usable per-position posteriors, and it fits the CPU budget.
