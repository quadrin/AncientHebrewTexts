# M6 Matcher upgrade

Date: 2026-09-24. Code: `src/pmatch.py` (the new matcher), `src/m5_eval.py`, `src/m5_report.py`, `src/m5_books.py` (the tests), `src/m7_run.py` (calibration in use). `prior_work/match.py` is kept for reference, but it is no longer used.

## What changed compared with `prior_work/match.py`

| | prior_work/match.py | pmatch.py |
|---|---|---|
| **Input** | a string with `?` and `[..]` letter classes | per-position probabilities from the recogniser (top 5) |
| **Score** | regex hit / no hit | Σ log((P_i[c] + 0.02) / E_q[P_i + 0.02]) per line: a log-likelihood ratio against random letters with corpus frequencies |
| **Reference** | MT + ETCBC scroll lines (reconstructions included) | MT (WLC) + **preserved** ETCBC letters only; barriers at lacunae, fragment breaks and document ends, so no match crosses a gap |
| **Multi-line** | each next line 15–140 letters after the previous one | the same window, chained through a sliding maximum. Each line can be skipped (score floored at 0), because junk bands (cracks, letter tips at broken edges) are common |
| **Significance** | analytic E-value (letters independent), far too optimistic | empirical: 10–20 shuffled readings per fragment, pooled per band of read letters, give a p-value; E = p × fragments searched |
| **ו/י mode** | yes | yes (`sk`), but weaker than exact mode on recognised text |
| **Own manuscript** | not handled | left out of the reference in tests, and in M7 for transcribed sets |

## Calibration

- **Null model:** shuffled readings keep the letter counts and the confidence profile of the real reading and destroy its order. So they model "a fragment of this size and quality that matches nothing".
- **Thresholds depend on size.** At 1% FP, the score threshold rises from 17.7 (1–9 letters read) to 25.5 (10–19), 39.7 (20–39) and 44.9 (40+). One pooled threshold (41.0) is far too strict for small fragments. M7 therefore compares each fragment with controls in its own read-letter band (its own 20 shuffles plus the M5 controls in that band).
- **Validation** on 121 biblical test images from held-out manuscripts (the own-book test in M5): above the 1% per-band threshold, 24 of 25 hits land in the manuscript's own book (chance about 6%).

## Tried and not adopted

- **Single-step gaps** (a letter missed or added by the recogniser), done as a DP row by row over the whole corpus: 2.3 times slower, and not better on the M5 test (own-book precision 23/24 against 24/25). The scores become less comparable across sizes (pooled 1% threshold 88.5). It stays in the code as `search(..., gapped=True)`.
- **A strict line chain** (every band must be placed): it did worse than the skippable chain on passage identification (14 against 15 of 42). Its predecessor with the bug (every line relative to line 1) scored 19 of 42, but it was wrong by construction, and the difference is within noise.

## Known limits

- **Synoptic passages** (Ps 18 / 2 Sam 22, Isa 36–39 / 2 Kgs 18–20) give two equally good hits. The queue lists the top hits, so the reviewer sees both.
- **Aramaic targets** match against Aramaic scrolls only as well as those scrolls are preserved; there is no Aramaic Bible reference beyond Daniel and Ezra.
- **Uncorrected E-values:** the E-value counts the fragments searched, but not the several hits a reviewer looks at per fragment. Treat E < 0.1 as a lead and E around 1 as noise.
