## How the join works

- **Image → SQE.** Leon Levy image `(plate_numbers, frag_num, side)` = SQE match `(catalogueNumber1, catalogueNumber2, catalogSide)`, i.e. the IAA plate and fragment number. The Leon Levy plate loses its `/Rec` or `/Vrs` suffix; the manuscript must also agree. Three link types:
  - `exact`: same object, side and manuscript (8,038 links).
  - `subsiglum`: Leon Levy files sub-manuscripts under the parent (images labelled `4Q249` are matched in SQE to `4Q249z`; `4Q418` → `4Q418a`; `4Q324` → `4Q324d`) (410 links).
  - `side_swapped`: same object and manuscript, opposite side. This happens on opisthographs (4Q503/4Q512, 4Q496/4Q506/4Q509), where the two sites disagree on which side is the recto (62 links).
  - Nahal Hever "star" plates (`*104`) share numbers with ordinary IAA plates. They are accepted only on an exact manuscript match. That rejects about 180 false collisions.
  - SQE matches with `confirmed: false` (160 images) are dropped.
- **SQE → ETCBC.** Normalise the manuscript siglum (case, spaces, `.`, `^`; alias `11QT^a^` = `11Q19`) and the text fragment name (`frg. 13 i_14` → `f13i_14`, `col. 6a` → `6a`). 98.6% of SQE text-fragment names in scrolls that ETCBC also has resolve to an ETCBC fragment. The SQE edition texts appear to share the Abegg base text with ETCBC.
- **Script filter.** Uses the Leon Levy `script_type` and `script_language`. Excluded: Greek (64 manuscripts), Nabatean (10), Paleo-Hebrew (7), Cryptic A (6), Latin (4), Cryptic C (1), Arabic (1), `Square?` (1), and blank or `Unidentified` language (24). One manuscript with a blank script type and a Hebrew/Aramaic language is kept and flagged.

Early look at M1 (identified, recto only):

- 6,636 recto images link to at least one ETCBC fragment.
- **4,132 are 1:1** (the image carries exactly one text fragment and that text fragment appears on no other recto image). Together they hold **110,737 preserved letters** (Hebrew 99,348, Aramaic 11,389; Herodian 54,291, Hasmonean 39,274). 4,043 of them are `exact` links.
- Letters per 1:1 pair: median 9, 75th percentile 22, 90th percentile 58. Most training fragments are small, like the targets.
- 779 text fragments span more than one recto image (joins such as `f1_4`) and hold 98,568 letters. M1 has to split these by fragment position or leave them out.

Where the rest of ETCBC goes. 471 ETCBC scrolls never appear in a linked image. The biggest are the Cave 1 scrolls kept outside the IAA (1QIsa^a, 1QS, 1QH^a, 1QM, 1QpHab), 11Q19 and CD, plus the `PAM 43.6xx` groupings. That leaves roughly 211k preserved letters with text but no Leon Levy image. They are still useful for the matcher and a language model, but not for training the recogniser.

## Target pool

- 120 usable manuscripts are unidentified (`Unidentified Text` or `xQ9999`): 2,459 IR images, 2,208 of them on parchment.
- 4Q9999: 2,023 IR images, all recto, language `Hebrew?`, parchment. All 1,999 names in `prior_work/ink_rank.json` belong to it.
- Other named sets: 4Q584 (Aramaic, 25 images), 4Q585 (Aramaic, 27), 3Q14 (24, of which 20 link to ETCBC), 4Q282 (22; ETCBC splits it into 4Q282a–t, so the SQE matches do not resolve), 11Q30 (18, of which 15 link).
- **4Q363 is Cryptic C script** per Leon Levy. The hard constraints exclude it, so it drops off the M7 list in the brief.
- **11Q9999** (75 images) has a blank language in Leon Levy, so it is excluded. Someone should check its script by eye before M7.

## Licence notes found

- SQE catalogue matches point to IAA IIIF images (`iaa.iiifhosting.com`) under a **CC BY-NC 4.0** licence statement, which is looser than the Leon Levy terms in the brief. Because the brief still says to keep all images out of git, nothing about the image-handling rules changes. But the IIIF source may be a cleaner way to fetch images in M2. Check the terms before relying on it.
- SQE edition copyright statements are stored per edition in `data/m0/sqe_editions.json` (for example 1Q1: CC BY-SA 4.0, Kratz et al.).
