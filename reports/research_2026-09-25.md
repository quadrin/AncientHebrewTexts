# Better ways to read and identify Dead Sea Scroll fragments: research report

Date: 2026-09-25. Written for the AncientHebrewTexts repo (`quadrin/AncientHebrewTexts`, main, reports M0–M9).

This report builds on reports M5, M7, M8, the M9_* files and M9_audit_forward.md. It repeats a result from those reports only when a recommendation depends on it.

## How this report was made, and its limits

- **Literature.** Eight parallel literature searches, then two fact-check passes that re-fetched about 33 of the key numbers from their primary sources (Section 7 lists the corrections).
- **Direct probe of the SQE API** (2026-09-25). All 1,369 public editions and their image catalogue were queried. The main new facts in Section A1 come from this probe, not from the literature.
- **Search budget.** The session used up its web-search budget partway through. After that, only known URLs could be fetched.
  - Coverage of non-English sources is therefore thin. A few Hebrew and German pages were read (Ynet, Makor Rishon, Qumran-Digital). No Italian or French sources were searched. Treat that part of the brief as **not done**.
- **Verification labels.** Each claim carries one of these:
  - **[V]**: checked against the primary source.
  - **[P]**: partly checked, for example a truncated page or only the abstract.
  - **[NV]**: not verified; from memory or my own estimate.
  - **[ANNOUNCED]**: stated but not released.

| Same, at p ≤ 0.01 | 0 in every band | same |
| Top-1 hit in own book (no threshold) | 10–19: 12/21 · 20–39: 9/14 · 40+: 21/24 | same |

The last two rows show the main gap. At 40+ letters the top-ranked book is right 21 times in 24, but only 7 of those 24 pass p ≤ 0.05. The ranking is good. The calibration, as built, cannot express much of that evidence. Three mechanical reasons, found in `src/m5_decoy.py`:

- **Resolution floor.** p = (1 + k)/(n + 1) over the decoys whose read-letter count is within ×1.5 of the piece's. p ≤ 0.01 is impossible unless at least 99 decoys fall in that window. This may fail for long readings [NV: n per piece is not in the committed files]. Report n per piece.
- **Decoys come only from manuscripts outside test and validation**, i.e. mostly training manuscripts, which the recogniser reads more cleanly. That makes the null heavier than it should be.
- **Decoys may share 6–7-letter runs with the corpus** (`DECOY_RUN = 8`).

Experiment E4 addresses all three.

**Actions outside the code** (cheap, possibly high value; details in Section 6):

- **IAA: ask for the other 11 single bands** (7 visible, 4 NIR) for a pilot set of about 200 pieces, after a public-data baseline on colour, IR and raking (E3e). Raking IR and versos are already public through SQE (A1). An IAA paper states that non-public exposures are "available for free to scholars on request" [V].
- **SQE / Tel Aviv / EPHE group** (Stökl Ben Ezra, Brown-deVost, Dershowitz): ask for three things:
  - the 1,278 hand-segmented letters;
  - their 2020–22 automatic line and letter alignments;
  - the origin of the public ROIs.

**What not to do now:**

- Self-supervised pretraining on about 2k fragment images: too little data. Transfer from labelled data beat it in the ICDAR 2024 study [V].
- GAN or diffusion handwriting generation: the gains show only below a few hundred real lines [P].
- Page-level models (DAN family): need too much data.
- CLIP-style image↔passage embeddings: no precedent, and not enough pairs.
- VLM-generated labels used directly as training targets: memorised text leaks in.
- Fusing a character language model into the posteriors that the matcher reads: it biases the evidence toward the reference texts.

**What no method will fix.** A fragment with 3 confidently read letters carries at most about 7.5 nats of evidence (about 2.5 nats per letter on average under the unigram background). The 1.85M-letter reference (MT 1.20M + preserved ETCBC letters 0.65M) costs about 14.4 nats of multiplicity before any spacing choices; the targums add 2M letters for Aramaic targets. No reader and no statistic will make most of 4Q9999 significant by text alone. Gains come from four places:

- more letters read per piece (items 3–5);
- less waste of the evidence (item 1);
- pooling pieces (item 1, joint placement);
- physical evidence such as hand, line spacing and verso.

**The image side (same probe).**

- `GET /v1/imaged-objects/{IAA-plate-frag}` lists IIIF images on `iaa.iiifhosting.com`. A typical fragment has 8 images (7.9 on average):
  - colour (445–638 nm composite, marked as the "master" image);
  - IR 924 nm direct;
  - IR 924 nm **raking left**;
  - IR 924 nm **raking right**;
  - each of these for **recto and verso**.
- All are 7216 × 5412 px at 1,215 ppi, with tiles of 256 px.
- `GET /v1/imaged-objects/institutions/IAA` lists 157,771 images for 19,998 imaged objects.
- This includes the 4Q9999 lead piece IAA-202-19 (B-359582): 8 images, raking on both sides.

**Two consequences for the brief.**

- The brief says public data gives one IR and one colour composite per photo. That is true for the Leon Levy site, but **raking-light IR and every verso are also public** through SQE's IIIF server. The raking images can be used for edge letters (surface relief, ink loss, ruling lines) and for the failed verso-ink test (M9: AUC 0.41–0.68 over its four signals).
- The SQE catalogue record gives the images as "CC BY-NC 4.0 … non-commercial uses such as lectures, public presentations". The Leon Levy site allows single copies for private use only. Keep the stricter rule of your brief (no redistribution) and ask the IAA which terms apply.

**How to use the ROIs.** They are too few to train on (3.4k letters against 47–72k), but they look clean: letter-sized, and inside the fragment masks. Use them in four ways:

1. **Candidate gold standard for alignment.** Measure whether `align.py`, `align_self.py` and any new aligner put each letter in the right band, and how far off its x-position is. This measures the "guess" in the brief directly.
2. **Letter-level recogniser test.** Measure per-letter accuracy and calibration, split by position in the line (inside or at an edge), and by partial versus whole letters.
3. **Partial-letter labels.** A polygon cut by the fragment mask marks a partial letter. This is exactly the label type M8 says is missing.
4. **Registration check.** Test whether the Leon Levy "B-" photos and the SQE IIIF images are the same captures. Overlay the ROIs on your M2 crops. You must know the scale and offset before you can use any of this.

A minimal harvester is in Appendix A. The coordinate frame needs one check by overlay: the ROIs lie inside the artefact masks, which are in the frame of the imaged object's master image (7216 × 5412). Confirm on 5 pieces before bulk use.

**What SQE was built for.** Its data model stores a polygon for every sign reading, and allows alternative readings of the same ink ([Scrollery project overview](https://github.com/Scripta-Qumranica-Electronica/Scrollery-website/blob/master/docs/Project_overview.md)) [V]. A 2022 abstract promised "future inclusion in the SQE database of the positions of lines, words and letters on the IAA photos" ([IOQS abstract](http://www.cs.tau.ac.il/%7Enachumd/papers/IOQS.pdf)) [ANNOUNCED]. The database is dated 2022-11-01, so a bulk import has not happened. The ScrollEditor, API and database repos were updated in August–September 2026, so the project is active ([GitHub org](https://github.com/Scripta-Qumranica-Electronica)) [V]. A 921.6 MB Docker dump of "all the base data" exists ([Docker Hub](https://hub.docker.com/r/qumranica/sqe-database)) [V]. It may hold more ROIs, but deployment "sanitizes data" [P]. Checking its `roi_shape` table is about one hour of work.

### A4. Can a strong VLM produce better training labels?

**Evidence.** LLMs used as post-correctors bring CER on 18th–19th-century English documents from 5.7–7% down to as low as 1.8% ([Humphries et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.03340)) [V]. On German city directories a multimodal LLM gets below 1% CER ([Greif et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00414)) [V]. Other results are less favourable:

- LLMs showed "limited ability to autonomously correct errors" ([Crosilla et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.15195)) [V].
- Zero-shot VLMs produce "contextually plausible" invented text. In CHURRO, 36% of 50 zero-shot Qwen2.5-VL-3B outputs had major hallucinations ([arXiv 2509.19768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19768)) [V].
- No study tests a VLM on DSS images [P].

**Assessment.** Do not use VLM readings as training labels. A model that has memorised the Bible and the sectarian texts will "read" reconstructed letters that are not on the parchment. This is your reconstructed-letter problem in a worse form.

Two narrow uses are worth one test each (proposals, no precedent found):

1. **VLM as arbiter, not author.** Show one band image and the 2–5 candidate editor lines of that fragment. Ask which line it shows, and whether the first and last letters are complete, partial or missing. The label text still comes from the editors. The VLM only fills the line-to-band and edge-state gaps. Validate against the SQE ROIs (A1).
2. **VLM as second reader in label cleaning.** Where the CRNN and the editors disagree, a third opinion from a different model family can flag bad crops (like the 4Q62 case in M4). Do not use it to create labels.

**The failure mode that matters most for you.** A VLM that reverts to a text it remembers (Masoretic Text, 1QS) will produce false matches with high scores. That breaks the matcher's statistics. No study measures this on scroll images [NV]. Experiment E1 measures it with the repo's own variant list (`M9_variants.json`: 2,069 substantive and 5,695 orthographic word differences from the MT).

**Tokens.** A Hebrew letter is 2 bytes in UTF-8. Byte-level BPE may merge several letters into one token or split one letter across tokens, so per-token log-probs are not per-letter probabilities. Two fixes:

- With open models, constrain decoding to single-letter tokens (or sum over a prefix trie of token sequences).
- With APIs, ask for letters separated by spaces and check how the model's tokenizer splits them.

**How the matcher can use a plain-text reading.** Your matcher needs P_i per position. Four ways to build it:

1. **Noisy channel (recommended first).** On the validation lines, estimate P(read | true) for each VLM, with a deletion column and a "?" (unreadable) column, split by position class: inside the line, first/last letter, next to a lacuna. Turn each output letter r into P(true | r) ∝ P(r | true) · P(true). Cross-fit: estimate on half the manuscripts, apply to the other half.
   - Greek-BLAST used this idea: a substitution matrix (GLOSUM) learned from human reading confusions ([Williams et al. 2014](https://acwio.github.io/pubs/escience2014.pdf)) [V].
2. **Sampling.** Take N = 5–10 samples at temperature > 0, or N test-time-augmentation passes. Align them and use each column's letter frequencies. Then apply temperature scaling on validation data.
3. **Product of experts with the CRNN.** After aligning the two readings, multiply calibrated distributions, then recalibrate. The errors are correlated, so fit a single weight on validation.
4. **Down-weight VLM letters in low-ink spans** (edges, lacunae), where invented text concentrates ([Gardella et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24077)) [P].

**The main lesson.** No project found in this search computes calibrated match significance [P: the search was incomplete]. They all rank candidates and hand the top of the list to experts. Your decoy calibration is ahead of these projects. What they have, and you lack, is a record of **open-world yield per 100 candidates reviewed**. Closed-world benchmark scores do not predict open-world precision:

- eBL: P@3 0.94 on synthetic fragments that were guaranteed to have a source, but about 1% confirmed finds among the top 300 real candidates.
- Genizah join finder: AUC 0.98 on the benchmark (a ranking measure, not precision), but 13–24% true joins among the top real candidate pairs.

### C9. Cuneiform

- **eBL alignment** ([ebl-api](https://github.com/ElectronicBabylonianLiterature/ebl-api), MIT) [V].
  - Method: global sequence alignment of a fragment's transliteration against reconstructed chapters.
  - Scoring:
    - match +16, mismatch −5, gap −5/−1;
    - an unknown sign "x" scores +3 on a match;
    - a line break scores +6 on a match, −10 on a mismatch;
    - **32 hand-listed pairs of confusable signs score +7** ("common mismatch").
  - Results are sorted by raw score, with no null model.
  - **Lesson:** the line-break reward is your multi-line constraint. The confusable-pair bonus is a hand-made confusion matrix; you can learn yours from validation data (C14).
- **eBL n-gram matcher** (Simonjetz, Laasonen, Cobanoglu, Fraser, Jiménez; [LREC-COLING 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.lrec-main.1197.pdf)) [V].
  - All manuscripts of a chapter are pooled into one n-gram set, scored by overlap + TF-IDF + a length weight (n = 1–5).
  - P@3 by method: 0.94 for this scoring, against 0.79 for Needleman–Wunsch and 0.90 for LCS. It runs in seconds; Needleman–Wunsch takes about 24 h.
  - The authors warn that in the real world most fragments "belong to no chapter at all".
  - **Lessons:**
    - Pool all witnesses of a passage (MT, scroll copies, targum) into one variant-tolerant index.
    - IDF down-weights common strings, which targets your אשר / ויאמר problem.
    - Always test open-world.
- **eBL OCR at scale** (Che et al., [arXiv 2606.22608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.22608)) [V].
  - A Deformable DETR detector trained on 124,504 labelled signs, then run on 87,668 fragments.
  - Its readings share on average only **0.232** of their 1–3-grams with the expert transliterations.
  - **Lesson:** the field's best automatic readers are as noisy as yours. The field matches human transliterations, not machine readings.
- **DeepScribe** (Persepolis Fortification tablets; [arXiv 2306.01268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01268); [code](https://github.com/edwardclem/deepscribe), GPL-3.0) [V].
  - Detection mAP@50 0.78. Classification top-1 0.692, **top-5 0.902**. End-to-end CER 0.683.
  - Recall on rare signs is near zero.
  - Experts valued the ranked top-5 output.
  - **Lesson:** top-k output is what makes a weak reader useful, which is the same design as your matcher.
- **Weak supervision from transliterations** (Dencker et al., [PLOS ONE 2020](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0243039)): 45.3 mAP → 63.2 with 745 manual annotations [V]. See A3.

  - A few hand-labelled "crackle" traces bootstrap a model. Its new detections become labels, and the loop repeats.
  - Grand Prize model: an ensemble of TimeSformer, ResNet3D and I3D, with about 15 rounds of label expansion ([repo](https://github.com/younader/Vesuvius-Grandprize-Winner), MIT).

    - the input window (256 px ≈ 614 µm) is "smaller than all letters";
    - the detector is "not trained on character identities … no language model";
    - strong label smoothing;
    - a reading must be stable over repeated passes and endorsed by several papyrologists.
- **What transfers to 2D IR photos of parchment:**
  1. **Keep a letter-blind ink detector separate from the letter reader and from the matcher.** Evidence (ink) stays separate from interpretation (letters, texts). Your M9 `traces.py` test failed with the letter recogniser. A pixel ink map, trained without letter labels, is the Vesuvius answer for faint edges.
  2. Seed labels from confident letters (and the SQE ROI polygons), self-train with a receptive field smaller than one letter, hold out whole fragments, and stop when held-out quality levels off (~5 rounds).
  3. Accept a faint edge letter only if it is stable across passes, test-time augmentations and ensemble members.
- **What does not transfer:** the 3D crackle signal. Raking light (A1) is your nearest substitute for surface relief.

### C14. Statistical analogues, and what carries over to the matcher

**A fact about your score.** Your per-position score is s_i(c) = ln[(P_i[c] + ε) / Σ_c′ q(c′)(P_i[c′] + ε)]. So Σ_c q(c)·e^{s_i(c)} = 1 **exactly**: each position is a likelihood ratio with mean 1 under i.i.d. background letters.

- For one offset, P(S_o ≥ s) ≤ e^{−s} (Markov's inequality).
- Over all offsets, spacings and skips, P(max S ≥ s) ≤ N_eff·e^{−s}.
- This is the Karlin–Altschul form E = K·N·e^{−λS} with λ = 1 nat and K ≤ 1. The same holds under the 4-gram background, as a martingale bound.
- HMMER uses this idea: probabilistic scores have a known λ, and only a location parameter is fitted by simulation ([Eddy 2008](https://journals.plos.org/ploscompbiol/article?id=10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000069)) [V abstract].

Real Hebrew is not i.i.d., and formulae longer than 4-grams make the true null heavier. So use decoys to fit two numbers (λ̂ ≤ 1, K̂ ≥ 1) per information level, not a full empirical distribution per band. Two parameters can be estimated well from 1,500 decoys, and p-values can then go far below 1/N_band. N_eff is roughly corpus letters × spacing choices (~126 per extra line) × skip patterns. That is ln N ≈ 14.4 nats for the 1.85M-letter Hebrew reference (≈ 15.1 with the targums), plus ~4.8 nats per extra line. This explains the ~20-letter threshold: a confidently read letter earns ln(1.02/(q + 0.02)) under the unigram background. That is about 2.0 nats for ו/י, up to about 3.7 for rare letters, and about 2.5 on average. Under the 4-gram background it often earns only about 1 nat.

## 5. Four experiments to run next

All four use the existing evaluation sets:

- **Validation CER:** the M4 validation lines (281 lines, 27 manuscripts), with the two clean subsets from M8 (79 lines with no editor-uncertain letters; 32 lines / 198 letters in tier A with none).
- **Decoy-calibrated M5 own-book rate:** `src/m5_decoy.py`, biblical held-out pieces, own book at decoy p ≤ 0.05 and ≤ 0.01, by read-letter band. Baseline: pooled 10+ letters is 13/59 at p ≤ 0.05 and 0/59 at p ≤ 0.01.

### E1. Frontier VLMs against the recogniser (E1a), and direct passage selection (E1b)

**E1a: reading head-to-head.**

- **Readers:**
  - baseline: CRNN run 4 (96 px);
  - **Gemini 3.1 Pro**, the strongest published handwriting results;
  - **Claude Opus 5.5**, strong in the Humphries tests and with zero-data-retention available on synchronous calls;
  - **GPT-6 Sol** with reasoning `none`, the only frontier route that may give token log-probs;
  - optional: an open model (CHURRO-3B or Qwen3-VL-8B through vLLM, output restricted to the 22 letters) for true logits.
- **Input:** the same M3 line crops the CRNN reads, contrast-normalised, upscaled ×2. Second condition: the whole fragment crop with the target line boxed, because context helps humans read damaged letters.
- **Prompt:**
  - Hebrew/Aramaic square script on damaged parchment, infrared photograph.
  - Consonants only, in reading order; `?` for an unidentifiable trace.
  - "Do not restore missing text or complete words from memory."
  - Three fixed few-shot line images with gold labels, from train manuscripts.
  - JSON output: per letter, top-3 candidates with confidence.
  - One call at temperature 0, then 5 samples at temperature 0.7 for letter distributions.
- **Measures:**
  1. CER (letters only, final forms folded, `?` = substitution) on all 281 lines and on the two clean subsets. Line-bootstrap 95% intervals and a paired difference against CRNN run 4.
  2. **Insertion rate** on lines whose label contains a lacuna: letters the reader invents where the editors saw none.
  3. **MT-reversion rate:** on lines with a known scroll-versus-MT variant (from `src/variants.py`), the share of variant sites where the reader outputs the MT form. Count the variant sites in the validation lines first. If there are fewer than about 50, add variant lines from training manuscripts: this measure tests the VLM, which has never seen them, not the CRNN.
  4. Calibration (ECE, Brier) of stated confidence and of sampled frequencies.
  5. End-to-end: convert each VLM reading to P_i with a noisy-channel model estimated on half of the validation manuscripts (B5), read the decoy pieces the same way, and run `m5_decoy.py`.
- **Success:**
  - A VLM becomes a second reader if it beats CRNN run 4 by ≥ 0.05 absolute CER on the clean subsets (interval excludes 0), with MT reversion ≤ 5% of variant sites and insertion rate no higher than the CRNN's.
  - It enters the matcher if the end-to-end own-book count at p ≤ 0.05 (pooled 10+ letters) rises by ≥ 5 over 13/59, with held-out decoy false positives at nominal.
- **Stop rule:** if every VLM is worse than the CRNN on all subsets and reverts to the MT at > 10% of variant sites, drop the VLM reader track.
- **Optional arm:** the documentary texts (Murabba'at, Masada, Naḥal Ḥever), where the CRNN gives only 18.5% usable lines against 57% for literary texts (`M9_doc_test.json`). A VLM may do relatively better on cursive hands. This is untested.
- **Cost:** 12 calls per line (1 + 5 samples, × 2 input conditions).
  - With the 3 few-shot images (about +2,100 input tokens per call), the CER test costs about $60 per model at Opus 5.5 prices without caching, or about $35 with the few-shot prefix cached. It is less for the others, e.g. about $30 for Gemini 3.1 Pro.
  - About $15–60 per model for the end-to-end run (~6k lines of test and decoy pieces).
  - Under $400 in total. Code: new `src/vlm_read.py`, and a noisy-channel adapter in `pmatch.py`.

**E1b: pick the passage (20 candidates).**

- **Items:** held-out M5 biblical pieces with a known passage, plus crops of 1–3 lines cut from larger pieces of test manuscripts, to fill the bands. At least **40 items per band** of CRNN letters read (1–9, 10–19, 20–39, 40+); 100 per band is better. Strip file names and metadata.
- **Candidates:** 20 consonantal windows of equal length (true span × 1.3), all normalised the same way (MT consonants, final forms folded, no verse numbers). If only the true window keeps scroll spelling, the model can find it without the image. Three arms:
  - **A**, matched random: other windows from the same book;
  - **B**, hard: the matcher's top 19 wrong hits;
  - **C**, letter-consistent: windows that contain the CRNN's read letters. This arm tests whether the image adds anything beyond the CRNN reading.
  - In every arm, add a 21st option, "none of these", so the same test can run on **decoy pieces**, where no candidate is true.
- **Controls:**
  - text only (no image);
  - wrong image (another piece from the same band);
  - an open recall prompt with no candidates. Any hit on a piece with fewer than 10 letters flags memorisation.
- **Protocol:** random numeric option IDs, 5 random orders per item, majority vote, and a top-5 ranking. Models: the two best from E1a.
- **Measures:**
  - top-1 accuracy by band (chance 5%) with Wilson intervals;
  - the paired difference against the matcher's own top-1 on the same candidate set;
  - the "false pick" rate on decoy pieces (the model chooses a candidate when "none of these" is true).
- **Sample size** [NV, own calculation]:
  - Against chance: n ≈ 16 detects 30% vs 5% (α = 0.05, power 0.9), and n ≈ 38 detects 20%. So 40 per band is enough to screen against chance.
  - For the paired comparison with the matcher (a ≥ 15-point gain, McNemar), about 90–150 items per band are needed.
  - Filler crops come from the same few test manuscripts, so bootstrap by manuscript.
- **Success:** in the 10–19 and 20–39 bands, arm B top-1 beats the matcher's by ≥ 15 points (McNemar p < 0.05), with the wrong-image control ≤ 8% and a false-pick rate ≤ 5% on decoys. Only then consider the VLM as a re-ranker of the matcher's top 20.
- **Calibration:** a 20-way choice cannot give a p-value below 0.05 per piece. For p-values that can be compared with decoy calibration, score each candidate with the open model as log P(candidate | image) − log P(candidate | blank image), and calibrate that score on the decoys like any other matcher score.
- **Cost:** ≈ $50–100 per model with 3 arms and 5 orders. Code: new `src/vlm_choice.py`.

### E2. Gold letters from SQE, whole-fragment CTC alignment, and a wildcard loss

1. **Harvest the gold** (`src/sqe_rois.py`, Appendix A): 3,403 letters with polygons on 351 IAA pieces.
   - Join each imaged object (IAA plate–fragment–side) to your Leon Levy image by the M0/M1 catalogue join.
   - Register SQE master coordinates (7216 × 5412) to the M2 crop frame, checking by overlay on 5 pieces.
   - Split the gold by your M4 manuscript split.
2. **Measure the current aligners** (`align.py`, `align_self.py`) on the gold from train manuscripts:
   - band-assignment accuracy of gold letters;
   - coverage: the share of visible gold letters inside a labelled span;
   - label error rate.
   This replaces the "alignment is a guess" statement with numbers.
3. **Build `src/align_ctc.py`:** per fragment, concatenate the band posteriors (run 4). Align the fragment's full preserved transcription as one finite-state graph with:
   - arcs that skip a band (extra text or noise);
   - arcs that skip a line (edge tips);
   - a bounded wildcard for each reconstructed stretch;
   - wildcards at band ends.
   Output label spans per band with γ and γ₆ confidences. Iterate align → train → align up to 3 times, and stop when the gold scores level off (Bullinger, Vesuvius: 3–5 rounds).
4. **Train run 5** (`train.py`):
   - CTC on fully aligned lines;
   - **W-CTC** (wildcards at both ends) on partly aligned lines;
   - editor-uncertain letters as optional (STC star) or as SoftCTC variants.
- **Success:**
  - Gold band assignment ≥ 95%; gold letter located within ±1 letter width ≥ 85% (Tel Aviv: 90.3%).
  - ≥ 90k training letters with a gold-estimated label error ≤ 5%.
  - Validation CER ≤ 0.36 (all) and ≤ 0.15 (clean), from 0.396 / 0.167.
  - Deletions of first and last letters on validation no higher than run 2.
  - **Target pool: ≥ 167 images with ≥ 10 letters read** (the run-2 level; run 3 fell to 96).
  - M5 own-book count at p ≤ 0.05 (pooled 10+) not below 13/59.
- **Cost:** CPU. About 1–2 weeks of work.

### E3. Recogniser v2: pretrained start, public raking channels, small ensemble

Four runs, each compared with run 4 on the same data (the best labels from E2 if ready, else run 3's):

- **3a:** kraken PP-OCRv6 *small*, fine-tuned (`ketos train --resize union`), 128 px lines, IR only.
- **3b:** 3a plus channels from SQE IIIF: IR direct, IR raking-left, IR raking-right, and IR − blue from the colour image.
  - If the pretrained input layer is single-channel, add a learned 4→1 input projection. Alternatively, run the channel test on your own CRNN with a 4-channel input layer.
  - Registration of the SQE images to each other must be checked; they appear to be same-geometry captures [NV].
- **3c:** the best of 3a/3b as a 3-fold cross-validation ensemble, with averaged posteriors and temperature scaling on validation.
- **3d (side test):** rerun `verso_ink.py` with the raking channels on *all* versos from SQE (M9 used 590 versos, of which 364 are linked). SQE links may follow the piece, not the side (a note in `verso_ink.py`), so first hand-label about 100 versos (text or no text) as ground truth.
- **3e (band question):** the 20 QSD fragments have pixel-level ink labels and all 12 bands (A2). Fetch their public images from SQE (colour, IR, raking L/R). Train a per-pixel LDA and a small U-Net on these public channels only, and compare ink IoU with MTEM's 0.671 on all 12 bands. If the public channels come within about 0.05 IoU, the band request adds little for ink. If they fall below about 0.55, the request is justified with a number.
- **Measures:**
  - validation CER (all, clean);
  - expected calibration error (ECE) of top-1 probabilities;
  - letters read at p ≥ 0.8 on the target pool;
  - M5 decoy-calibrated own-book rate;
  - verso AUC.
- **Success:**
  - Validation CER ≤ 0.34 (all) and ≤ 0.14 (clean).
  - ECE ≤ 0.05 after scaling.
  - Pooled own-book at p ≤ 0.05 ≥ 17/59.
  - Verso-ink AUC ≥ 0.75 on the hand-labelled versos (M9 signals: 0.41–0.68).
  - If 3b does not beat 3a by ≥ 0.01 CER, the raking channels are not worth their cost for reading. They may still help verso and edge detection.
- **Cost:** about 10–30 GPU-hours (≈ $30–100 rented). The SQE image fetch is about 4 images per piece for the pieces you already use.

### E4. Matcher calibration v2

Steps (`pmatch.py`, `m5_decoy.py`, `m7_run.py`, `group.py`):

0. **Rerun the baseline** with run-4 readings for both the test pieces and the decoys. Make the readings path a parameter; it is now hard-coded to run 2. Report n (length-matched decoys) per piece.
1. **Decoy audit.** Three checks:
   - Decoys are pieces with no preserved run of ≥ 8 letters elsewhere (`DECOY_RUN = 8`), so they can still share 6–7-letter phrases and quotations with the corpus. List the top 50 decoys by score and read their hits. Remove quotations and allusions. Any real parallels found are discoveries in their own right.
   - Decoys come only from non-held-out (mostly training) manuscripts. Check whether their readings are cleaner (mean top-1 probability) than the test pieces' readings.
   - Check the ×1.5 length window: p ≤ 0.01 needs ≥ 99 decoys in the window.
2. **Source-excluded decoys:** each identified held-out piece is searched with every witness of its passage removed (MT verses, scroll copies, aligned targum verse). This gives realistic nulls that have near-neighbours.
3. **Island tail samples:** from every decoy search, keep the top non-overlapping local maxima (≥ 200 letters apart).
4. **Statistic** T = S − ln N_eff. Fit ln p = ln K − λT per bin of reading information I = Σ KL(P_i ‖ q) and number of lines. Check on held-out decoys with a QQ plot at 0.05 / 0.01 / 0.001.
5. **Rescoring:** T, the gap to the best hit in another book, lines matched ÷ lines read, spacing plausibility, the mismatch-versus-confidence likelihood ratio, and the hand prior (`prior.py`). Fit a logistic model with manuscript-grouped cross-validation, and calibrate it on a third decoy split.
6. **Forward sum** over line spacing, with a prior from the hand's letter width, in place of the maximum.
7. **LCA report:** the lowest node (corpus / group / book / chapter) with posterior mass ≥ τ. Tune τ by exclusion runs.
8. **Entrapment:** add a Hebrew corpus that cannot contain any source of a DSS fragment, of about the same size as the reference, with biblical quotations masked (for example Sefaria rabbinic text; check its licence). Report the entrapment FDP bounds for the M7 queue at q = 0.05.

- **Success:**
  - Pooled 10+ letters own-book at p ≤ 0.05 rises from 13/59 to **≥ 25/59**, and at p ≤ 0.01 from 0/59 to **≥ 10/59**. For comparison, the current top-1 count is 42/59. E4 may change the ranking. Also count a top hit on a scroll copy of the same book as own book; the current code counts only MT top hits.
  - Held-out decoys flagged at α = 0.05: ≤ 6%; at α = 0.01: ≤ 1.5%.
  - Entrapment combined FDP upper bound ≤ 0.10 at q = 0.05.
  - Book-level assignments at ≥ 95% precision for ≥ 50% of the 10–19-letter biblical pieces.
  - B-359582 (4Q12a / Gen 50:15) stays at the top of the rerun M7 queue.
- **Cost:** CPU, about 1–2 weeks. **Do this experiment first.** It needs no new data, and it decides how much the other three experiments are worth.

## Appendix A. Minimal SQE ROI harvester (sketch for `src/sqe_rois.py`)

```python
"""Harvest public SQE sign ROIs (letter polygons on IAA images). Local data only."""
import json, os, time, requests
B = 'https://sqe-api.deadseascrolls.org.il/v1'
OUT = 'data/sqe_rois'; os.makedirs(OUT, exist_ok=True)

eds = requests.get(f'{B}/editions', timeout=60).json()['editions']
eds = [e[0] if isinstance(e, list) else e for e in eds]
rows = []
for e in eds:
    sl = requests.get(f'{B}/editions/{e["id"]}/script-lines', timeout=120).json()
    if 'interpretationRoiId' not in json.dumps(sl):
        continue
    arts = {a['id']: a for a in requests.get(f'{B}/editions/{e["id"]}/artefacts', timeout=120).json()['artefacts']}
    for tf in sl['textFragments']:
        for ln in tf['lines']:
            for ar in ln['artefacts']:
                for ch in ar['characters']:
                    for r in ch.get('rois') or []:
                        a = arts.get(r['artefactId'])
                        if not a or not a.get('imagedObjectId'):
                            continue          # skip CD (Genizah) and virtual line strips
                        rows.append(dict(edition=e['name'], text_fragment=tf['textFragmentName'],
                                         line=ln['lineName'], sign=ch['signInterpretationId'],
                                         char=ch.get('character'), imaged_object=a['imagedObjectId'],
                                         side=a['side'], artefact=a['id'], shape=r['shape']))
    time.sleep(0.2)
json.dump(rows, open(f'{OUT}/rois.json', 'w'), ensure_ascii=False)
print(len(rows), 'ROIs;', len({r['sign'] for r in rows}), 'letters')
# Images of an imaged object (colour, IR, IR raking L/R; recto and verso):
#   GET {B}/imaged-objects/{imaged_object}  -> images[].url (IIIF, 7216x5412, 1215 ppi)
```

Expected output on 2026-09-25: 4,050 ROIs; 3,403 letters.
