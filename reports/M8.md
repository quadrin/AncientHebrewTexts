## Summary

- **The 4Q12a lead stands.** B-359582 is the top M7 hit again with the new model (E = 0.17), and at full resolution the reading is clearer still. A local specialist packet is ready.
- **More training data (+53% letters) did not make the recogniser better.** Validation CER stayed at 0.39. The model also reads fewer letters on the targets. The remaining error is dominated by uncertain edge letters and label noise.
- **Resolution:** a taller model input (96 px lines instead of 64, same data as run 1) lowers validation CER on every subset: 0.409 → 0.396 on all lines, and 0.217 → 0.167 on the cleanest ones. Fetching full resolution is not needed for that: the half-resolution crops are already about 230 px tall.
- **Grouping small pieces** works as a ranking in validation (the own book is 10–15× more common than chance for groups of 5–8 pieces of one manuscript). But its significance is not calibrated well enough for claims, and on 4Q9999 plates it gives no credible lead.
- **Aramaic targums** (1.98M letters) are now searched for Aramaic targets. They produce hits, but none beyond chance level.

- **Self-alignment:** the run-2 recogniser reads each band. Its reading is searched as an approximate substring of the transcription lines linked to the image (edit rate ≤ 0.35, beating the second-best line by ≥ 0.15). The label is always the editors' text, not the model's.
- **Label check:** by eye, 15 of 16 random self-aligned labels match the visible text.
- **Papyrus segmentation:** line counts agree exactly on 63% and within ±1 on 93%. That is better than parchment, because papyrus pieces are smaller.
- **Run 3** trained on 8,214 lines / 71,865 letters (run 1: 5,255 / 46,884). Validation CER (letters):

|---|---|---|---|
| All (281 lines) | 0.409 | 0.399 | 0.404 |
| Tier A (111 lines) | 0.349 | 0.339 | 0.337 |
| No editor-uncertain letters (79 lines) | 0.312 | 0.327 | 0.304 |

- **Effect on M5 (own book, biblical held-out fragments, above the per-band 1% threshold):** run 3: 5/7 (10–19 letters read), 7/7 (20–39), 14/14 (40+). Run 2: 4/4, 5/5, 15/16. So about the same. Real fragments without a ≥ 10-letter parallel that still pass the pooled 1% threshold: 7 of 165, of which 5 fit the manuscript's own content (4Q10 → Gen 2:1, 4Q9 → Gen 42:37, 4Q82 → Amos, 4Q40 → Deut 7:19, 4Q252 → Gen 48:21). A self-aligned label covers only the stretch that matched, so visible letters outside it go unlabelled, and the model learns to leave edge letters out. For M7, run 2 remains the better reader. A fix would be to keep self-aligned lines only when the match covers the band's ink width.

## 3. Resolution

- The recogniser scales every line crop to a fixed height. At 64 px (runs 1–3), the half-resolution crops (median line pitch about 165 px, crop height about 1.4 × pitch ≈ 230 px) are shrunk by a factor of about 3.6. So full resolution cannot help unless the model takes a taller input.
- **Run 4:** input height 96 px, the same training data and schedule as run 1 (tiers A + B, 40 epochs; about 250 s per epoch against 115 s). Validation CER on letters:

| Validation subset | 64 px (run 1) | 96 px (run 4) |
|---|---|---|
| All (281 lines) | 0.409 | 0.396 |
| Tier A (111 lines) | 0.349 | 0.342 |
| No editor-uncertain letters (79 lines) | 0.312 | 0.303 |
| Tier A, no uncertain letters (32 lines) | 0.217 | 0.167 |

- A taller input helps a little everywhere, and most on clean lines. The half-resolution level still gives more pixels than a 96 px model uses, so full-resolution fetching is only worth it for inputs above about 200 px. The better next step is 96 px combined with the extra training data, once the edge-label problem of the self-aligned lines is fixed.

## 4. Grouping small pieces

- **Method (`group.py`).** For each piece: its best score in each of 1,034 documents (MT books, scrolls), for the real reading and for shuffled readings. A per-document p-value comes from the pooled shuffled maxima in the same read-letter band. The group statistic is Fisher's sum of −log p per document, and the best document is the argmax.
- **Wrong control, found and replaced.** Shuffled readings are too weak a control at document level. Real readings contain real words and beat shuffled letters everywhere: 23 of 74 single small pieces passed the shuffle-based 1% threshold, and only 7 of them were correct. The replacement control is **decoy groups**: real pieces drawn from different manuscripts.
- **Validation** on held-out biblical pieces with 3–19 letters read (12 manuscripts, random groups drawn per manuscript):

| Group size | Groups | Best document = own book | Chance (decoys) | Above decoy 5%: own book | Above decoy 1%: own book |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 240 | 15 (6%) | 3.6 | 0 / 26 | 0 / 6 |
| 3 | 140 | 19 (14%) | 2.1 | 4 / 19 | 0 / 2 |
| 5 | 100 | 22 (22%) | 2.2 | 8 / 11 | 2 / 4 |
| 8 | 80 | 28 (35%) | 2.0 | 5 / 24 | 1 / 7 |

  As a ranking, grouping helps a great deal (10–15× chance at 5–8 pieces). But the pieces that clear a threshold are not reliably right, and there are only 12 manuscripts with overlapping draws, so the numbers are noisy.
- **4Q9999 plates.** The 1,112 pieces with ≥ 3 letters read sit on 31 IAA plates, about 38 pieces each, surely from many manuscripts. Statistic: truncated Fisher (only pieces with p < 0.05 count), against random sets of the same size. The top plates point to MT Leviticus (plate 78, p = 0.001) and to ETCBC "PAM 43.6xx" collections (plates 95, 91, 203, 75). On inspection, the contributing pieces do not have these texts as their own top hits. The plate-level signal comes from document structure (many short runs of common words), not from consistent passages. **No credible lead.** The per-plate list is in `reports/M8_plates.json`, for completeness.

## Where this leaves the project

1. Most 4Q9999 pieces show too few letters for statistical placement, whatever the reader. That limit has not moved.
2. The recogniser has stopped improving with more of the same kind of data. The next gains would come from:
   - cleaner edge handling: labels that mark partial letters, instead of dropping them or keeping them as full letters;
   - a character language model in the decoder;
   - a 96 px input (section 3), combined with the extra training data.
3. The one strong result is the 4Q12a lead. It is a question for a specialist, and it cannot be settled here.
