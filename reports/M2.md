# M2 Preprocessing

Date: 2026-09-24. Code: `src/prep.py` (per image), `src/prep_batch.py` (batch plus line-count check), `src/prep_sheets.py` (contact sheets for inspection by eye). All images and derived files stay in `data/m2/` (not committed). The inspection record is `reports/M2_inspection.json` (image names and verdicts only).

Set: the 3,159 clean one-to-one parchment recto pairs from M1.

## Pipeline

1. **Fetch** the pyramid level with `inverse_scale=2` (half resolution). At that level the line pitch has a median of about 165 px, so letters are about 60–80 px tall, which is more than a recogniser needs. The raw level is cached in `data/m2/raw/` (1.3 GB).
2. **Exposure**: stretch so that the 99.9th percentile is 230. Some images are underexposed (see M1).
3. **Parchment mask**: Gaussian blur, then Otsu on the foreground pixels to separate mid-grey tissue from bright parchment. Next, a small closing (15 px) and the choice of components: keep the largest component plus large pieces near it that do not touch the image edge. Last, a large closing (45 px) and hole filling on the kept piece only.
   - Two orders failed first. Per-pixel texture filtering cut the letters out of the mask, because strokes look "textured". A large closing before choosing components joined neighbouring fragments to the main piece.
4. **Binarisation**: Sauvola (window 61, k = 0.25) inside the mask. The border of the mask is peeled off, and specks under 12 px are dropped.
5. **Dark ink**: in infrared, ink is near black, but cracks and shadows are grey. The line profile and the deskew therefore use only ink components darker than 0.6 × the parchment median. On faded fragments, where dark ink is less than half of all ink, they use all ink. Each image gets an `ink_contrast` value (the area-weighted relative darkness of its ink) for later filtering.
6. **Deskew**: projection-profile search over ±18° with a prior towards 0°. An angle *a* must raise the profile score above the 0° score by more than 2% × |*a*|.
   - A plain ±15° search chose ±15° for 407 images, mostly 1–2 line fragments whose text was level when viewed. Short texts give flat score curves.
   - Real tilts exist, such as B-363736 at about 9°, so the range cannot be narrow either.
7. **Line segmentation**: the row profile of dark ink, normalised by mask width. The line pitch comes from the first autocorrelation peak after the first zero crossing. Peaks must be at least 0.6 pitch apart and above 20% of the strongest peak. Each line band carries `y`, `top`, `bottom`, the ink extent `x0`–`x1`, and a strength value.

## Results

**Automatic check.** The number of detected line bands was compared with the number of transcription lines that have preserved letters, on all 3,159 images (processed with 0 errors).

| Transcription lines | n | Exact | Within ±1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 590 | 0.76 | 0.94 |
| 2 | 985 | 0.65 | 0.97 |
| 3–4 | 972 | 0.49 | 0.85 |
| 5–8 | 448 | 0.41 | 0.79 |
| 9+ | 164 | 0.27 | 0.59 |
| All | 3,159 | 0.57 | 0.88 |

This count is a strict test. A transcription line whose only preserved letters are ascender tips at a broken edge is often not a separate band, and a line of tips that ETCBC does not count is sometimes found. The version history on the same set: v1 (±8°, all ink) 0.55 / 0.88; v2 (dark ink, ±15°) 0.57 / 0.88, but with the ±15° pile-up; v3 (prior towards 0°) 0.57 / 0.88, with a median |angle| of 0.25°.

**Inspection by eye: 52 random fragments** (contact sheets with the line bands and ink drawn on top, v1 segmentation; I judged them from the sheets).

- **Mask correct: 49 of 52.** Two masks include a tissue strip, and one keeps two pieces with different slopes together.
- **Lines physically correct: 41 of 52.** This means every visible text line has one band and there is no false band; partial lines at broken edges are allowed. The errors:
  - cracks read as lines (2): fixed by the dark-ink profile
  - faded ink giving false or doubtful bands (2)
  - a sparse line missed (2)
  - two large or multi-piece images with different slopes, over-segmented (2)
  - a tiny blurred fragment, a false band on tissue, and a large letter split in two (1 each)
- **One pairing problem.** 4Q9 (B-366516) shows 4–5 clear lines, but its ETCBC fragment has one line with preserved letters. The M3 fit test drops it.

## Known weaknesses (not fixed)

- Tissue sometimes stays inside the mask. The Otsu split fails when tissue is almost as bright as the parchment.
- Large images (9 or more lines, often whole columns) segment poorly. Different pieces can have different slopes, and a single global angle cannot fit them. They are few (164 of 3,159), and M1 already marks many of them as assemblies.
- Faded fragments give weak, noisy bands. `ink_contrast` above about 0.55 marks them.

## Changes to the plan

- Kraken `blla` was not needed for the one-to-one set. Projection profiles with the dark-ink profile are good enough for M3 to align. Try Kraken again only if the multi-line and large images become a priority.
- Per-piece deskew (one angle per mask component) is the next fix if large images matter.
