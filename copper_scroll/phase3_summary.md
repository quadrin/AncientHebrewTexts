# Phase 3 summary: candidate sites, scored and mapped

Session 2, 2026-09-28. Desk research on texts, editions and maps only.

**What the maps and tables show.** They show where scholars place the *named places and landmarks* of the scroll, at the level of the site (for example "Kh. Qumran" or "Wadi el-Qelt at Choziba"). They do not show where any deposit would be. The scroll's cubits, depths and directions were **not** converted into positions and are not plotted. Nothing here proposes or supports excavation.

"BK" marks background knowledge that does not come from the files. Page numbers are printed pages.

## 1. Method

### 1.1 Candidates and scoring

- **Dossiers.** One dossier per entry (61 entries). Each holds:
  - the master table fields;
  - Puech's place claims (2006; 2015);
  - Lefkovits's site identifications, with the other scholars he reports;
  - Milik 1960 (ADAJ) and Milik 1962 (DJD III, sections C and D);
  - the Phase 2 lexicon rows for the entry's terms;
  - the later studies (*Copper Scroll Studies* 2002; Høgenhaven 2020).
- **Other sources.** The reviewers also used:
  - SWP *Memoirs* III (1883) and Palmer's name lists (1881);
  - Elitzur 2004;
  - Pleiades.
- **Every proposal is recorded.** Four reviewers worked in parallel, one per group of entries. They listed **every candidate that any source proposes**: 292 candidates in all, including the ones they ruled out.
- **Scoring rules.** The rules were written before the scoring:
  1. **Name test.** This is Elitzur's two-condition test (F2.21):
     - (i) the ancient sources fix the area;
     - (ii) the Arabic or modern name matches in all or almost all letters, at or near that area.

     A name resemblance alone is weak evidence.
  2. **Feature fit.** Does the published description of the site include the landmark that the text requires?
  3. **Sequence fit.** Does the site fit the areas of the neighbouring entries? A sequence argument alone cannot make a site best-supported.
  4. **Reading dependence.** If the place name or the key landmark exists only in one edition's reading, the verdict is at most "possible", unless the other evidence is strong.
  5. **No hider theory.** A theory of who hid the treasure (Temple, Qumran, rebels) must not choose between readings.
- **Verdicts.** Each candidate gets one of four verdicts: best-supported, possible, weak or ruled out. Each verdict has a confidence level and 2–4 sentences of reasons with pages.
- **My review.** I read the four result files. I checked sample citations against the sources, for example Puech 2002 (CSS p. 83) on the direction of Doq. I also re-read every best-supported verdict that depends on a reading (§4.4). I changed no verdict.

### 1.2 Coordinates

- **Hand curation.** Automatic geocoding gave wrong items too often (F3.6), so all 37 places were curated by hand.
- **Sources.** Each place has one coordinate, taken from:
  - Pleiades (CC BY);
  - Wikidata (CC0);
  - or a label on PEF Survey Sheet XVIII (1880), which I georeferenced by an affine fit to 4 control points. The residuals are 15–106 m.
- **Recorded with each place:** the source, the precision, and the kind of place.
  - **point:** a site.
  - **area:** a valley, plateau or district. The point is only an anchor for the label.
- **No point at all.** Three areas have no point: the Valley of Shaveh / King's Valley, the Jordan ford, and the Tekoa–Herodium sector. No source fixes them, so the maps show them as labels only.

## 4. Contested cases, stated fairly

### 4.1 The Valley of Achor (1, 17)

There are three placements:

| Placement | Held by | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Wadi Nuweiʿimeh, the late tradition | Milik; Puech; the Onomasticon's "north of Jericho, beside Galgala" | best-supported (1 low, 17 medium) |
| el-Buqeiʿah, the Iron Age "Valley of Trouble" | Allegro; Eshel; the majority view for the biblical valley | possible |
| TIR's "Achor Vallis" label NW of Jericho | TIR | see below |

- **No name survives.** None of the three passes Elitzur's test.
- **TIR's label is the same wadi (F3.2).** On PEF Sheet XVIII, the springs ʿAin ed-Duk and ʿAin en-Nueiameh lie together NW of Jericho, and *Wady en Nueiameh* runs east from them, north of Jericho, past Kh. el-Mafjar. So TIR's "NW" and Milik's "NE" label two stretches of one wadi. They are not rival sites.
- **What stays open.** The choice between the late tradition and the Buqeia is still open (Q13).

### 4.2 Koḥlit (4, 11, 15, 16, 19, 60)

- **Result: unknown.** Twelve candidates are recorded. No ancient source outside the scroll places Koḥlit.
- **Leading working hypothesis:** Tell es-Sultan (Puech; Høgenhaven), rated possible, low.
- **Why the others fail.** Milik's Carmel spring, Goranson's Transjordan, and Zissu's Samarian desert break the sequence of the entries.

### 4.3 Sekakah and Qumran (20–23)

- **Result.** Qumran is best-supported at site level (medium), but it **fails the name test**: no name survives. The placement rests on two things:
  - V 13, "from Jericho to Sokokah";
  - the water installations at Qumran.
- **The aqueduct (21) is the firmest single landmark.**
- **Weaker points.** Two readings weaken the link:
  - Eshel's יגר "dam" (20) is rejected by Puech and doubted by Høgenhaven.
  - Puech 2015 (p. 58) doubts the "Solomon" reading at 23.

### 4.4 Verdicts that depend on a reading

- **55, Bethesda.** The name "Bethesda" exists only in Milik's emended reading. Puech's own reading, "the house of the two reservoirs", still describes a double pool. The double pool is a known 1st-century installation (John 5:2; Eusebius, Onomasticon; excavations reported by Høgenhaven p. 84). The reviewer applied rule 6 explicitly and kept "best-supported" at medium. Puech calls the location "not completely certain" (2015 p. 103).
- **37, Valley of Shaveh.** The place name is secure. Only the landmark words ("irrigated land"; Puech's emended "underground chamber") depend on readings, so the valley-level verdict stands at low confidence.

### 4.5 Absalom's monument (48) and the King's Valley (36–37)

- **Absalom's monument (48).**
  - Puech and Høgenhaven place it in the Kidron: best-supported, low.
  - Milik places it in the SW necropolis (DJD D68): weak. That placement rests on his chain of equations (King's Valley = ha-Šoʾ = Bethso = Baqʿah), which Puech does not fully accept (2015 p. 74). It also interrupts the Kidron sequence that Milik's own Addenda reading of entry 47 implies (p. 301).
- **The same valley on two maps (Q22).** 2 Sam 18:18 puts Absalom's monument in the King's Valley. The Group 3 reviewer placed the King's Valley (as the Valley of Shaveh, 36–37) south of the city, towards Ramat Raḥel. The Group 4 reviewer placed Absalom's monument in the Kidron. Both reviewers mark "which valley" as unresolved.

### 4.6 Doq (31)

- **The only full name-test pass in the Jericho group.**
  - 1 Macc 16:15 fixes Dok by Jericho.
  - ʿAin Duk keeps the name (SWP III p. 173; Elitzur p. 139).
- **Result.** The summit fortress on Jebel Qarantal (Milik; Eshel) is best-supported, medium.
- **Caveats.**
  - The name belongs to the spring, not to the summit.
  - SWP placed the fort at Kh. Abu Lahm (pp. 173, 209).
  - The landmark word is disputed: Puech reads a "drying floor", Milik a "fortress".
  - Puech's direction for Doq changed between his publications (F3.4).

### 4.7 Names that exist only in Milik's emended text

These places are rated weak or ruled out, because each depends on an emendation or on a reading that Puech shows came from a misplaced fragment:
- Masada (44);
- Kephar Nebo (42);
- Qobʿeh / el-Qabu (41);
- el-ʿAṣur / Baal-Hazor (33).

Milik's Masada and Kephar Nebo remain on record in the tables.

## 5. The entries left open

- **37 entries have "possible" places only.** Most of them fall into three areas that the sequence implies:
  - the Jerusalem Temple area (3, 5, 7, 8, 12, 12a, 13, 14);
  - the Jericho oasis (27, 29, 30, 33);
  - the Tekoa–Herodium sector (39–45).
- **The area is not a place.** For these entries the honest result is "no specific site identifiable".
- **One entry has nothing to map.** Entry 41 has no toponym on the reading that the reviewer accepted.

## 8. What this means for Phases 4 and 5

- **Phase 5** can check the published archaeology of the 23 best-supported places. Mostly this means asking whether the landmark the text requires is reported at the site in the 1st century CE. The "unknown" feature fits in §3 are the first questions.
- **Phase 4** (the Greek letters). All seven Greek-letter groups fall in entries 1–15 (columns I–IV). In those entries the placements alternate: entries 1, 4 and 15 are in the Jericho area (Achor, Koḥlit), and entries 7, 9 and 12a are in the Jerusalem Temple area; entry 6 is unknown. Phase 4 can test whether the letters follow these areas. The test is weak, because most of these placements are only "possible".
