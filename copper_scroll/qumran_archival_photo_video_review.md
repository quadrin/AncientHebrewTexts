# Qumran: archival photographs, drone footage and entries 20–23

Reviewed 28 September 2026. This follows [the tunnel-photo comparison](qumran_photo_correspondence.md). The new material supplies historical and moving views of the gorge. It does not yet connect the documented upstream boulder, pothole, wall and intake in one verified sequence.

## What the images establish

**Davey CJD979 → Magen–Peleg Fig. 87 / Reeder–Jol Fig. 2:** high visual confidence that these show the same mouth. Shared details include the overhanging slab and recess above the aperture, the vertical rock projection to its left, and the jointed ledges to its right. CJD979 adds an independently photographed 1974 view. The western-mouth attribution still follows Magen's caption and p. 79 text; the photograph by itself supplies no compass bearing or ancient flow direction. Its identity as a Copper Scroll feature remains low confidence.

**Davey CJD981 → Davies slide 14:** high visual confidence in the same gorge view. Both show the projecting cliff with a lower dark aperture and upper horizontal recess on the left, the stepped rock promontory beyond it and the opposing wall on the right. The aperture probably belongs to the CJD979 tunnel mouth, but its small size in these wider views warrants a weaker match than the close-up comparison. Their captions place them in the dam area. Neither photograph demonstrates surviving dam masonry or independently assigns the basin to Ilan point 2.

**Davey CJD980:** shows the gorge throat and a substantial foreground block. A block's presence does not identify Reeder's approximately three-metre boulder. The image does not establish its height or show the complete pothole–wall–channel relationship required for that assignment.

**Davey CJD998 → BibleStock approximately 01:15–01:30:** probable visual correspondence with the cave catalogued as Cave 28. The rectangular outer opening, irregular inner roof and pale sloping apron below it agree in their relative arrangement. The archive's cave number is retained as its identification, not independently re-surveyed. This gives the footage a specific comparison landmark; no camera calibration, control point or geographic coordinate follows from it.

**BibleStock approximately 01:15–01:40:** changing aerial views connect that cave-bearing cliff with successive bedrock steps, basin floors and the downstream waterfall. The camera withdraws toward a wider view. Sampled frames do not isolate a securely identifiable constructed wall, Reeder's rock or Ilan point 3. Natural channels and ledges must not be traced as aqueduct masonry without a closer match. No sampled frame supplies a passage through the long tunnel or identifies its two internal breaches individually.

CJD977 shows the settlement/plateau from the aqueduct area; CJD978 supplies a broader wadi view. Both assist orientation but leave the upstream feature identities unresolved. Pennarts's 1953 aqueduct photograph was already recorded in our earlier work and is not counted as a new find.

## Joint test of entries 20–23

The Puech p. 188 and p. 189 page images were rechecked against the existing constraint register. The following is a conditional test under Sekakah = Qumran, not four independent confirmations of that identification.

- **20: cairn/heap or dam.** Puech's discussion prefers a stone heap and objects to using the proposed dam to settle the word. Ilan point 4 is a reconstructed dam with no surviving remains reported by those authors. The new images supply no specifically identified cairn. A dam-based model therefore remains dependent on a disputed interpretation.
- **21: conduit head and northern relation.** Ilan point 3 remains the first functional candidate for the whole conduit. The tunnel photo match supports a downstream landmark, not a promotion of its mouth to the head. No new observation settles the boulder's relation to point 3 or the grammatical referent of north. A branch-head interpretation continues to permit a different ranking.
- **22: fissure east of a named reservoir.** The long-tunnel openings lie generally east of basin 2 in Ilan's plan, so the proposed relationship remains geometrically possible. Neither the reservoir's ancient name nor an individual opening's identity is independently established. The exterior drone sequence does not close this gap. A settlement-reservoir model remains a separate alternative.
- **23: named channel, large landmark and sixty cubits.** Puech explicitly discusses Solomon/Shallum and uncertainty in the channel phrase. The new images establish neither a named channel nor the two distance endpoints. No sixty-cubit buffer is drawn around a selected rock, and no identity between the landmarks of entries 21 and 23 is assumed.

No combined model presently satisfies all four entries with independently identified features. This comparison preserves the A–B–C–D investigative order and all low exact-feature confidence labels. No geographic geometry changes.

Coverage of the surrounding gorge does not demonstrate coverage of every component. The basin is therefore not assigned Ilan number 2, and no ground-registered channel trace or north/boulder test is produced. The two source plans remain separate coordinate frames.
