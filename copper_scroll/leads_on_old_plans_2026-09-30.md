## 1. Who first read ha-Melaḥ as a place (evidence)

The quotations come from the Lefkovits text layer. The OCR garbles the Hebrew, which is shown here as […].

- **Item 13 (III 8), p. 145.** Lefkovits translates "in a/the deep pit which is in (the) Melah" and "a/the grave which is in (the) Melah" (item 14). He adds: "Salt City, is one of the six cities in the Wilderness of Judah near … the Salt Sea … (Josh. 15:61-62). Secacah, another of these cities, is mentioned in the Scroll".
  - He also lists other possible links: towns called Milḥa and Milḥayya in the Talmud, "the Tower of Milḥa", and "Melaḥ Raziza" in the Rehov inscription.
- **Item 20, p. 183:** "If […], Melah, of the Scroll is identical with […], City of Salt, then it was located near Secacah (see #13, 3:8)."
- **Item 6 (II 1), p. 104.** He cross-refers "the pit/cistern of (the) Melah/Salt" to items 13 and 14. So he treats all three as one possible place.
- **Item 14, p. 149.** These translators treat the word as a place:
  - Pixner: "At the tomb which is to the north-east of the Milham";
  - Wolters: "In the tomb which is in east Milham, on the north";
  - García Martínez: "In the tomb which is Milcham, to the North-east";
  - Wise: "In the grave that is in Milham, on the northeast".

  Beyer reads as Milik ("terrace"), and Allegro and Vermes agree with Milik.
- **Puech 2006, p. 184.** He admits both *šbmlh* and *šbmlḥ*. But "*šbmlḥ* « qui est dans la saline » paraît peu vraisemblable pour ces deux entrées". He prefers *mlh* "le terrassement, l'esplanade" (masculine), "dont les angles … sont directement concernés", and allows Milik's Temple Esplanade as "tout à fait possible".
- **Correction.** The follow-up (§0.3) and the third report (§8.5) said the ha-Melaḥ = City of Salt link was not found in print. That is wrong. Lefkovits proposed it, conditionally, in 2000, and linked it to Secacah. What I have not found in print is a specific identification of the scroll's Melah with **Kh. Qumran**. **Inference:** the third report's contribution to ha-Melaḥ is the new support (the north frame, §2.1 there, and the order), not the idea itself.

**The gender argument (inference).** Puech (p. 184) and Lefkovits (p. 146) both note that the suffix of "its north" (בצפונו) is masculine. Lefkovits adds that "place names are feminine". There are three ways to read this:
- Lefkovits: the suffix refers to the pit or grave, "in the north section of the deep pit".
- Puech: the Melaḥ is the masculine "embankment".
- A place named after a masculine thing, "the Salt", treated as masculine. Lefkovits allows this himself ("perhaps the compiler(s) considered the place … also masculine").

The Copper Scroll names other places with the article: הסככא (IV 13), השוא (VIII 10, 14) and החורון (IX 7). So the article in המלח does not count against a name. **The grammar leaves the question open.**

## 2. Koḥlit = Tell es-Sultan: what lies north of the tell

**Evidence (SWP *Memoirs* III, 1883).**
- **Tell es-Sultan, pp. 222–223.** "The spring (Elisha's fountain) comes out beneath the mound on the east … The water comes out beneath piles of broken stones into a shallow reservoir, 24 feet by 40 feet, of hewn stones, well dressed, and of moderate dimensions."
- **p. 223.** "On the north side of the Tell there are many traces of ruins, called Khurbet Ras el 'Ain." The finds are Byzantine: "a pillar shaft, 9 inches diameter, of Santa Croce marble", and a rude Ionic capital. "In Byzantine times a town extended along the lower ground on the north and east."
- **Kanat Musa, p. 206.** The ʿAin el-ʿAujah aqueduct runs south past ʿAin Duk and ʿAin Nuweiʿimeh, "communicates with various cisterns and with Khurbet el Mefjir", and ends at Bir Heider. "A shorter aqueduct from 'Ain es Sultan joins the above at Khurbet el Mefjir. The water in this case is conveyed through pipes like those of the high level aqueduct in Wady Kelt." A fourth aqueduct runs from the spring **south**.
- **p. 226 (Warren).** "On the northern bank of Wady Nuei'ameh, about a mile from 'Ain es Sultan, north-east, are the remains of a village", with a chapel.
- **Phase 5 (Sala 2014 p. 117).** A cemetery lies north and west of the tell, with tombs down to the Roman period.

**Geometry (evidence, from the project's coordinates).** The Wadi Nuweiʿimeh point (the Achor anchor for 1 and 17) is 1.38 km from Tell es-Sultan at a bearing of about 345°, north-north-west. Milik places the ruin of entry 1 at the monastery of Chorembe "near Kh. el-Mafjar", and Puech follows him (`readings.json`, e1-ruin). Kh. el-Mafjar lies about 2 km north-east of the tell (BK).

**Inference (weak).**
- Puech puts Koḥlit at Tell es-Sultan and Achor at Wadi Nuweiʿimeh. Taken together, those two places put the Achor entries 1–2 km north of the Koḥlit mound.
- All five Koḥlit entries point north, to "north of Koḥlit" (19, 60), "on the north" (4), and so on. That is where the tell's cemetery, the ruins of Kh. Ras el-ʿAin and the aqueduct line to Kh. el-Mafjar lie.
- The text and Puech's pair of sites therefore agree. This is a consistency check, not a proof. The same wording would fit other pairs of sites that lie north and south of each other.
- The finer sequence model in the third report leaned slightly the other way (Achor in the Buqeia, likelihood ratio ≈ 2.4). Both hints are weak.

**Feature by feature (evidence and inference):**

| Entry | Needs | North or east of the tell | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | a pool **east** of Koḥlit, with a "northern corner" | Built reservoir of 24 × 40 ft, of hewn stones, at the spring at the tell's east foot (SWP III pp. 222–223). Undated | Matches in kind. The corner and the date are untested |
| 4 | a mound; a conduit with an opening "on the north"; a rock cleft of immersion | The mound itself. A piped aqueduct from the spring running north to Kh. el-Mafjar (p. 206), undated and probably Umayyad (BK). No cleft is reported | Partly |
| 15 | a great cistern with a pillar on its north side (Koḥlit restored by Puech) | None reported. The only pillar is a Byzantine marble shaft in the ruins | No |
| 19, 60 | pits north of Koḥlit; tombs at the mouth of one (60) | Cemetery north and west of the tell (Sala 2014). No pits reported | Partly |

**Effect.** No change of confidence: Tell es-Sultan stays **possible, low**. The north frame tells the next search where to look: the belt between the tell and Wadi Nuweiʿimeh, not the palaces south of the tell.

## 3. ha-Melaḥ on the ground: Kh. Qumran or ʿAin el-Ghuweir (P1)

| Entry | Needs | Kh. Qumran | ʿAin el-Ghuweir |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6 | a cistern "under the steps" | Many stepped pools (BK; the Humbert–Chambon volume was not available here) | No cisterns are named in the source read |
| 13 | a pit on its north side, whose entrance is under the western corner | Not tested | Not tested |
| 14 | a tomb on its east side, in its north part; "dig three" (about 1.3–1.6 m) | The main cemetery is east of the site, and a small cemetery lies "ten minutes' walk north". Graves are 0.8–2.5 m deep ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qumran_cemetery)) | The cemetery is "approximately 800 meters north of the settlement"; shaft graves oriented north–south ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain_el-Ghuweir)) |
| Date | before about 68 CE | Occupied into 68 CE (BK) | Coins "37 BC and 44 AD"; destroyed about 68 CE |

**Inference (weak).**
- Kh. Qumran fits more of the features. "Near Secacah" (Lefkovits) fits both sites: ʿAin el-Ghuweir is about 15 km south of Qumran, and Puech places Ir ha-Melaḥ there (p. 176 n. 62).
- **The conflict stays.** Puech and the project's anchors 21–22 make Kh. Qumran = Secacah. Kh. Qumran as Melaḥ therefore needs Milik's view, in which the scroll's Secacah is the whole Wadi Qumran, or Cross and Milik's view that biblical Secacah is Kh. es-Samrah.
- An Iron Age settlement at ʿAin el-Ghuweir "was identified with the biblical place Nibshan" (Wikipedia; the identifier is not named there). In that scheme ʿAin el-Ghuweir is not the City of Salt either.

## 4. The Temple group on Warren's plans (entries 9, 3 and 10)

### 4.1 Entry 9: "the cistern opposite the East Gate, nineteen cubits away" (evidence; negative)

- **Evidence: the map.** Warren's Plate VI, "Rock contours of the Noble Sanctuary", uses the Ordnance Survey tank numbers at 1:1000. It shows the dashed outline of the Golden Gate on the east wall.
  - Cistern **XV** lies to its west. SWP *Jerusalem* p. 221: "North of the last and west of the Golden Gate. It is about 18 feet in diameter … The depth is 35 feet, the whole being cut in rock".
  - Nos. XII–XIV lie further south-west. A surface-water conduit, drawn on the plate, runs south past XV to No. 13, which it enters on the east side (p. 221: "from a small cistern 250 feet further north").
- **Measurements.** Measured on the rendered plate, against its own scale bar (224.9 px per 100 ft). The scale checks against SWP's size for tank No. 1 to within about 1%.

  | Cistern | Distance to the Golden Gate outline | In cubits (0.445–0.525 m) | Direction |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | XV (centre) | **98 ft = 30.0 m** (rim about 88 ft) | 57–67 | West, at the north end of the gatehouse |
  | XIV | 139 ft = 42.4 m | 81–95 | South-west |
  | XIII | 148 ft = 45.1 m | 86–101 | South-west |
  | XII | 186 ft = 56.7 m | 108–128 | South-west |

  No cistern is drawn within about 60 ft of the gatehouse. The figure `entry9_golden_gate_plateVI.png` shows the plate with these distances and a 19-cubit line.
- **Outside the gate.** Warren's shaft was sunk "143 feet from the south end of the gate, and in a line perpendicular to its front". His gallery west "crossed" "a tank or rock-cut tomb" at 18 ft 6 in. It reached "a massive masonry wall … running north and south" at 97 ft, "i.e., 46 feet from the Sanctuary wall" (*Recovery* pp. 154–156). The tank or tomb therefore lies about 124 ft (38 m) outside the wall.
- **Result.** 19 cubits is 8.5–10 m (28–33 ft). No recorded cistern at the Golden Gate position, inside or outside, lies near that distance. The nearest is about three times as far.
- **Inference.**
  - The specific feature match for entry 9 fails at the level of recorded features.
  - This does not refute a Temple setting for entry 9, for four reasons:
    - (a) the survey records only cisterns with accessible mouths;
    - (b) the ground in front of the gate lies 30–40 ft deep in debris (Warren);
    - (c) the Herodian east gate may not lie exactly under the Golden Gate (BK);
    - (d) "nineteen cubits" might be measured from some other point.
  - The project's "East Gate (Golden Gate position), medium" should record that no known cistern satisfies the distance.

### 4.2 Entry 3: "the great cistern that is in the court of the peristyle" → the Great Sea, No. 8? (possible, low)

- **Evidence.**
  - SWP *Jerusalem* pp. 219–220: "No. 8. Usually known as the 'Great Sea,' … It is a fine cavern with rocky piers, reached from the south by a narrow staircase. … It is the largest of all the Sanctuary tanks, and has numerous manholes from the surface … A conduit enters this tank from the east. … The capacity of this tank is at least two million gallons."
  - No. 6 lies "near the Great Sea (No. 8), between the Aksa and the platform". So No. 8 is south of the raised platform, under the southern part of the esplanade.
  - m. Eruvin 10:14 names two Temple cisterns: "from the Cistern of the Exiles and from the Great Cistern (הבור הגדול)" ([Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Mishnah_Eruvin.10.14)).
  - The *Israel Hayom* report of 2021 says Benjamin Mazar took No. 8 to be "a cistern mentioned in Jewish sources that was used as a main water source by pilgrims" ([Jewish Ledger reprint](https://www.jewishledger.com/2021/08/49-cisterns-tell-the-story-of-the-destruction-of-the-temple-and-much-more/)).
- **Inference.**
  - Entry 3's phrase "the great cistern", with the article, matches the Mishnah's name for a Temple cistern.
  - The largest recorded cistern lies under the southern outer court. That court was lined with porticoes (the Royal Stoa on the south: BK), so it can be called a colonnaded court.
  - Entry 3's "opposite the upper opening" fits a cistern with several mouths.
  - Against it:
    - Milik takes the "peristyle" as the inner court;
    - the date of No. 8 is not established here (Gibson and Jacobson 1996 not read);
    - entry 15 also has "the great cistern" (in Koḥlit), so the phrase is not unique.
  - A caution: if Milik is right that IV 1 has no "Koḥlit", then entry 15's "great cistern … in the pillar on its north side" could also be No. 8, with its rock piers and "a small circular chamber" on the north-east. That remains speculation.
- **Status: possible, low.** Worth adding to the atlas as a named candidate with these limits.

### 4.3 Entry 10: "the cistern under the wall, on the east … its entrance under the great threshold" (untested)

Warren's gallery met, about 46–50 ft in front of the Golden Gate, "a massive masonry wall … running north and south" of large drafted blocks (*Recovery* pp. 155–158). It is also drawn on Plate VI. **Inference:** this is a possible "wall on the east" outside the gate. No cistern under it is recorded, so it stays untested.

## 5. A historical frame for the two groups in 1–19 (inference)

- **Evidence.**
  - b. Taanit 27a: "There were twenty-four priestly watches in Eretz Yisrael, and twelve in Jericho". Half of each watch went up from Jericho to supply water and food to their brothers in Jerusalem ([Sefaria](https://www.sefaria.org/Taanit.27a)).
  - Entry 4 stores "vessels of offering … the offering and the seventh-year store, and second tithe" at the Koḥlit mound.
- **Inference.** In the third report, group (i) was the named places of the Jericho district, and group (ii) the unnamed built features with Temple vocabulary. A list of priestly or Temple property would naturally mix the two: the Temple precinct, and the priests' stores in the Jericho district. This gives explanation (a) of the first sub-list a historical setting. It is not evidence that the treasure was real.

## 6. An observation on the Temple-area deposits (inference, depends on the Phase 3 placements)

Where Phase 3 places them, the Temple-area deposits lie on the east and south-east of the enclosure or in the Kidron:
- in 1–19: 9, 10 (east), 12 (south corner), 12a (east corner);
- in 48–56: 48, 51–54, 56;
- only 55 (Bethesda) is to the north, and 49 (Siloam) to the south.

None is placed firmly on the west side (the Upper City). If this is real, the deposits face the Kidron and the road down to Jericho, where the rest of the list lies. This is an observation, not a test.

## 11. What this report does NOT show

- It identifies no site with more confidence than before. The one clear change is negative (entry 9 at the Golden Gate).
- The Plate VI distances are from a scan of an 1884 lithograph (the plate is reduced; its scale bar was used). A few per cent of error does not change the result. Only cisterns with open mouths were recorded.
- The Qumran features come from summaries (Wikipedia, BK), not from the excavation plans.
- The priority search covered Lefkovits and Puech in full, and others only as they report them. Høgenhaven 2020 and *Copper Scroll Studies* were not read.
- The historical frame in §5 is an inference, not a test.
