## Part 2. Read 29 September 2026 (user uploads, not in the repository)

### Stacey 2007, "Some Archaeological Observations on the Aqueducts of Qumran," *DSD* 14.2, pp. 222–243 — read in full

- Stacey dates the main aqueduct to no earlier than Herod, after the 31 BCE earthquake (against de Vaux's pre-31 BCE date). Its channel is sunk into earthquake debris (pp. 222–226, 239).
- Two upstream construction stages (p. 228):
  1. Earlier: a simple, wide run-off channel "running down from below the cliffs to the site". He cites Ilan–Amit 2002 Fig. 1, "no. 16 eastward" (n. 28). It had a low wall to catch run-off (quoting Ilan–Amit 2002 p. 385, n. 29).
  2. Later: the dam at the foot of the waterfall, and the tunnelled cliff aqueduct that carried water "from the dam to the beginning of the earlier aqueduct" (Fig. 1, sections D-D and E-E, n. 30).
- In the 1989 Hebrew numbering, point 16 is the right-angle bend where the channel leaves the cliff for the plain. That matches Stacey's description. The equivalence of the 2002 and 1989 figure numbers is assumed, not checked.
- **Effect on entry 21 (inference, medium).**
  - If the scroll postdates the dam stage, the head of the whole system is still the dam intake (points 3/4). That is the current lead.
  - Stacey's sequence makes point 16 a documented former head of the earlier line. So "head of a subsection" becomes a concrete alternative, not an abstract one.
  - Both depend on Stacey's phasing, which is a hypothesis.
- Secondhand, from the unread 2002 chapter: p. 385 assigns the aqueduct to Herod Archelaus (Stacey n. 3). That date differs from the discussion on p. 286 of the 1989 Hebrew chapter.
- Also:
  - Qumran had no rock-hewn underground cisterns, unlike the Hasmonean desert forts (p. 237).
  - The tower watched the path up to Hyrcania (p. 236).

### Zertal & Bar, *Manasseh Hill Country Survey* Vol. 4 (*From Nahal Bezeq to the Sartaba*, 2019), 8 uploaded parts — searched

- Ibziq is not in it either.
  - Vol. 4 covers the Jordan Valley strip south of Nahal Bezeq. Its western boundary is the eastern boundary of Vol. 2 (p. 1, "Chapter One").
  - North of grid N 1950, all 36 sites lie at E ≥ 1923. The nearest to Kh. Ibziq (about 1876/1972) is at 1923/2001, about 5.5 km away.
  - "Ibziq" does not occur in the text.
- The "Bezeq Canal" (site 23(1)) is a Chalcolithic site named after a modern water channel. It is not a candidate for the scroll's "great conduit".
- **Context for entries 57–59 (inference, low).**
  - Road B7 runs from Shechem to Beit Sheʾan via the Bezeq Pass and the Tubas Valley. It is identical with the Neapolis–Scythopolis Roman road (Ch. 1, citing Zertal 2008: 28).
  - So Gerizim/Shechem, Bezek and Beth Shean lie on one route.
  - The scroll's order (Gerizim → Beth Sham → Bezek) does not follow the road, because Bezek lies between the other two.
- Vol. 2 (*The Eastern Valleys*: pp. 191–197 for Ibziq; p. 28 for the roads) is still the volume needed.

### Zertal, *Manasseh Hill Country Survey* Vol. 2 (*The Eastern Valleys and the Fringes of the Desert*, Brill 2008), 9 uploaded parts — Ibziq entries read (page images checked)

- **Site 44, Kh. Ibziq (Upper)** = SWP "Kh. Ibzik", the atlas candidate (pp. 195–198).
  - Grid 1878/1971. A tell and large ruin of 32 dunams.
  - Features: 45 cisterns; walls, quarried places, doorposts and lintels; caves reused as dwellings; a built pass (naqb) nearby; "a paved Roman road runs east of the site".
  - Pottery (102 shards): IrA II 5%, IrA III 2%, Per 3%, LR 20%, Byz 30%, EM 10%, MA 30%. No Hellenistic, and no Early Roman in the percentages. Fig. 122 does illustrate two ER vessels (nos. 8 and 10), so there is a small, unquantified ER presence.
  - No conduit, drain or channel is described.
  - Zertal: "The identification with Biblical Bezeq (Judges 1:4–5) is unfounded according to the results of the survey" (p. 197).
- **Site 42, Kh. Ibziq (Lower)** (pp. 191–193).
  - 1 km NE of the upper site. 38 dunams.
  - "About 70 burial caves, mostly containing a hewn opening, vault and internal building"; 45 cisterns; remains of a Roman road; a winepress with Greek names (Jews or Samaritans).
  - Pottery: Byz 80%, EM 20%. The settlement "apparently reached this site during the Byzantine Period".
- **Name history** (Ch. 3, pp. 104–107).
  - Eusebius's "two neighbouring villages called Bezeq 17 milestones from Neapolis on the way down to Scythopolis" = sites 42 + 44, "with high probability".
  - Zertal's model moves the name: Canaanite Bezeq at Kh. esh-Sheikh Safiriyan (site 2), then Kh. Salhab (site 23), then Ibziq, at the end of the Iron Age. The name is attested at Ibziq only from the Byzantine period on.
- **Site 23, Kh. Salhab** (pp. 151–153) is Zertal's Biblical Bezeq (1 Sam 11:8).
  - A 15-dunam tell on road B7. Pottery includes ER 15% and LR 15%; 30 cisterns; Roman–Byzantine walls.
  - No conduit reported.
- **Site 43, el-Quleh** (pp. 193–194): a small fortress with Hellenistic–Herodian drafted masonry on the Neapolis–Scythopolis road. Pottery "Hel (?) 100%", 4 shards.
- **Effect on entry 59 (inference, medium).**
  - The landmark ("great conduit") is still not reported at either Ibziq site.
  - The period fit is weak. Lower Ibziq starts in the Byzantine period, and Upper Ibziq's 1st-century presence rests on two illustrated sherds.
  - The name evidence (Eusebius) is 4th century and fits the Byzantine villages. It does not show which site carried the name in the scroll's period; Zertal's own model allows Salhab.
  - The burial caves at Lower Ibziq match Puech's alternative "burial chamber" reading only as a type, and in an undated (probably Byzantine) setting.
  - Suggestion: keep "possible", lower the confidence from medium to low, and record Salhab as an alternative bearer of the name.
  - *Revised later the same day:* *HA* 40 (1971) p. 22 reports a kokhim tomb of the 1st–2nd centuries CE at Kh. Ibziq. That restores the period evidence, so Kh. Ibziq is "possible, medium" again, and Kh. Salhab is mapped at "possible, low" from Zertal's grid. See [entry59_bezek_review.md](entry59_bezek_review.md) and F9.8–F9.9. The "Applied" note below records the intermediate state.
  - **Applied 29 September 2026:** Kh. Ibziq is "possible, low" in the atlas (a dated revision in `atlas/research/build_atlas.py`) and in the entry 59 reading notes. Kh. Salhab is named there as an alternative; it is not mapped, because no coordinate has been taken from Zertal's grid. The Phase 3 tables keep their original assessment. *Updated on merge:* the Phase 5 index (`tables/phase5_archaeology_index.csv`), the entry 59 row of `tables/phase5_assessments.csv`, the Phase 5 summary and the public web page also carry the change.
