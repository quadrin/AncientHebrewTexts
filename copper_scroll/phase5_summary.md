# Phase 5 summary: what the published archaeology says

Session 2, 2026-09-28. Desk research on published reports and editions only.

**Review update, 2026-09-28:** the full Szanton 2023 article is now available. Entry 49 returns to medium overall confidence, conditional on the Siloam reading; its pool/outlet setting is supported, but its trough is unverified. The counts below incorporate this review. See [site identification review](site_identification_review.md) and F5.10–F5.13 for the evidence and the distinction between a site, a landmark type, and an individual feature.

**Follow-up, 2026-09-29:** Zertal's survey of Kh. Ibziq (*Manasseh Hill Country Survey* vol. 2) and Stacey 2007 on the Qumran aqueducts have been read. Entry 59 (Kh. Ibziq) was first lowered from possible, medium to possible, low. Later the same day, *HA* 40 (1971) p. 22 was read: a kokhim tomb of the 1st–2nd centuries CE at Kh. Ibziq. So entry 59 stays possible, medium, and Kh. Salhab is added as a low alternative. No other verdict changes. See [source_leads_2026-09-29.md](source_leads_2026-09-29.md), [entry59_bezek_review.md](entry59_bezek_review.md) and F9.1–F9.11.

**Scope.** This phase asks one question of each Phase 3 place:

> Do published reports describe the kind of landmark that the entry requires (cistern, aqueduct, pool, tomb, spring, gate) at that site, in use in the late Second Temple period?

- Extend the type-and-period comparison to individual installations and candidate locations.
- Model the scroll's cubits, depths and directions under explicit readings, unit ranges and reference landmarks. Compare alternatives and report which observations discriminate among them.

"BK" marks background knowledge. Page numbers are printed pages.

## 1. What was done

- **Three parallel reviewers** checked 37 assessments:
  - the 23 best-supported entries;
  - the "possible" places at medium confidence (ʿAṣla, the Jericho estate and Tell el-Qos, Kh. Ibziq, the Buqeiʿah);
  - four blocks: Tell es-Sultan for Koḥlit, Hyrcania, the Temple enclosure, and the Tekoa–Herodium sector.
- **Records.** They recorded 254 reports, each with page or URL, report type and period.
- **Sources:**

  | Source | Coverage |
  |---|---|
  | *ʿAtiqot* 41 (the 1993 IAA cave survey, Hebrew) | Jebel Quruntul, the escarpment by Qumran, and the Kidron |
  | Eshel's chapter on the aqueducts (CSS pp. 92–107) | Qumran, Hyrcania, Cypros, Doq |
  | The SWP *Memoirs* (1881–83) | 19th-century descriptions |
  | Warren and Wilson (1871) | Jerusalem |
  | The editors' archaeological notes | Milik, Puech, Lefkovits, Høgenhaven |
  | IAA reports online (*Hadashot Arkheologiyot*; *ʿAtiqot* 113 and 119) | Recent excavations |
  | The Tel Aviv University report on Ramat Raḥel (2006–07) | Ramat Raḥel |

- **My checks.**
  - I read every assessment and checked the wording against the rules.
  - All 29 cited URLs respond, and two reports were read to confirm what the reviewers took from them (Ramat Raḥel; the Siloam stepped street).
  - I decided the final verdicts myself.
- **Terms used in the tables:**
  - **Landmark reported:** "yes" (the required kind of feature is reported at the site), "partly" (a related or partial feature, or one without a date), "not reported".
  - **Period:** in use 1st c. BCE–70 CE, earlier only, later only, not stated.

| Verdicts lowered | 2 (Ramat Raḥel, 46: medium → low; Tell el-Qos for 30: possible → weak); in the 2026-09-29 follow-up, Kh. Ibziq for 59 went from medium to low and back to medium after *HA* 40 |
| Verdicts kept from Phase 3 | all others; entry 49 retains medium with an explicit reading condition |

(Inference, medium confidence) **The clearest type-and-period matches are water installations.**
- The four full type matches are aqueducts, outlets, pools or springs. Entry 49 has a partial match.
- These are compatibility checks. No reported installation is independently identified as the scroll's individual feature.
- Tombs, cairns, pits and dovecotes are reported at best as a type in the area, never as a feature that fits one entry.
- Inference (medium): water installations survive, and are dated, more often than tombs, pits or cairns. So this result may show what archaeology can see as much as what the scroll describes.

## 3. The changes

- **49, Siloam: medium overall, conditional on the reading.**
  - The initial Phase 5 upgrade to high is withdrawn. The name-test result applies only if the proposed Siloam reading is accepted.
  - Puech's restored של remains a substantive dependency (2006 p. 200; 2015 p. 92). Lefkovits reads no Siloam place name (2000 pp. 352–354).
  - **Corrected 2026-09-29:** Puech does not correct an engraved ר; he reads the letter as a cursive waw (2006 p. 200). The plate check finds a waw-shaped stroke without a head bar, which leans to his reading without deciding it ([plate_check.md](plate_check.md)).
  - Szanton (*ʿAtiqot* 113, **2023**) distinguishes the Silwan pool at the tunnel outlet from Birkat el-Ḥamra; see the review for the competing identifications.
  - The pool/outlet setting fits. The material checked in this review does not establish a period trough of the required type. The index therefore records a partial match.
- **46, Ramat Raḥel: lowered to low.**
  - The excavators report an earlier pool-and-channel enclosure buried under fill containing pottery as late as the second century BCE (Lipschits et al., 2006–07 report, pp. 15–18).
  - In the scroll's period the site was a small village with ritual baths (INJ 17 pp. 59–60).
  - The reports checked do not establish a large reservoir in use then. This weakens the match to the known pools; it does not establish that every reservoir at the site was absent.
  - Aharoni's identification of Beth ha-Kerem also rests partly on the scroll's own sequence (Lefkovits p. 333 n. 11).
- **30, Tell el-Qos: lowered to weak.** SWP (p. 222) describes a heap of stones, "not a Tell in the strict meaning". No source reports ancient remains there. The Jericho-area placement for entry 30 is kept at "possible".
- **59, Kh. Ibziq: kept at possible, medium, after a same-day revision (follow-up, 2026-09-29).**
  - Zertal's survey (2008, vol. 2 pp. 191–198) reports cisterns, burial caves and a Roman road at the two Ibziq sites, but no conduit.
  - Upper Ibziq's pottery is mainly Late Roman to Mamluk, with only two illustrated Early Roman vessels. Lower Ibziq starts in the Byzantine period.
  - Zertal attests the name at Ibziq only from the Byzantine period and puts biblical Bezek at Kh. Salhab (pp. 104–107, 151–153). Salhab is recorded as an alternative site for the name.
  - On this evidence the verdict was first lowered to low. *HA* 40 (1971) p. 22 then reported a robbed kokhim tomb at Kh. Ibziq with pottery of the 1st–2nd centuries CE. The period is attested, and the tomb matches Puech's alternative "burial chamber" reading in type. The verdict stays medium, and Salhab is added at low. See [source_leads_2026-09-29.md](source_leads_2026-09-29.md) and [entry59_bezek_review.md](entry59_bezek_review.md).

## 4. Other results worth recording

- **Qumran (20–22).**
  - The aqueduct with a dam at its head is Second Temple (Eshel pp. 94–95).
  - Stone-capped graves are reported (SWP p. 210; Milik D8).
  - Natural cracks in the marl with 1st-century activity are documented (Cohen & Porat, *HA-ESI* 132).
  - So both readings of יגר at IV 13, "cairn" and "dam", name a kind of feature that existed there. No source attests a reservoir called "Solomon's".
- **Doq (31).**
  - A Hasmonean fortress with an aqueduct and cisterns stood on the summit (Eshel pp. 100–101), and caves on the mountain were used up to the First Revolt (*ʿAtiqot* 41, Eisenberg p. 120).
  - Puech's "drying floor" has no archaeological counterpart. Milik's "fortress" has one.
  - So the archaeology favours Milik's reading of the landmark word. Rule 7 applies: the archaeology must not choose a reading. The verdict is therefore kept, and Q24 stays open.
- **Absalom's monument (48).**
  - The standing Kidron monument is dated by style to the 1st century CE, but its earliest labels (4th century) name Zacharias (Zias 2023).
  - No source shows that it was called Absalom's in the 1st century, so the Kidron placement still rests on the sequence.
- **Bethesda (55).** Feature and period fit. But Warren (pp. 196–197) describes a second rock-cut twin pool in the same quarter (BK: the Strouthion). So "the house of the two reservoirs" alone does not single out St Anne's.
- **The Kidron (35).** The Region XIV survey (*ʿAtiqot* 41, Dahari pp. 233, 239) describes the Kidron as a canyon also where it leaves the escarpment, with 1st-century use. Inference (medium): "the mouth of the gorge" does not by itself fix the Mar Saba end.
- **Tell es-Sultan for Koḥlit.** The tombs are the one supported landmark: a cemetery north and west of the tell with tombs to the Roman period (Sala 2014 p. 117). The great cistern and the pits are not reported, and the dated pools and aqueducts belong to the palaces south of the tell. The hypothesis stays "possible, low".
- **Beth Shean (58).** Perennial springs (SWP II) and a dam and pool of the period (*HA-ESI* 2016) move the feature fit from partial to good. The verdict stays medium, because springs are common in the valley and the final mem of the name remains a problem (Q16).
- **Gerizim (57).** The dated steps and cisterns in the sources checked are later (Hadrian's stairway; Byzantine works). Magen's excavation reports were not available, so this is a limit of the evidence checked, not a claim about every installation on the summit.
  - *Follow-up, 2026-09-29:* Magen's NEAEHL Supplement entry (2008, pp. 1742–1748) is free online but not yet read in full. It reports Hellenistic staircases and a courtyard cistern. The Hellenistic city ends about 111 BCE (BK), so their use in the scroll's period needs a check (Q47).

## 6. Limits

- **Not accessible:** several key reports.
  - Magen on Gerizim.
  - Zertal's Manasseh survey (Ibziq). *Read 2026-09-29* (vol. 2): no conduit is reported. With *HA* 40's Roman kokhim tomb, Ibziq stays medium; see [entry59_bezek_review.md](entry59_bezek_review.md) and the follow-up.
  - Hirschfeld's Herodium survey (print only).
  - Patrich on Mar Saba and Hyrcania. *Located 2026-09-29, not read:* the Hyrcania aqueducts chapter is in the same 1989 Hebrew volume as the Ilan–Amit chapter already obtained; the Mar Saba map is print only.
  - The full Akeldama volume (abstract only). Szanton 2023 has since been read in full; see the review and `sources.md`.
  - Kenyon 1981 (second-hand).
- **Second-hand only:** the Bethesda excavations (Vincent & Abel; Jeremias), cited through the editors.
- **Translations.** The *ʿAtiqot* 41 quotes are our translations from the Hebrew.
- **Old descriptions.** 19th-century descriptions are used for sites with no modern report. They rarely give dates.
