# Zissu 2023: caves, hiding places and cisterns of the late Second Temple period and the Bar Kokhba Revolt

## Overall character of the chapter (evidence)

- This is a synthesis, not a site report. **No coordinates, grid references, cave plans or dimensions are given for any cave.** Caves are named and referenced; details are left to the cited works.
- Dating frame: "the late Second Temple period (first century BCE–70 CE) and up until the days of the Bar Kokhba Revolt (132–136 CE)" (p. 193).
- Counts:
  - First Revolt: Porat & Eshel (2008) report finds "in as many as 40 caves" (p. 204).
  - Bar Kokhba: "As of 2019, 34 caves (or groups of caves)" were known, plus "18 others" with uncertain evidence (pp. 206–207, citing Eshel & Zissu 2019).
  - These lie in three strips: "21 caves in the eastern strip, along the escarpment; 8 caves in the central strip … and 5 caves in the western strip" (p. 207).
- Extent of the refuge caves: "between Wadi er-Rashash (Raviv 2021) in the northeast and Naḥal Shillo in the northwest, with the southernmost … in Naḥal Qina and Yahel, south of ‘Arad (Eshel and Zissu 2019:77, Fig. 110)" (p. 207).

## Flagged areas: what the chapter says

- **Jericho / Wadi Qelt:** items 1–5 (hiding caves with cut cisterns, Pheretae, Ketef Jericho, Jericho Ridge, Qarantal). Also Dok above Jericho (item 20).
- **Hyrcania, Doq:** named only as fortresses (p. 196); no caves or cisterns described individually. Only Alexandrion's cisterns are illustrated.
- **Wadi Qumran:** only "11 caves near Qumran" (p. 194). The nature of Qumran is "beyond the scope of the current paper" (p. 194).
- **Buqeia, Naḥal Kidron / Mar Saba:** **not mentioned.**

## Statements relevant to water (evidence)

- The desert allows storage "if extensive efforts are invested in digging and plastering cisterns, cutting and reinforcing channels to conduct runoff from the slopes, and building dams to divert winter flows … through sedimentation and filtration implements (Ilan 1973)" (p. 185).
- Three refuge caves "had plaster-lined pools to store water: Cave No. 1 in Wadi Murabba‘at, the Cave of the Pool in Naḥal David, and the Cave of the Mikve in Naḥal Harduf" (p. 208).

## What the source does NOT say about the question

- It gives no coordinates, grid references, dimensions, entrance counts, shafts or tunnels for any cave or cistern.
- It says nothing about the Buqeia or Naḥal Kidron / Mar Saba.
- It gives no cistern volumes or details for Hyrcania or Dok (it points to Garbrecht & Peleg 1994).
- It gives no coin or pottery lists per cave; datings are summary statements, with details deferred to Eshel & Zissu 2019, Porat & Eshel 2008, and Eshel & Porat 2009.
- It does not discuss the Copper Scroll.
