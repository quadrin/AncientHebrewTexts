## What this contributes

The book supplies a detailed reassessment of the settlement's northwest water inlet. It does not supply the missing numerical observations or geodetic control for the 2002 exterior-aqueduct survey. No occurrences of Reeder, Schniedewind, Masterman, Ilan, Amit, or “total station” were found in the extracted text. Negative text searches are not a substitute for an exhaustive visual reading of every page.

**Plate XI, PDF p. 151:** The north enclosure plan identifies the inlet, bath 138, Locus 132, channels 500/673, and adjacent walls. Its A–C / 1–4 cells are illustration locators. They are not geographic coordinates. Chapter 11 explicitly says, “The coordinates refer to plates I to XII” (printed p. 129). Chapter 10 cautions that the architectural identification drawings do not establish stratigraphic or chronological associations (printed p. 125). Do not treat everything drawn on a plate as contemporary.

**Figure 174, printed p. 435 / PDF p. 436:** Four diagrams illustrate the authors' proposed sequence at the inlet. This is an interpretive phase reconstruction based on surveys and plans, not four separately surveyed historic landscapes. The text distinguishes:

1. Runoff capture by walls 701/708 and collector 709, feeding channel 673 before the proposed gorge-dam/long-aqueduct project.
2. Construction of wall 599 and replacement with collector 707 and a narrowed sluice 658.
3. Addition of bath 138 and altered supply, including a reconstructed missing feed 700.
4. Reoccupation, disuse of bath 138, and restored channel 500 across the northern enclosure.

Provenance: these are **the authors' proposed phases**, not independently established dates. For the number of phases, printed p. 434 states: “We propose it was in four phases”. For the chronology, the same page describes Level 4 as “after 68”. Their numbered architectural features must not be confused with Ilan–Amit's aqueduct feature numbers.

## Preserved evidence versus reconstruction

**Earlier channel versus restored channel, printed pp. 434–435.** The authors describe “a drain 673 sealed by the more substantial drain 500, of Level 4.” They assign the visible restored channel to the reoccupation. This is a reason to separate construction phases when comparing a route with the Copper Scroll; it does not date the scroll or disprove an identification by itself.

**Bath 138 supply, printed pp. 427, 434–435.** The volume quotes the excavation diary of 4 April 1955: “Impossible to find the point where water arrives in bath/cistern L. 138”. The authors propose an upstream supply but acknowledge missing fabric. Printed p. 435 calls it “missing feed 700” and distinguishes a surviving waste pipe from the reconstructed feed. The position of the sluice and surviving outlet can constrain a reconstruction; the complete feed route should not be plotted as an observed ancient channel.

**Locus 132, printed pp. 415–419.** De Vaux interpreted this area as a large decantation pool. Humbert and Chambon reject that interpretation. Their objections include the height of diversion 677, lack of waterproof coating on other supposed boundaries, different construction phases, the relationship with bath 138's overflow, and buried pottery/bone deposits. Their claim should be recorded as a dispute over the area's function. It is not grounds to label all other Qumran basins doubtful, or to declare this interpretation settled.

**Scope, printed p. 434.** The authors explicitly defer a detailed study of the overall hydraulic system: “Qumran’s hydraulic system is complex and on another occasion we shall undertake a detailed study of it.” Consequently this volume cannot replace the exterior tunnel survey or provide an independent set of tunnel measurements.

## Implications for Copper Scroll candidates

- The volume does not identify the reservoir of Solomon, establish the reading Shallum/Solomon, or connect a particular fissure to the scroll.
- Any proposed identification using Locus 132 as a reservoir needs an explicit function-disputed flag and the older interpretation alongside this reassessment.
- A candidate using the water inlet must specify which phase and which surviving feature it relies on. Reconstructed feed 700 cannot serve as an independently observed match.
- The tunnel/fissure comparison from Masterman 1903, Ilan–Amit 1989 and Magen–Peleg 2018 remains a separate evidence chain. This book does not independently corroborate their exterior-aqueduct measurements.
- No map coordinates, survey registration or identification-confidence scores were changed.

## Follow-up identified in the volume

Printed p. 123 describes two original folded inserts in *Revue Biblique* 63 (1956), at p. 576: a locus-number overlay and a stone-by-stone plan, with a north-oriented 20 × 20 m grid. The grid size is explicitly stated: “a 20 x 20 m grid, oriented to the north.” It is not a global coordinate datum.
