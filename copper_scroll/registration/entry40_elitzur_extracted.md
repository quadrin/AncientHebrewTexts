## A. Upper Beth-Horon (Beit ʿUr el-Foqa) against entry 40

### A0. The readings, and what is new

**The entry.**

- IX 7: בצריחי החורון/החורין ברוח/ב‹צ›ריח הצופא ים/דרום
- IX 8: בזרב חפור אמות שש עסרה
- IX 9: the sum.

**Puech 2006, p. 196** [read; the PDF text layer of the repository copy, part 4, PDF index 11]

- He summarises the edition. Milik chose ḥwrwn and Upper rather than Lower Beth-Ḥoron, with the article standing in for "Beth" (DJD 268). He did so "sans doute sous l'influence de la finale lue hṣwpʾ ym « orienté vers la Mer »".
- Jeremias read ḥwryn, "des Ḥorrites", troglodyte giants whose tombs lay in the Beth Gubrin–Eleutheropolis region "au sud-ouest de ʿAïn en-Naṭoph".
- "Cette lecture qui a maintenant les faveurs de l'éditeur" (n. 338 cites Milik, *RB* 67, 1960, pp. 222–223). Pixner accepts it but puts the place in the Yarmuk region. Puech adds: "il est recommandé de rester dans la même région".
- Direction: "il est possible de lire drwm : traces de dalet, reš suivi d'un waw/yod et mem certain."
- Then: "La lecture drwm assurée retire le principal appui à la lecture ḥwrwn pour l'identification à Beth-Ḥoron le Haut."
- n. 336: Milik's reading and identification are accepted by Wolters (1996), Lefkovits and Wise, "sans doute" by García Martínez and Tigchelaar, and by Bedman.
- n. 337:
  - Jeremias, *RB* 67 (1960) pp. 220–222.
  - de Saulcy mentions a "grotte d'Adoullam ou de Khareitoun" near Herodium.
  - SWP III p. 375 describes the Mughâret Maʿṣa above ʿAin Naṭuf.
  - Abel, *Géographie* I p. 441, judges "cette identification" unfounded.
- זרב: Puech keeps the usual sense "rigole, gouttière, bordure" (DJD 246), in preference to "fosse, bassin" (Milik, García Martínez) and "lit mortuaire" (Pixner).

**Milik, "Observations", *RB* 67 (1960) pp. 222–223** [read; the Internet Archive OCR of the 1960 volume; the Hebrew is garbled in the OCR]

- He quotes his own DJD III manuscript notice, sent to the Clarendon Press in summer 1959. In it:
  - he reads the place as (Bet) Ḥoron, with the article replacing the determinative;
  - "Le choix du terme [ym] au lieu de l'habituel [mʿrb] est intentionnel." The two Hebrew words are garbled in the OCR; my identification of them is [inferred];
  - "Il s'agit sans doute de Bethoron-le-Haut, Beit ʿOr el-Fôq, situé au bout d'un éperon de la montagne" (617 m, overlooking the plain towards the coast).
- He says he had arrived at Ḥoron "en partant de la lecture [ḥwrwn], matériellement aussi bonne que [ḥwryn]". The Hebrew is garbled in the OCR, so which word is which is [inferred] from his argument. So Milik himself treated waw and yod as equally possible on the metal.
- His earlier *RB* 1959 placement of "Tombeaux des Horréens" in the SW necropolis of Jerusalem "m'a paru arbitraire et gratuite".
- After reading Jeremias he would "volontiers revenir à ma première lecture « Tombeaux des Horréens »", with Jeremias's Beth Guvrin identification.
- Conclusion (p. 223): the two identifications are "plausibles à peu près au même degré, mais par quelques détails la première a plus de vraisemblance". He promised to take this into account "dans l'édition définitive".
- (Inference) DJD III (1962) still prints ḥwrwn and Beth-Horon. Puech p. 196 reports "L'édition a opté pour la lecture ḥwrwn", and Lefkovits p. 288 quotes Milik's DJD translation "Among the graves of (Bet) Horon". So the promised change was apparently not made, or the manuscript was already closed. Whether DJD D32 p. 268 mentions Jeremias is **unverified**.

**Jeremias, *RB* 67 (1960) pp. 220–222** [read]

- He reads "Tombeaux des Horréens … l'hypogée qui est face à la Mer" (p. 220). His proposal was therefore made **under the ים reading**.
- His basis:
  - the Midrash: "« Et les Horréens (Gen., xiv, 6) : (c'est) Eleuthéropolis »" (Genesis Rabbah on Gen 14:6, ed. Theodor p. 412; the section number is garbled in the OCR);
  - Jerome, *Comm. in Abdiam* 1 (PL 25, 1104): "Eleutheropolis, ubi ante habitaverunt Horraei";
  - Bet Gubrin as "maison des Géants";
  - the derivation ḥorim < ḥor "cave".
- He points to a vast necropolis of shaft tombs about 2 km north of Beit Gibrin. The *Vitae Prophetarum* call it the "cimetière des Enakim".
  - "Selon toute vraisemblance, c'est cette nécropole qui est visée en 3Q 15 IX 7" (p. 221).
  - He measured one shaft tomb at 4.89 m deep.
  - "Facing the sea": "La nécropole se trouve … à l'ouest de l'ancienne route romaine Jérusalem-Beit Gibrin" (p. 221).
- He also argues from the sequence (see A3).

**Lefkovits 2000, pp. 288–292** [read; OCR text layer, part 6, PDF indices 11–15]

- His translation: "At the catacombs of Horon, in the direction which is facing the sea … by the canal."
- Ḥoron "may well be identical with the city of Beth-horon, as Milik suggests" (p. 289).
- He notes: "Horin, is a variant of Horon" (p. 290). He also lists Hebron (by bet–waw interchange) and the talmudic Hawran as possible readings.
- On ים = west, he cites m. Shevi. 9:2, "from Beth-horon till the sea is a single district" (p. 291).
- He adduces the tannaitic story of a burial pit at "the Rock of Beth-Horon/Horin" (t. Nid.; b. Nid. 61a). "This large pit might had been one of the … 'catacombs of Horon/Horin,' of the Scroll" (p. 292).
- He reads זרב as "canal", or בירך "bottom/side" (p. 292).

**Rabbinic texts** [read on Sefaria]

- t. Niddah 8 (Machon Mamre numbering §3; Lefkovits cites 8:7): "מעשה בסלע בית חורין שהיו מחזיקין בו טומאה … ומצאו שם בור גדול מתים". https://www.sefaria.org/Tosefta_Niddah.8
- b. Niddah 61a, the same story as a baraita of Abba Shaul: "מעשה בסלע בית חורון … ומצאו בור גדול מלא עצמות". https://www.sefaria.org/Niddah.61a

### A1. The name test (Elitzur's two conditions)

**(i) Do the ancient sources fix Beth-Horon? Yes.** [read, except where marked]

- SWP III p. 17 [read]:
  - "This, with the last, represent the Upper and Lower Beth Horon (Joshua xviii. 13, 14)."
  - The Onomasticon gives 12 Roman miles from Jerusalem.
  - Josephus gives 50 stadia from Gibeon and 100 stadia from Jerusalem (*BJ* ii.19.1).
  - SWP measures 5 English miles to el-Jib and 10 miles by road to Jerusalem from the upper village.
  - The two villages are 1.5 miles apart, with a 700 ft descent to et-Taḥta.
  - https://archive.org/details/surveyofwesternp03conduoft (printed p. 17)
- The project's lexicon row `horon` [project] lists the attestations:
  - Josh 10:10–11; 16:3, 5; 21:22; 1 Chr 7:24; 2 Chr 8:5;
  - m. Nedarim 5:6 and m. Sheviit 9:2;
  - Josephus AJ 5.58, 12.287, 12.406, 13.15; BJ 2.228, 2.546;
  - Onomasticon (Wolf n. 212).
- 1 Macc 3:16 and 7:39 [BK; also the Avi-Yonah EJ entry, https://www.encyclopedia.com/religion/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/beth-horon].
- Elitzur 2004 p. 16, no. 51 [read]. See B below.

**(ii) Does Beit ʿUr preserve the name? Yes, with two irregularities, and Elitzur accepts it.** [read]

- Elitzur lists "51. Βηθωρών – בית חורון – [bēt ʿūr]" among his 177 "positive or almost positive identifications" (Preface; list on p. 16).
- p. 297: /ḥ/ > /ʿ/ is rare, attested "in my wide corpus … בית חורון – [bēt ʿūr] [51]".
- pp. 45 and 235: ḥolem > /ū/ is the normal result ([bēt ʿūr] beside Ashdod > [ʾesdūd] and Shiloh > [sēlūn]).
- p. 300: the taw is preserved.
- (Inference) The final -ōn is lost. By Elitzur's own relaxation (p. 13), a poorly preserved name is acceptable when the sources fix the place (his example: al-Ǧīb = Gibeon). So condition (ii) passes.

**Result**

- **Name test for the place Beth-Horon = Beit ʿUr: yes / yes.**
- The test cannot choose between Upper (el-Foqa) and Lower (et-Taḥta). Both villages carry the name.
- Milik's preference for Upper rests on the view from the spur. SWP p. 17 notes of el-Foqa: "The west view is very extensive, including the sea, the plains of Lydda and Ramleh." [read]

**The critical caveat** [inferred, high confidence]

The name test validates Beth-Horon = Beit ʿUr. It does not show that the scroll's word refers to Beth-Horon. The link from scroll to place needs three things:

- (a) החורון/החורין as a toponym with the article standing in for בית. This is Milik's grammatical rule, and he gives parallels in the scroll (*RB* 1960 p. 223).
- (b) The ים reading, which was Milik's own stated motivation.
- (c) Rejection of the ethnonym reading "Horites", which Milik himself briefly preferred.

This is the same structure as Beth-Shean (58), where the place passes but the scroll spells בית שם.

**Phase 5-style verdict** [inferred]

- **Upper Beth-Horon.** The landmark type (rock-cut burial chambers) is reported **partly**: four bench tombs, undated. The bench-and-repository type is typical of Iron II [BK], and Roman sherds make up only 6%. Kokhim tombs with Roman-dominated pottery lie 0.5–0.6 km SW, at Kh. ez-Zeit. Period: not stated at the village.
- **Lower Beth-Horon.** **Yes**: a published kokhim tomb with pottery of the end of the Second Temple period in its fill (Peleg 2004), with a second-hand report of ossuaries.
- **Neither village.** No report of a chamber "facing the sea", of a זרב, or of anything 16 cubits deep. No individual feature is identifiable.
- Kokhim tombs are the standard Jewish tomb type of the period across Judaea and Benjamin. Their presence has **little discriminating value**, as Phase 5 already noted for tombs in general.

### A3. The sequence problem

**Puech** [read]

- p. 175: "(38) à Naṭoph et (39) à (45) dans les environs de Tekoa – Bethléem, (46) à Beth ha-Kérem".
- p. 198: "les colonnes VIII – IX situent les localisations dans une même région et un périmètre assez restreint".
- p. 175: the order "peut difficilement être dû au pur hasard". He concedes that the identifications in cols. I–III are not fully assured.
- Puech's cluster for 39–45 contains **no identified toponym**. Each entry reads a common noun: terrace, hole, bend of a conduit, pit, dovecote/small fort, cistern.
- The cluster is anchored by 38 (ʿAin en-Naṭuf; Phase 5: feature not attested, "rests on the name alone") and 46 (Beth ha-Kerem = Ramat Raḥel; lowered to **low** in Phase 5) [project].
- Puech restores entry 44's last word as בתח[ום ה]דרום "in the district of the south" (p. 198) [read]. (Inference) If that is right, it points south of the Bethlehem–Tekoa area rather than north-west.

**Milik, DJD III** [project; Puech pp. 196–197]

- His placements for 38–45 are scattered:
  - 38 ʿAin en-Naṭuf;
  - 39 Tekelet ha-Šani (unidentified; *RB* 1960 n. 1 calls the identification "désespérée");
  - 40 Upper Beth-Horon;
  - 41 Qobʿeh, on the Eleutheropolis–Jerusalem road (an emendation);
  - 42 Kephar Nebo, at the ʿAyun Musa cascade in Transjordan (Puech p. 197; DJD 265);
  - 44 Masada.
- 43: the project's Phase 2 attributes Bet Tamar near Gibeah to Milik. Puech p. 197 n. 366 attributes it to **Beyer**. This needs a DJD check.
- Beth-Horon is no more out of sequence in Milik's scheme than his other placements.

**Milik 1960 and Jeremias used the sequence *against* Beth-Horon.** [read]

- Milik: "L'ordre géographique des cachettes est également favorable". His sequence:
  - Kidron (VIII 4–9);
  - the SW necropolis (VIII 10–16);
  - the Judaean desert S of Jerusalem (IX 1–6);
  - Bet Guvrin (IX 7–9);
  - the Eleutheropolis–Jerusalem road (IX 10).
- That chain depends on 41 = Qobʿeh, an emendation the project rates weak or ruled out [project].

**How much weight** [inferred]

- The project's rule 3 says a sequence argument alone cannot make a site best-supported. The corollary used here is that a sequence misfit alone should not rule one out.
- The misfit is real under Puech's scheme. But that scheme is itself partly a sequence construct, resting on two anchors of medium and low confidence and on common-noun readings.
- Jeremias concedes the list is "pas disposé selon un ordre strictement géographique" (p. 221). The scroll makes long jumps elsewhere: 57 Gerizim, 58 Beth Shean, 59 Bezek.
- Weight: **moderate against Beth-Horon**. It is enough to block "best-supported", not enough to drop it below "possible".

### A5. Candidate assessment and recommended Phase 3 status

**Upper Beth-Horon (Beit ʿUr el-Foqa)**

- Site anchor: 31.88530 N, 35.11341 E. This is the IAA survey point for sites 28 and 143, a village-level anchor with about ±300 m uncertainty. It is **not** a feature.
- Lower Beth-Horon anchor: 31.89475 N, 35.08591 E.

**Confidence by axis**

- **Reading: low–medium.**
  - ים leans on the plates, but Puech saw traces of דרום on better images.
  - The toponym needs the article = "Beth" rule.
  - Milik himself rated "Horites" at Beth Guvrin at least equally plausible, and slightly more (*RB* 1960 p. 223).
  - The waw/yod question is neutral, because בית חורין is attested in the Tosefta.
- **Site: low.**
  - The name test passes for Beth-Horon = Beit ʿUr (Elitzur no. 51), but the link from scroll to place is reading-dependent (rule 4 caps it at "possible").
  - Upper versus Lower cannot be decided. The only published tomb of the period is at **Lower** Beth-Horon.
  - The sequence is poor under Puech's scheme.
- **Feature: low.**
  - Rock-cut tombs exist: four undated bench tombs on the W slope at el-Foqa; Roman-dominated kokhim sites 0.5–1.6 km away; a kokhim tomb at et-Taḥta with pottery of the end of the Second Temple period.
  - A chamber "facing the sea", a זרב, or a 16-cubit depth is reported nowhere.
  - Kokhim are ubiquitous, so the fit is type-level only.

**Recommended Phase 3 status** (conservative)

1. **Keep entry 40 at "possible only"**, with no best-supported place.
2. **Add "Beth-Horon (Beit ʿUr el-Foqa / et-Taḥta)" as a possible place, confidence low.** Label it conditional on the ים reading and on Milik's toponym analysis. Name test yes / yes for the place, reading-dependent. Feature fit partial, type only. Sequence poor under Puech. If the project wants a single point, use Upper, following Milik, and state in the note that Lower is equally compatible and has the only tomb with pottery of the period.
3. **Keep ʿAin en-Naṭuf as possible, low.** Its reason should say it rests on sequence alone. It has no Horite tradition and no reported tombs.
4. **Record the Beit Gibrin shaft-tomb necropolis** (Jeremias) at weak or possible-low. Cite Milik's own 1960 preference. Record Milik's SW-Jerusalem placement as withdrawn.
5. Do **not** upgrade on the plate check alone. The plate check restores the textual basis Milik used, but not a decision.

## 12-line summary

1. Milik's own *RB* 67 (1960) pp. 222–223 note matters most. He rated "Horites at Beth Guvrin" and "Beth-Horon" about equally plausible, with Horites slightly ahead.
2. Jeremias (*RB* 1960 pp. 220–222) proposed the Horite tombs under the ים reading. His basis is the Midrash and Jerome linking the Horites to Eleutheropolis, and its shaft-tomb necropolis.
3. t. Niddah spells the place בית חורין, so Puech's yod does not exclude Beth-Horon. The direction word and Milik's article-for-"Beth" rule carry the weight.
4. Name test for the place: yes / yes. Elitzur lists Beth-Horon = [bēt ʿūr] as no. 51 of his 177 almost-positive identifications (p. 16; phonology pp. 45, 235, 297, 300).
5. That test does not tie the scroll's word to Beth-Horon, and it cannot choose between the Upper and Lower villages.
6. Upper Beth-Horon: SWP p. 86 reports no tombs. The IAA/Benjamin survey no. 143 has four undated bench tombs on the W slope; Roman sherds are only 6%.
7. Kh. ez-Zeit, 0.6 km SW, has 5 kokhim/arcosolia tombs (site pottery 70% Roman). Lower Beth-Horon has a kokhim tomb with end-of-Second-Temple pottery (Peleg, *HA-ESI* 116, 2004).
8. No report anywhere gives a chamber facing the sea, a זרב channel, or a 16-cubit depth. The feature fit is type-level only, and kokhim tombs are ubiquitous.
9. Sequence: Beit ʿUr lies 27–29 km NNW of Puech's Naṭuf/Herodium cluster. That weighs moderately against it, but Jeremias's Beit Guvrin is further off, at 32 km.
10. Recommendation: keep "possible only". Add Beth-Horon as possible (low, conditional on ים). Keep Naṭuf possible (low, sequence only). Record Beit Guvrin as weak or possible-low.
11. Elitzur supports the Doq, Siloam and Bezek passes. He counts Beth-shean = Bēsān as positive, so its "partial" is the scroll's mem. He is silent on Achor, Secacah, Kozeba and Naʿaran.
12. Fixes: the Dok note is entry 27 n. 2, not entry 28; Beth ha-Kerem is p. 185 n. 2; Bet Tamar near Gibeah is Beyer's per Puech. DJD III was not re-read (restricted).
