# Masterman 1903: aqueduct extraction

E. W. G. Masterman, “Notes on Some Ruins and a Rock-Cut Aqueduct in the Wady Kumran,” Palestine Exploration Fund Quarterly Statement 35(3), pp. 264–267. Public PDF downloaded and all four article pages read on 28 September 2026; printed pp. 266–267 additionally checked visually. The download has five PDF pages including a distributor cover.

Provenance V: explicit primary-source text. Quotations below come from printed p. 267 / PDF p. 5; line wrapping has been normalized.

> From the natural, somewhat funnel-shaped beginning the aqueduct runs for 60 feet as an open rock-cut channel, it then traverses for about 40 feet a rock-cut tunnel now blocked up at both ends; thence it runs about 30 feet along the side of the rock by what must have been a half built-up channel, one side being of natural rock, the other built up;

> This tunnel, which can be traversed throughout, is 3 feet high, 2 feet wide, and 43 feet long.

> It is broken at the bottom, 11 feet from the entrance, at a point where the floor has been partially built up of stone, and another 12 feet along there is a small window, made probably accidentally, above the level reached by the water, looking to the south.

The second quotation describes the second, accessible tunnel; the preceding blocked tunnel is a separate feature. Masterman's estimate for that blocked segment should not be equated automatically with a later short-tunnel length.

For comparison only, multiplying the printed feet by 0.3048 gives 13.1064 m for the accessible tunnel, 3.3528 m for the first break's offset, and 3.6576 m for the interval to the next opening. These are derived conversions, not measurements accurate to four decimal places. Rounded comparisons are approximately 13.1 m, 3.4 m, and 3.7 m.

Against Magen–Peleg 2018's reported long tunnel and fissures, this is a plausible correspondence in sequence, scale, and south-facing openings. Lengths and descriptions differ: Masterman distinguishes a floor break from a further elevated window; Magen describes the first crack as a window affecting wall and bottom, followed by another repaired southern crack. Retain these differences. Later authors may have used Masterman, so agreement alone does not prove independent measurement.

Figure 2, printed p. 266 / PDF p. 4, photographs an open rock-cut channel near its beginning. The image contains no coordinate grid or measured scale. A gun appears in the channel; its unknown dimensions cannot provide a defensible metric scale.

This report records aqueduct features before the Copper Scroll's discovery and before the later excavation reports. It supports a historical comparison of physical features. It supplies neither a Copper Scroll identification nor modern survey control. Geographic registration and candidate confidence remain unchanged.
