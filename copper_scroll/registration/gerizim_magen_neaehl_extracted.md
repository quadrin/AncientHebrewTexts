# Mount Gerizim (entry 57, Q47): were the staircases and the mansion cistern in use after c. 111–110 BCE?

## Short answer

**The sources give no evidence that any staircase or the mansion cistern was used or repaired between 110 BCE and 70 CE.**

- Magen states that "The city and temple stood abandoned through the Hasmonean and Roman periods" (p. 1742) (evidence).
- He also writes: "There is a break in the numismatic record at Mount Gerizim from after Jannaeus until the beginning of the fourth century CE" (p. 1747) (evidence).
- The one thin counter-signal is in Gerizim III: three coins of Festus (58/59 CE) from a single locus in Area P, which NEAEHL identifies as "The Mansion (Area P)" (p. 1745). See below. Magen treats all 1st-c. BCE–1st-c. CE coins as stray finds (evidence).

## History (NEAEHL p. 1742, evidence)
- Temple "first built in the mid-fifth century BCE". A city grew "at the end of the fourth century BCE". Under Antiochus III "the temple and its compound were rebuilt and the city expanded greatly".
- "Based on the coins found at the site, it is clear that the temple and the city were destroyed by John Hyrcanus around 110 BCE, and not in 128 BCE".
- "The city and temple stood abandoned through the Hasmonean and Roman periods, during which the Samaritans made a number of unsuccessful attempts to reestablish their control of the mountain" (citing *Ant.* XVIII 85–89; *War* III 307–315).
- Later phases:
  - The temple of Zeus on the northern ridge was inaugurated "During the reign of Antiochus Pius (160 CE)" (as printed; i.e. Antoninus Pius) and completed by a Severan.
  - "At the beginning of the fourth century … the Samaritans reconstructed the sacred precinct".
  - "In the Late Roman period, a fortress was erected near the precinct". In 484 CE Zeno built the church of Mary Mother of God on the ruins; Justinian rebuilt it (p. 1742).

## The mansion and its cistern (NEAEHL p. 1745, evidence)
- "The Mansion (Area P). To the west of the sacred precinct, a large mansion … composed of three wings: an olive-oil press (building I), a dwelling and commercial section (buildings II–III, V), and luxurious living quarters (building IV)."
  - "Measuring 40 by 40 m, the complex has five entrances". It was "constructed abutting the wall of the sacred precinct and in certain places has been preserved up to its second story".
- Building I, "22 by 19 m", held an olive-oil press. It "may originally have been a dwelling, which was converted into an olive-oil press at a later stage". "Later" is **not dated**, so it gives no evidence of post-110 BCE use (inference).
- **The cistern:** building IV "measured 20 by 14 m and was built around a stone-paved central courtyard with a cistern".
  - It had a reception room "(13 by 5.5 m) with walls covered in stucco and colored frescoes" — "the only example of colored frescoes known so far on Mount Gerizim".
  - **The cistern's dimensions, date, fill and latest finds are not given.**
- Placement: the mansion sits within the list of Hellenistic residential quarters (p. 1745). No later phase is described (evidence of absence).

## Coins bearing on 110 BCE–70 CE

- NEAEHL p. 1747 (evidence): "some 16,000 coins". "From the Hasmonean period, 546 coins were recovered, dated to the reigns of John Hyrcanus, Aristobulus, and Alexander Jannaeus". Then comes the numismatic break quoted above, "but numerous Late Roman, Byzantine, Late Islamic, and Crusader period coins were retrieved."
- Gerizim III (evidence):
  - "the latest coins … related to the destruction … are the coins of Alexander Jannaeus"; "After being destroyed, the site was abandoned for around 400 years" (p. 59).
  - Seven 1st-c. BCE–1st-c. CE coins "are not related to historic events occurring at the site, but came there by chance" (p. 59).
  - "No coins have been found from the two Samaritan attempts … Pontius Pilate … and Vespasian, in 67 CE" (p. 59).
  - Find-spots (Catalogue p. 159):

| Cat. no. | Coin | Area / Locus |
|---|---|---|
| 411 | Augustus, 7 BCE | Area S, L7027 |
| 412 | Ambibulus, 9/10 CE | Area S, surface |
| 413 (×3) | Festus, 58/59 CE | **Area P, L5178** |
| 414 | Ascalon, late 1st c. CE | Area S, L443 |
| 415 | Nero, 68 CE | Area S, L461 |

- **Inference:** NEAEHL (p. 1745) confirms that Area P is the mansion, so the three Festus coins come from somewhere in the mansion complex. Neither source says which building, room or installation L5178 is, so they cannot be tied to building IV's courtyard cistern. Magen dismisses them as chance finds (Gerizim III p. 59). A single-locus group of three coins of the same issue is a weak sign of some 1st-c. CE presence in the mansion ruins. It is not evidence of the cistern being used.
- The Jannaeus coins run to 76 BCE (BK), slightly past the 110 BCE destruction. NEAEHL does not say where on the site they were found.

## Roman and Byzantine reuse of the summit (evidence)
- Temple of Zeus on the northern ridge, 160 CE onward (NEAEHL p. 1742). It is not on the Hellenistic city summit (Gerizim III pp. 23, 46).
- The sacred precinct was reconstructed by the Samaritans in the early 4th c. CE (p. 1742). Its cult is covered on p. 1748: "Dozens of Samaritan inscriptions in Greek".
- A "Late Roman fortress" (plan key no. 2, p. 1743; text p. 1742) stood at the precinct's NE corner.
- Church of Mary Mother of God, 484 CE, "destroyed the Hellenistic compound to its foundations, with only its outer walls surviving" (p. 1746).

## Internal inconsistencies in NEAEHL (evidence, noted as printed)
- p. 1746: the temple was "rebuilt following their destruction by John Hyrcanus". This contradicts p. 1742; it is probably an editing slip.
- p. 1746 says the precinct "remained abandoned until the fifth century CE". p. 1742 says the 4th-century Samaritan reconstruction came first.
- p. 1747: "not destroyed in 128 BCE after the death of Antiochus IV, but in 110 BCE during the reign of Antiochus IX". Here "IV" should read VII (compare p. 1742, "Antiochus VII Sidetes").

## What the sources do NOT say about the question
- No use, repair, blocking or deposit dated after 110 BCE for any of the three staircases.
- No dimensions, date, fill or latest finds for the mansion cistern, and no description of any other cistern in Area P.
- Which structure L5178 (the Festus coins) belongs to.
- No latest-find list per area or locus in the NEAEHL entry.
- Whether the Late Roman fortress or 4th-c. restoration reused the eastern (23 m) staircase. The eastern gate is described only in its Hellenistic form.
- The fuller locus-level answer would be in *Mount Gerizim Excavations* II, *A Temple City* (JSP 8, 2008), which was not read.
