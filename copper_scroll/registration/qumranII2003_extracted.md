## Galor: plans, remeasurement and inlet interpretation

Katharina Galor, “Plastered Pools, A New Perspective,” printed pp. 291–320 / PDF pp. 321–350.

Printed p. 291 states that the study used the excavation archive and visits under a permit for July–December 2000. It reports: “Father Jean-Baptiste Humbert, with the assistance of the author, has remeasured and newly recorded the pools.” The following sentences explain that original plans were corrected where necessary and sections newly drawn. These are renewed observations of settlement installations, distinct from the exterior-aqueduct survey undertaken in 2002.

Printed p. 294 / PDF p. 324, Figure 3, shows Locus 138 in plan and section, with a north arrow, staircase and scale bar marked 0–3 m. Visual inspection confirms these features. The figure supports identification of the bath and comparison of its openings with later plans. It contains no geographic coordinates or survey-control table.

On the same page, Galor describes the aqueduct reaching Locus 136 at the northwest corner of the settlement, then continuing around the eastern side of bath 138 and the eastern extremity of Locus 130 toward circular pool 110. She records de Vaux's catchment/decantation interpretation but questions its operation as a combined system. This is an earlier stage of the research later developed in Humbert–Chambon's volume IIIA. Shared personnel, archive material and research history make it inappropriate to count the two interpretations as independent corroboration.

Printed p. 318, note 12, also records a disagreement over the southwestern opening of bath 138: de Vaux interpreted it as an overflow, while Strobel interpreted it as a supply channel. The identification of an opening and the interpretation of flow through it are separate propositions.

## Shimron: plaster samples and proposed chronology

Aryeh E. Shimron, “An Archaeometric Study of Plasters from the Water Installations,” printed pp. 321–337 / PDF pp. 351–367.

Printed p. 323 / PDF p. 353, Figure 2, photographs a plaster section labelled “Q10b (Upper Aqueduct).” Figure 1 maps the settlement installations on a base credited to de Vaux and Avigdor Orgad. The upper-aqueduct sample photograph supplies neither an exact sampling coordinate nor a demonstrated association with a particular tunnel or numbered inlet feature.

Printed pp. 335–336 discuss chemical groupings and masonry styles. Shimron tentatively associates construction of installation 71 with the last plaster applications in cistern 110 and the upper aqueduct. He explicitly calls this a “tentative conclusion” based on cadmium content. Treat it as a proposed relative association of repair phases; it supplies no independent absolute date for the inlet or tunnels.

Printed p. 336 also states that the study found no unambiguous inorganic chemical signature separating cisterns, ritual baths and industrial installations. At p. 335, the author acknowledges that evaporite accumulation could postdate use. Chemistry therefore informs comparison of materials and repairs but does not establish a pool's ancient name or specific Copper Scroll association.

## Effect on entry 21 candidates

This source-specific working order preceded the cross-survey synthesis. The [current ranked comparison](../entry21_feature_comparison.md) separates Reeder’s upstream rock, the short-tunnel branch and the long tunnel; it retains the settlement inlet as a secondary semantic alternative.

The working comparison remains conditional on identifying the scroll's conduit with Qumran's water system. Rank by functional fit, rather than by the amount of surviving illustration:

1. **Upstream intake, Ilan–Amit point 3:** strongest candidate for the head of the whole aqueduct. Its position at the channel's beginning is the principal support. The named-site association, northern phrase and stone reading remain separate tests.
2. **Settlement inlet, Locus 136 / sluice 658 in the later architectural numbering:** candidate for the head of the settlement's distribution channel. Galor's plan and description make the neighboring bath and channel route more explicit. This interpretation requires “head” to refer to that local subsystem. The phase of the chosen inlet fabric also matters.
3. **Branch junction 8/9 and tunnel mouths 10/11 in Ilan–Amit:** alternatives if “head” means the start of a branch or enclosed section. They lie downstream of the whole aqueduct's intake. Distinguish each branch and tunnel rather than treating their entrances as one feature.

This is an interpretive working order, not a numerical probability estimate. The new volume improves the documented geometry around candidate 2; it does not resolve the textual assumptions that distinguish candidates 1–3. Geographic points and atlas confidence scores have not changed.

## Specific source leads

Galor's printed p. 318, note 7, names Masterman 1903, Schulz 1960 and Ilan–Amit as aqueduct investigations. It then states: “Humbert was the first to mention and describe the dam in detail, HUMBERT and CHAMBON 1994: 192 and 342.” This gives exact pages to inspect in the 1994 volume. Until those pages are read, the dam's identity and correspondence with Ilan–Amit point 4, point 19 or Reeder's upstream wall remain open.

The bibliography on printed p. 320 identifies:

- S. Schulz, “Chirbet Kumran, ‘en Feschcha und die Buke’a,” *ZDPV* 76 (1960), pp. 50–72. The publisher's issue contents confirms the article beginning at p. 50: https://www.palaestina-verein.de/wp/wordpress/?portfolio=zdpv-76-1960
- A. Strobel, *ZDPV* 88 (1972), pp. 55–86. Galor prints the title with “der”; the publisher's contents gives “Die Wasseranlagen von Hirbet Qumran”: https://www.palaestina-verein.de/wp/wordpress/?portfolio=zdpv-88-1972

The publisher pages were read; they offer contents downloads. The article texts were not recovered in this review. A search of the local research Markdown, CSV and JSON found no occurrences of Schulz, Galor, Shimron or Strobel before this extraction; that scoped check does not establish absence from every source in the wider GitHub repository.

Galor's note 7 dates the English Ilan–Amit chapter to 2002, while her bibliography prints 2000. Retain the discrepancy and verify against the chapter's own title/copyright material before changing the established citation.
