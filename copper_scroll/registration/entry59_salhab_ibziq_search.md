# Entry 59 (Bezek): is a conduit, drain or large water channel reported at Kh. Salhab or Kh. Ibziq?

## Short answer

**No source found describes a conduit, drain or water channel at either site.** Every water feature reported is a **cistern** (plus silos and caves). (evidence of absence across the sources below; Zertal's own text not re-checked)

## Hits, source by source

### 1. SWP *Memoirs* II (1882), full text
- **Kh. es Selhab (O m)**, p. 240: "Traces of ruins." The entry then quotes Guérin (below) in English: "a hill whose rocky sides are pierced by numerous cisterns … circular heaps round silos or sub-terranean magazines cut in the rock" (p. 240). Page number from IA page map, leaf 282 = p. 240. (evidence)
- **Kh. Ibzik (O m)**, p. 237: "Evidently an ancient site, with traces of ruins, cisterns and caves … There is a kubbeh in the ruins sacred to Sheikh Hazkin" (p. 237). (evidence)
- **Bezek**, Sheet XII, Section A, p. 231: the *Onomasticon*'s two Bezecs "17 miles from Neapolis" are "evidently Khurbet Ibzik (O m), 14 English miles from Nablus" (p. 231). (evidence)
- Also: "Wady es Selhab", a flat arable valley near Zebabdeh (pp. 221, 229), is a different place name from the ruin. (evidence)
- No channel, aqueduct or conduit is mentioned at either site. (evidence of absence)

### 2. Guérin, *Samarie* I (1874), p. 355, full text
- "Kharbet Salhab, petite ville entièrement détruite, sur une colline dont les flancs rocheux sont percés de nombreuses citernes antiques" (p. 355). The remains lie "en tas circulaires autour de silos ou de magasins souterrains pratiqués dans le roc" (p. 355). He notes "Au nord s'élève le Djebel Bezeik" (p. 355). (evidence)
- No conduit or channel is mentioned. (evidence of absence)

### 3. *Hadashot Arkheologiyot* 40 (Oct. 1971), printed p. 22 (PDF p. 23; index p. 44: "Kh. Ibziq 22")
- "ח' אבזיק": at Kh. Ibziq, "המזוהה עם בזק המקראית" (identified with biblical Bezek), a **burial cave** was excavated by the staff officer for archaeology, Abdul Rauf Majid. (evidence)
  - A rock-cut façade with a barrel-vaulted ceiling above it.
  - A small opening leads to a burial chamber "(4x5 מ')" with loculi (כוכים).
  - Square closing stones and many lamp and vessel fragments "מן התקופה הרומית (מאות הא' - הב' לסה"נ)" (Roman period, 1st–2nd c. CE); "נראה שהקבר נשדד עוד בימי קדם" (the tomb was apparently robbed in antiquity) (p. 22).
- No water installation is mentioned. The report does not say whether this is Upper or Lower Ibziq. (evidence)

### 4. Gaß, "Besek", *WiBiLex* 2011 (a summary of Zertal and earlier work), PDF pp. 5–6
- Kh. Ibzīq I: 1878.1971; Ibzīq II: 1881.1980 (PDF p. 5).
- On Ibzīq II: "zahlreiche Gebäudereste aus großen zugehauenen Steinen, sowie viele Zisternen und an die 70 Grabanlagen … Weinpressen mit griechischen Inschriften"; pottery "vor allem in die römisch-byzantinische Zeit" (PDF p. 5). (evidence, secondary)
- Kh. Salhab: "(Koordinaten: 1853.1957; N 32° 21' 15'', E 35° 22' 25'')", proposed as original Bezek "(Zertal 105)". Surface finds "in die Eisenzeit I/II, in die persische und in die römische bis arabische Zeit (Zertal 151f)"; "Überreste von Fundamenten und Wänden" of Roman–Byzantine date; "in der Spätbronzezeit nicht besiedelt" (PDF pp. 5–6). (evidence, secondary)
- No conduit or channel is mentioned at either site. (evidence of absence)

## What the sources do NOT say about the question

- No drain, conduit, aqueduct or channel at either site, in any source seen.
- No dated water installation. The cisterns are called "antiques" (Guérin) or undated (SWP).
- Unchecked: the ʿAtiqot 2026 watchtower article, the *HA–ESI* Ḥorbat Rosh report, and Zertal's own site descriptions, which are the most likely place for a channel to be recorded.
