## 3. The two aqueducts: sources, lengths, dates

**Evidence (snippet), p. 17.** This is from the volume's introductory summary table, not Patrich's chapter. The page number is partly hidden in the snippet header ("…7"). The same table continues with Jerusalem, Samaria etc., so I read it as p. 17. Hyrcania has two aqueducts:

| Aqueduct | Source | Length (km) | Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| short / lower / southern | run-off, W[adi] Abu Shu'la | 1.95 | Hasmonean |
| longer / upper / northern | run-off, N[ahal] Qidron | 9 | Herodian |

- **Inference.** This matches Garbrecht & Peleg's table ("~2 km; ~9 km", "Diversion from two Wadis"). It names the second wadi they left unnamed: Wadi Abu Shu'la.
- **Evidence (snippet), p. 20 (introduction).** "the 20 cisterns at Hyrcania held about 20,000 m³". Also: "The water cisterns of the fortresses had rock-cut ceilings, while those of Qumran were like open pools, presumably protected by a covering cloth, or by a second storey."
  - **Inference.** 20 cisterns / 20,000 m³ differs from Garbrecht & Peleg (16 cisterns / about 16,000 m³ plus 3 pools of about 4,000 m³). 16 + 3 = 19, close to 20, and 16,000 + 4,000 = 20,000. The introduction may be counting the pools as cisterns. Not confirmed.

### Bridges at the fortress

- **Evidence (snippet), p. 344.** "… aqueduct, on the N side of the older bridge, shows that the water for it came from the north, from the aqueduct of Wadi Abu Shula. From the W bridge, the two aqueducts flowed on slightly different courses. The earlier aqueduct runs at a higher level; the course of the lower or Byzantine aqueduct is marked by the footpath."
- **Evidence (snippet), p. 344.** "The central bridge. The bridge (c.50 m in length) spans the next saddle. Bedouin sheepfolds are now attached to either side of the bridge; the footpath continues at a lower level. The Byzantine aqueduct is not attached to the N face of the …" (the snippet ends there).
- **Evidence (snippet), p. 351.** "After it crossed the bridge, the aqueduct divided into two branches (N and S) that encompassed the hill and supplied water to the rock-cut cisterns on its slopes. The N branch supplied the east cisterns. On the S side, Feldman found remains of two aqueducts on two different levels. These two southern aqueducts supplied the south cisterns. It is clear, however, that at any given period the aqueduct crossed the bridge at one level only. The lower aqueduct is to be associated with the Hasmonean bridge (the lower of the two), while the upper …" (the snippet ends there).
- **Evidence (snippet), p. 351.** "This area with the N pools served as a kind of moat that cut off the fortress from the ridge that approached the site from the west (the easiest way of approach)."
- **Inference.** Features named by position, as the prompt asks: "the W bridge", "the central bridge" (c. 50 m, "the next saddle"), "the older bridge", "the Hasmonean bridge (the lower of the two)", "the N pools", "the east cisterns", "the south cisterns", the N and S branches, "the lower or Byzantine aqueduct", "the earlier aqueduct … at a higher level".
- **Inference, a point to check in the full text.** p. 344 calls the *lower* aqueduct west of the W bridge "Byzantine". p. 351 associates the *lower* southern aqueduct with the *Hasmonean* bridge. These are different sectors, so they need not conflict, but the snippets cannot settle it.
