# Entry 40: Beth-Horon, Beit Guvrin or Naṭuf? The three open sources (Q45)

## Page numbers: how they were fixed

- **Jeremias.** IA returns leaf numbers, and the item's page-number file is access-restricted. The book's own index gives "bet dschibrin 24, 73f., 81, 83f., [86]f., 100f." and "Maresa 83". Those match the leaves where the words occur (leaves 28, 77–78, 85, 87–88, 90–91, 104–105) with **printed page = leaf − 4** in every case. A cross-reference inside the text confirms it: "(s. o. S. 82)", on leaf 89, refers to the passage on leaf 86. Page numbers below use this offset. **Inference**, but checked against 6 independent index entries.
- **DJD III.** The running heads come back in search: leaf 287 = "D. SITES ET MONUMENTS 267", leaf 289 = "269", and so on. So leaf 288 = **p. 268**. **Evidence** (running heads).

## 1. Jeremias 1958: which tomb or necropolis at Beit Guvrin, and what date

The Beit Guvrin discussion in pp. 82–86 is **no. 32, "Der Prophet Micha (chirbet el-basal)"**. The site is 2 km north of Beit Guvrin, not in the town.

- **Evidence, p. 82.** Section heading: "32. Der Prophet Micha (chirbet el-basal)". The Vitae prophetarum place Micah's tomb "ganz in der Nähe des Friedhofes der Enakiter" (close to the cemetery of the Anakim). The note refers to Jeremias, "Moreseth-Gath, die Heimat des Propheten Micha", PJB 29 (1933), 42–53.
- **Evidence, p. 83.** Maresha and Beit Guvrin: "40 v.Chr. wurde die Ortschaft Maresa von den Parthern vernichtet … Die Reste der Bevölkerung siedelten sich 1,5 km weiter nördlich an der Stelle des heutigen bet dschibrin an." A footnote gives the city status under Septimius Severus: "Beginn der Ära von Eleutheropolis: 199/200".
- **Evidence, p. 84.** The giants' tradition is linked to "die außerordentlich große Zahl mächtiger Höhlen, die sich rings um bet dschibrin finden". Late Jewish tradition sought Moresheth-Gath "in der nächsten Umgebung von bet dschibrin". Eusebius is quoted: Moresheth lies "πρὸς ἀνατολὰς Ἐλευθεροπόλεως" (Greek garbled in the OCR).
- **Evidence, p. 85.** Sozomenos: Bishop Zebennos found Micah's bones in 385 at "Βηρασαβα [OCR garbled] (= birath satia — Kastell Satia)", "etwa zehn Stadien von der Stadt Eleutheropolis". The monument was called "Grabmal des Getreuen", Aramaic "nephscha [di]mehemena", and "scheint eine besonders prächtige Grabanlage gewesen zu sein".
- **Evidence, pp. 85–86.** Date of the Jewish tomb monument: "so wird man mit der Möglichkeit, ja Wahrscheinlichkeit rechnen müssen, daß der jüdische Grabbau über dem Michagrab bis in die Tage Jesu zurückreicht."
- **Evidence, p. 86. The necropolis and its date.** On 4 Oct 1932, with A. Alt, "2 km nördlich von bet dschibrin" they left the Roman road Eleutheropolis–Jerusalem westward and "stießen … auf eine ausgedehnte Nekropole von **seleukidischen Schachtgräbern**. Etwa hundert Gräber liegen in unregelmäßigen Reihen nebeneinander und bilden ein großes Viereck."
  - Measurements: "Tiefe von 4,389 m von der Bodenoberfläche bis zur Sohle der Grabanlage"; "je zwei Grabtröge von 0,60 (0,70) m Breite und 1,30 m Länge sowie eine rechteckige Nebenkammer von 2,10 zu 1,84 m".
  - "Diese bedeutende Schachtgrab-Nekropole besitzt im ganzen Innern Palästinas an Größe nicht ihresgleichen." He identifies it as the "Friedhof der Enakiter".
- **Evidence, p. 86.** The church: "ein größerer Bau von 15,70 m ostwestlicher Länge zu 10,30 m nordsüdlicher Breite, offensichtlich eine Kirche, auf der 350 m entfernten chirbet el-basal (die nach dem keramischen Befund in römischer und byzantinischer Zeit besiedelt war)" is the Micah church. "tell ed-dschdêde" is Moresheth-Gath, with an "in frührömischer Zeit angelegte[s] Kastell" (Kastell Satia).
- **Evidence, p. 87.** Tell ej-Judeideh is "3½ km nördlich von bet dschibrin" (OCR "31, km").

**Summary (evidence).** Jeremias discusses a **necropolis of about 100 Seleucid-period shaft tombs** 2 km north of Beit Guvrin ("cemetery of the Anakim"). He also discusses **the tomb of Micah** at Kh. el-Basal nearby, whose Jewish monument he thinks probably goes back to the time of Jesus, with the bones "found" in 385 CE. **Inference:** the only date he gives for the necropolis itself is "seleukidisch", i.e. Hellenistic, 2nd century BCE. He says nothing about the direction the tombs face.

Other Beit Guvrin references in the book (context only): p. 24 (Jonah / Gath near Eleutheropolis); p. 69 (Paul of Eleutheropolis builds a chapel in the Kidron); p. 73 (Zechariah at zakarija "12 km nördlich von bet dschibrin", 415 CE); p. 74 (Ezra's tomb at eschwa'); p. 81 (Habakkuk at Kh. Kila, 12 km east); pp. 100–101 (Nahum "südlich bet dschibrin").

## 2. Milik, DJD III (1962): D32, p. 268, and the Addenda

- **Evidence, p. 268 (D 32; text of 3Q15 ix 7–9).** "Egalement dans un cimetière se trouve le dépôt suivant, de 22 talents (d'or et d'argent), ix 7-9. … Le tombeau 'est orienté vers la Mer (Méditerranée)', ix 7. Le choix du terme [ים] au lieu de l'habituel [מערב] est intentionnel. Il s'agit sans doute de Bethoron-le-Haut, Beit 'Or el-Fôqâ, situé au bout d'un éperon de la montagne, 617 m. d'altitude, qui surplombe la plaine s'étendant vers la côte."
- **Evidence, p. 268. Does Milik cite Jeremias here? Yes, but not the Heiligengräber book.** The sentence reads: "Une localisation alternative, assez attrayante: J. Jeremias, Revue Biblique, xvii, 1960, pp. 220-2."
  - **Inference.** "xvii" is almost certainly an OCR error for "lxvii". *RB* 67 is the 1960 volume; OCR errors like "Ixili" for "lxiii" occur elsewhere in the same text. So Milik cites **Jeremias, RB 67 (1960) 220–222**, not *Heiligengräber* pp. 82–86, and does not say what the "alternative localisation" is. **Check RB 67:220–222 to find out whether it is the Beit Guvrin necropolis.**
  - The Hebrew words in the passage are garbled in the OCR. [ים] and [מערב] are my reconstruction from the French ("la Mer … au lieu de l'habituel …"). **BK / inference.**
- **Evidence, pp. 271–272.** Milik cites *Heiligengräber* elsewhere, but for other items:
  - D 40 (xi 3): "le Tombeau des (Grands) Prêtres, dans la vallée du Cédron; J. Jeremias, Heiligengräber in Jesu Umwelt, pp. 62-64 … il n'est pas à identifier avec ceux-ci, comme le voudrait Jeremias."
  - D 42 (xi 8): the tomb of Huldah, "Jeremias, l.c., pp. 51-53".
  - A later item: Beit Ummar, "voir Jeremias, l.c., p. 88".
- **Evidence (absence), Addenda pp. 299–302.** A full-text search for "Jeremias" returns 5 hits, all in the D section (pp. 268, 271 ×2, 272 ×2). **None is in the Addenda.** The Addenda search hits cite D 20a (p. 300) and other items (pp. 300–301), with nothing for D 32. **Inference:** the Addenda do not return to D32 or to Jeremias, subject to OCR completeness.
- Table of contents (evidence): "D. Sites et monuments 259"; "F. Transcription et traduction annotées 284"; "Addenda 299".
- The French translation of item 32 (p. 284ff. section) was not searched for the tomb-orientation wording.

## What the sources do NOT say

1. Jeremias gives **no direction** that the Beit Guvrin shaft tombs or the Micah tomb face. He gives no plan, only one tomb's measurements.
2. Jeremias dates the necropolis only as "seleukidisch". He gives no finds and no dates for individual tombs. The Micah monument is dated by inference ("bis in die Tage Jesu").
3. None of the Jeremias passages returned for pp. 82–86 mentions Beth-Horon or Naṭuf. The book was **not** searched for "Horon", "Bethoron" or "Natuf", so other pages are unchecked.
4. Milik (p. 268) prefers **Upper Beth-Horon** for D32 and cites Jeremias's **RB 1960** note, not *Heiligengräber*. He does not say what Jeremias's alternative location is.
5. The DJD III Addenda do not mention D32 or Jeremias (search-based).
6. Benjamin survey site 143: nothing read.
