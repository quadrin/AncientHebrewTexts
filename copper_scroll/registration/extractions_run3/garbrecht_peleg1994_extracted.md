## General statements (all fortresses)

- **Evidence, p. 162.** The fortresses covered: "Alexandrion, Dok, Cypros, Hyrcania, Herodion, Masada, and Machaerus".
- **Evidence, p. 163.** Storage: water "had to be stored for use during at least 12 and up to 24 months … only large underground cisterns were suitable".
- **Evidence, p. 163.** Demand: "A minimum of 10-20 liters a day for every inhabitant can be assumed under siege conditions"; at Herod's fortress-palaces "200-300 liters and more".
- **Evidence, p. 164.** The system has two levels. Storage cisterns "had to be situated outside the fortress on the slopes of the hill"; water went up "in jars or water skins by porters or pack animals". Cisterns inside the walls "were the mainstay of water supply during a siege".
- **Evidence, p. 164.** The four-step scheme: catchment; "Conveyance of the water by a channel to the foot of the hill"; "Intermediate storage on the slope"; "Transportation by pack animals into cisterns in the fort". The authors refer to "Garbrecht/Peleg 1989 and Amit et al. 1989" for details.
- **Evidence, p. 170 (Summary).** Chronology as the authors give it: "The oldest fortified vantage point was probably Dok (about 167 BCE), followed by Hyrcania (134-104 BCE). Alexander Janneus (134-107 BCE) probably founded Masada, Alexandrion, and Machaerus. Cypros, rebuilt by Herod, was most likely built by the Hasmoneans. Herodion was founded by Herod."
  - **Inference.** The regnal dates printed for Alexander Jannaeus (134–107 BCE) are wrong as printed. They repeat John Hyrcanus I's dates. Do not cite this sentence for dates.
- **Evidence, p. 170.** Storage cisterns were filled by "the diversion of run-off water from wadis (Dok, Hyrcania, Masada, probably Machaerus), the collection of runoff water from an adjacent hilltop (Alexandrion, Cypros), or the capture and conveyance of spring water (Cypros, Herodion)".
- **Evidence, p. 170.** The circumferential collecting channels on nearby hilltops are called "a seemingly local invention".
- **Evidence.** The article nowhere gives excavation evidence for its phase dates. The dating is by historical attribution ("possibly", "probably"). See each fortress below.

## Alexandrion (Sartaba)

- **Evidence, p. 168.** "Run-off from the nearby mountain Ras Kuneitra was collected by a circumferential collecting channel."
- **Evidence, p. 168.** Inverted siphon: "a masonry channel (W 0.60 m, H 0.80 m) covered by 0.30 m high stone slabs and plastered on all its interior surfaces." "Only the beginning and the end of the inverted siphon have been preserved."
- **Evidence, pp. 168–169.** "The discharge end of the siphon lies 2.51 m lower than its intake, giving a fall of 1.3% along its 190 m length". The pressure was "at least 5 atmospheres and perhaps reached 10".
- **Evidence, p. 169.** The authors conclude "the siphon theoretically could not have functioned. After failure of the siphon, most likely a higher crossing was built."
- **Evidence, p. 169.** "At Alexandrion there is a 2.20 m wide masonry wall alongside the siphon". Its courses run parallel to the slope, "possibly indicating that the wall was intended to carry a second inverted siphon" (p. 170).
- **Evidence, p. 169, figure caption.** "Inverted siphon at Alexandrion according to Amit (1989), showing a composite cross-section of the wall and the siphon with different probable construction stages." The figure has a 1 m scale bar.
- **Evidence, table p. 169.** 14 cisterns, about 4,700 m³; canal about 1.2 km; lift about 110 m.
- Dating: the only date given is the Summary attribution to Alexander Jannaeus (p. 170). **Evidence** that the date is an attribution, not a find.

## Dok (Dagon), Jebel Quruntul

- **Evidence, p. 164.** "probably founded during the Maccabaean uprising by Ptolemy, the son of Abubus". Simon "was murdered there in 134 BCE". "Dok is apparently the only fort that was not later rebuilt by Herod." The peak "was cleared at the beginning of this century for the building of a church, which was never completed."
- **Evidence, p. 164.** "water was diverted from a wadi on the west to nine cisterns. One other cistern was situated somewhat higher." The channel "began at a small diverting weir above a small cliff in the wadi. Only traces of masonry on the cliff show the position of the weir."
- **Evidence, p. 165.** "The first cistern is 500 meters away. The channel, partly built of field stones and partly hewn in the rock, is 0.50 to 0.70 m broad, with a fall of about 1%." At a flow depth of 0.30 m "it could deliver up to 1.2 cubic meters per second."
- **Evidence, p. 165.** Cisterns: "approximately rectangular in plan (L[ength] 7-11 m, W[idth] 3-5 m, H[eight] 5-7 m). Their capacities range from 190-270 cubic meters (m³); the total capacity of the nine cisterns is 2100 (m³)." The high openings were "later blocked by masonry to enlarge the cistern capacity. All the cisterns were lined with several layers of plaster."
- **Evidence, p. 165, figure.** Plan: "Gebel Karantal and fortress Dok, diversion weir, channel and cisterns according to Amit (1989)". The plan has a north arrow and a 100 m scale bar. The figure is not copied here.
- **Evidence, p. 164, figure caption.** "Channel leading to the cisterns on the eastern slope of Gebel Karantal."
  - **Inference.** The caption places the cisterns on the east slope. The text says the water came from a wadi to the west. The plan (p. 165) shows the channel running around the hill. No contradiction, but the text does not describe the route.
- Dating of the water system: no separate date. **Inference:** the authors treat it as Hasmonean (with the fort), since Herod did not rebuild Dok.

## Cypros (Kypros), Tell el-Aqaba

- **Evidence, p. 165.** "3 km south of Dok … 250 m above the Jericho plain … a 1800 m² acropolis and a lower terrace of about 4500 m² to the south. The ruins are from the Hasmonean and Herodian periods."
- **Evidence, p. 166.** "There is no literary evidence of a Hasmonean fort here". "it is assumed that one of the forts Trax and Taurus (mentioned by Strabo …) occupied this site." (The spelling "Trax" is from the OCR text and was not checked against the image.)
- **Evidence, p. 166.** Hasmonean phase: "The older Hasmonean fortress was supplied by a collecting channel." A channel "at the height of the col (saddle) between the two peaks, 55 m below their summits, encircles the lower hill, an area of about 43,000 m²". The catchment was "about 100,000 m²". About "3,500 m³ can be 'harvested' yearly".
- **Evidence, p. 166.** "The total capacity of the four cisterns on the eastern slope is more than 2000 m³." "The cisterns are built at approximately 40 m below sea level, 5 m below the saddle, and approximately 55 m below the acropolis."
- **Evidence, pp. 166–167.** Herodian phase: "the outflow of two springs in the upper part of the nearby Wadi Kelt, 7.5 and 12 km distant as the crow flies, was captured". Ein Fara was joined to Ein el-Fawar "by a 6 km long channel". "The channel from Ein el Fawar to Cypros was 14 km long and incorporated ten bridges and five tunnels".
- **Evidence, p. 167.** Bridge: "the longest bridge (L 135 m) … The maximum height of the bridge was about 14 m, and the span of the widest arch was 12.60 m."
- **Evidence, p. 167.** Yield: about "3 million m³" a year at the end of the conduit, "0.10 m³/s". Between cisterns 2 and 3 a channel "continued on downhill in a serpentine course to supply the winter palace of Herod on the south bank of Wadi Kelt and to irrigate the nearby plantations."
- **Evidence, p. 166, figure.** "Water supply system of Cypros according to Meshel-Amit (1979). The collecting system served the Hasmonean fort. Herod built a most elaborate aqueduct in order to bring spring water to Cypros." The plan has a north arrow and a 50 m scale bar.
- Phase attribution is by the authors' reading: collecting channel = Hasmonean, spring aqueduct = Herodian. **Evidence** that this is stated (p. 166). **Evidence** that no finds are cited for either phase.

## Hyrcania

- **Evidence, p. 168.** "The highest saddle crossing is found at Hyrcania. Here, the natural saddle was deepened for defense purposes. Only a narrow path was left on which the foundation of the water-supply channel was laid, a substructure 112 m long and 16.80 m high."
- **Evidence, p. 168.** "Two phases are discernible in the masonry. During the first phase, possibly of the Hasmonean period, only the four lower cisterns on the south slope could be supplied. During the second, possibly Herodian, phase twelve more cisterns higher up the slope could be filled."
- **Evidence, p. 168.** "Three unroofed pools measuring about 18 x 15 x 5 m deep were quarried in the rock on both sides of the path and plastered on the inside (total capacity about 4000 cubic meters). These reservoirs were filled after the cisterns on the slope of the fortress were already full."
- **Evidence, p. 168.** "Hyrcania received its water from the quite frequent winter floods in the Kidron (Wadi Nur)". The reservoirs were probably used first and the cisterns kept "for later use or during a siege".
- **Evidence, p. 168.** "Excavation of the reservoirs served several purposes: the creation of a moat, the provision of quarried stone for the construction of the fort, and water storage."
- **Evidence, table p. 169.** Two wadi diversions, canals of about 2 km and about 9 km; bridge 112 m; 16 cisterns, about 16,000 m³; lift about 22 m. Storage inside the fortress is left blank.
- **Evidence, p. 168, photo caption.** "Hyrcania, channel bridge across saddle between the fortress and the Judean mountains. In foreground one of the rock cut pools."
- **Evidence, p. 170.** Founding date "Hyrcania (134-104 BCE)", i.e. the reign of John Hyrcanus I, as an attribution.
- **Inference.** The two-phase reading (Hasmonean 4 cisterns / Herodian +12) rests on masonry only and is marked "possibly" twice. The authors do not give pottery, coins or plaster analysis. Relevant to entries 16, 29, 35: this source supports **16 slope cisterns and 3 open pools** but not a firm date for either phase.
- **Inference.** The table gives two canals (about 2 km and 9 km) from "two Wadis", but the text names only the Kidron (Wadi Nur). The second wadi is not named in this article.

## Herodion

- **Evidence, p. 166.** "supplied by spring water from the so-called 'Solomonic Pools'". Table p. 169: canal about 7 km; 3 cisterns, about 2,500 m³; about 200 m³ inside the fortress; lift about 25 m.

## Masada

- **Evidence, table p. 169 only.** Diversion from two wadis; canals about 350 m and 200 m; 12 cisterns, about 38,500 m³; lift about 40 and about 80 m; about 8,000 m³ inside the fortress. Masada is not described in the text beyond the Summary (founding attributed to Jannaeus, p. 170) and Herod leaving his family there (p. 162, citing Josephus, *War* 1.13.8).

## Machaerus

- **Evidence, p. 167.** "at Machaerus a wall carried the supply channel to the fort." Table p. 169: two wadis; "Wall"; 6 cisterns. No figures.

## Comparison with Qumran

- **Evidence (absence).** The article does not mention Qumran anywhere, in the text, captions, table or bibliography. It makes no comparison with Qumran's system.
- **BK.** Qumran's aqueduct also diverts winter flood water from a wadi (Wadi Qumran) through a channel with a rock-cut section. That is the Dok/Hyrcania type ("diversion of run-off water from wadis"). This comparison is mine, not the authors'.

## What the source does NOT say

1. Hyrcania: it gives no excavated dating evidence (pottery, coins, plaster) for either water phase. Both are "possibly". It gives no date for the pools.
2. Hyrcania: it does not name the second wadi, give channel widths or gradients, give individual cistern dimensions, or say where the four lower cisterns and twelve upper cisterns are relative to each other beyond "south slope" and "higher up the slope".
3. It does not mention Byzantine monastic reuse of Hyrcania (Kastellion) or of any other water system.
4. Dok: it gives no finds or date for the channel and cisterns apart from the fort's history. It does not say which slope the 10th cistern is on.
5. Alexandrion: it gives no cistern dimensions and no date for either siphon phase.
6. Masada and Machaerus: data are in the table only; there is no description.
7. It gives no storage figure for Hyrcania inside the fortress (blank in the table), and "?" for Alexandrion, Dok and Machaerus.
8. It says nothing about Qumran and makes no comparison with it.
9. It gives no coordinates or grid references for any installation.
