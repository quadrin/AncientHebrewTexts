# Kh. Salhab and Kh. Ibziq: any conduit, drain or large water channel? (entry 59, Q46)

## Short answer

**No published description of a conduit, drain or large water channel at Kh. Salhab, Upper Kh. Ibziq or Lower Kh. Ibziq was found in the sources searched.** The water installations recorded at all three are **cisterns** (and caves). See §5 for the limits of this negative result.

## 1. Zertal 2007, site 23, Kh. Salhab (pp. 151–153), read as page images

- **Evidence, p. 151.** Header: "Site 23: 18-19/55/1 / Khirbet Salhab (SWP map: Kh. es-Selhab)". "Israel grid: 1853 1957"; "UTM grid: 7233 5824"; "Elevation: 430 m a.s.l., 10 m a.s.a."; "Site type: tell"; "Area: 15 dunams (3.75 acres)"; "Topography: hilltop and valley"; "Rock type: alluvium cover".
- **Evidence, p. 151.** "Cisterns: 30". "Nearest water source: spring ('Ain el-Far'ah, no. 85), 10 km distant". "Nearest road: Salhab – Beth Shean junction (B7), passes by site". Visits: "Nov. 30, 1980; Feb. 2, 1987; 46 shards".
- **Evidence, p. 151.** Description: "A small tell in the southeastern part of Zebabdeh Valley, near the road from Tubas to Kh. Ibziq … Three modern houses stand on the western part of the tell, with a considerable concentration of cisterns and quarried places nearby. The northern slopes are relatively steep, with large scatters of shards dating up to the Roman period. There are also foundations and walls from the Roman-Byzantine period all over the tell, with few cisterns and quarried places."
- **Evidence, p. 152.** "Pottery: IrA IA – 3%; IrA II – 2%; Per – 5%; ER – 15%; LR – 15%; Byz – 20%; EM – 10%; MA – 15%; Ott – 15%." "Special finds: Pottery handles with indentations and 'face' decorations, all dated to Iron Age I." "Identification: With Biblical Bezeq (I Samuel 11:8) … Another possible identification is with Palga Deshlaf from the Rehov inscription". **"Previous surveys: Gophna and Porath 1972, no. 65."** "Bibliography: SWP II, 240; Guérin 1969 (IV), 354-355; Avi-Yonah 1966, 152; 1976, 92; Hutteroth and Abd el-Fatah 1977, 129; Zussman 1981, 167."
- **Evidence, pp. 151–153, figures.** Fig. 75: the tell "looking north … In front of the tell is the Jordanian anti tank barrier of 1967". Fig. 76: "The south Zebabdeh Valley, Ras es-Salmeh and Kh. Salhab … Bezeq (Ibziq) Pass is in the right side". Fig. 77: sherds (IrA I handles; MA bowls; IrA III bowl; Hellenistic base; IrA I "stepped" jar).
- **Evidence (absence).** The site 23 entry mentions **no channel, conduit, drain, pool or aqueduct**. Water features are "cisterns" (30) only.

## 3. SWP Memoirs vol. II (1882), full OCR text

- **Evidence, p. 240.** "Khurbet es Selhab (O m). — Traces of ruins." It quotes Guérin, *Samarie* i. 355: "A little town, now destroyed, on a hill whose rocky sides are pierced by numerous cisterns. The place which it occupied is now covered with confused materials, the remains of demolished dwellings, and disposed for the most part in circular heaps round silos or subterranean magazines cut in the rock."
- **Evidence, p. 237.** "Khurbet Ibzik (O m). — Evidently an ancient site, with traces of ruins, cisterns and caves, as at Khurbet Yerzeh. There is a kubbeh in the ruins sacred to Sheikh Hazkin, apparently Ezekiel. (See Bezek, Section A.)"
- **Evidence, p. 231 (Section A).** "Bezek … In the 'Onomasticon' two places called Bezec are said to have existed close together 17 miles from Neapolis, on the road going down to Scythopolis. This site is evidently Khurbet Ibzik (O m), 14 English miles from Nablus, on the road in question."
- **Evidence, pp. 221, 229.** The valley is named "Wady es Selhab", described as "flat and open, forming a sort of narrow plain of good arable soil". Zebabdeh sits "at the south edge of the arable plain called Wady es Selhab, supplied by a well on the east".
- **Evidence, p. 222.** "Ras Ibzik, rising just south of Raba, is the highest point …" (orography only).
- **Evidence (absence).** No channel, conduit, aqueduct or drain is recorded at Kh. es Selhab or Kh. Ibzik. SWP records only cisterns, caves, silos and magazines.
- Page numbers come from the running heads in the OCR text. Line positions were checked against the page-number lines: Selhab entry after the "240" head; Ibzik entry before the "238" head, so on p. 237; Bezek before the "232" head, so on p. 231.

## 5. Limits of the negative result

1. Zertal's Ibziq entries (pp. 191–196) were seen only as snippets. A channel described there without the words "conduit" or "channel" in the top hits could have been missed. **Your bundle `entry59-zertal-sources.bundle` should be checked for pp. 191–196.**
2. Gophna & Porath 1972 (survey site no. 65, cited by Zertal as a previous survey of Salhab) was not consulted.
3. Hadashot's own search was down, so a small IAA/SOA report after 2007 cannot be fully excluded. None appeared in Google site searches.
4. Guérin 1869/1875 was read only through the SWP translation.

## What the sources do NOT say

- No conduit, aqueduct, drain or channel of any size at Kh. Salhab (Zertal pp. 151–152; SWP p. 240; Guérin via SWP).
- No conduit, aqueduct, drain or channel at Kh. Ibzik in SWP (p. 237). Zertal's Ibziq entries were unread beyond snippets.
- No dimensions for any water installation at either site other than the cistern count (30) at Salhab.
- No excavation at either site was found.
