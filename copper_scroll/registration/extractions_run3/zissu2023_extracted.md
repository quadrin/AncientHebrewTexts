## Page concordance

## Nature of the chapter (read this first)

- **Evidence.** This is a **synthesis**, not a site catalogue. "Below is a survey of human activity, flight and settlement in the Judean Desert during the late Second Temple period and the Bar Kokhba Revolt" (p. 194).
- **Evidence (absence).** The chapter gives **no coordinates, grid references or cave numbers with locations**, **no measurements** of caves, shafts or cisterns, and **no inventory of dated finds per site**. For the full data it refers to Eshel & Zissu 2019 (*The Bar Kokhba Revolt – The Archaeological Evidence*), Porat & Eshel 2008 (First Revolt caves), Eshel & Amit 1998 and Eshel & Porat 2009 (*Refuge Caves* I–II).
- **Inference.** For the atlas, this chapter is useful for **named sites, their period attribution and the references** to follow. It cannot supply cave-level features or coordinates.

## Site types defined

- **Evidence, pp. 202–203.** Refuge caves "are natural cavities of various sizes created by dissolution of the limestone". They differ "from the hiding complexes that were hewn out of the rock below Jewish settlements … linking them with narrow twisting burrows and fitting them with camouflage and blockages".
- **Evidence, p. 203.** There is a hybrid group in the northern Judean Desert: "natural cavities of moderate size that were adjusted by carving the rock to serve as hiding places … Located high along the cliff wall … cisterns and other facilities were cut in some, to permit a protracted safe stay." Location: "the cliffs in the area between the west of Jericho and the eastern slopes of the Benjamin Hills, centering around Wadi Qelt and Naḥal Mikhmash (Wadi Suweinit; see Patrich 1985a; 1985b; Zissu 2001:276)". Date: "most of them were first hewn out in the Second Temple period and later served as hiding places during the First Jewish Revolt and again, at the end of the Bar Kokhba Revolt."

## Numbers

- **Evidence, p. 204.** First Revolt: Porat & Eshel 2008 report finds from that period "in as many as 40 caves". The dating evidence is "Bronze coins from the years preceding the revolt, coins minted by the rebels, ceramic assemblages and papyri (a few documents were found in Wadi Murabba'at and at Ketef Jericho …)".
- **Evidence, p. 205.** First Revolt caves "were more easily accessible and located closer to settled areas". Occupation was "short-term … by only a handful of people".
- **Evidence, pp. 206–207.** Bar Kokhba: "As of 2019, 34 caves (or groups of caves) … were known". In 18 more the evidence is not unequivocal. Extent: "between Wadi er-Rashash … in the northeast and Naḥal Shillo in the northwest, with the southernmost caves found thus far in Naḥal Qina and Yahel, south of 'Arad (Eshel and Zissu 2019:77, Fig. 110)". Three strips: "21 caves in the eastern strip, along the escarpment; 8 caves in the central strip …; and 5 caves in the western strip, in the Judean Hills."

## Fortress cisterns (context, not caves)

- **⚑ Evidence, p. 196.** "Dok (Dagon) was strategically located above Jericho"; "Hyrcania overlooked the Hyrcania Valley and the longitudinal road running along it". p. 190: "the road from Jericho to 'En Gedi via the Hyrcania Valley".
- **Evidence, pp. 197–199.** Alexandrion: "note the openings of a cistern on the slope" (Fig. 9); "one of the cisterns cut into the slope" (Fig. 11). No dimensions.
- **Evidence, p. 198.** The planners had an "impressive ability to channel and store runoff water in large cisterns (Fig. 11), to withstand a prolonged siege (Garbrecht and Peleg 1994)".
- **Evidence, p. 197.** "The most important fortresses—Alexandrion, Hyrcania and Machaerus—were also Hasmonean royal treasuries, in which food, weapons and military equipment were stored". Ant. 13:417 is quoted on Salome keeping "her most valuable possessions" there.
- **Evidence, p. 200.** Alexandrion: "scientific excavations … have not revealed clearly dated remains from the underlying Hasmonean phase … the site was recently damaged by looters".
- **Evidence, p. 202.** Masada: "apparently initiated in the days of Alexander Jannaeus, but no firmly dated remains from this period were found."
- **Evidence, p. 192.** Along roads: "remains of cisterns and occasional guard stations and stops (Porat 2009:19–22)".

3. No cave-by-cave dated find lists. Dates are by revolt, with coin and document types named only generally.
4. Nothing on caves or hiding complexes in the Buqeia, at Hyrcania, in the Kidron / Mar Saba area, or at Doq, except the Qarantal-cliffs reference (Herod vs. Antigonus) given only as a citation.
5. No Qumran-area refuge or hiding caves (scroll caves only).
6. No list of the 34 + 18 BK caves or the 40 FR caves. Those are in Eshel & Zissu 2019 and Porat & Eshel 2008.
7. No underground hiding complexes of the Judean Hills by name. Kloner & Zissu 2009 is cited for them.
