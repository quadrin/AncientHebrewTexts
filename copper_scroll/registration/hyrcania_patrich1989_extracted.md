# Hyrcania aqueducts (entries 16, 29, 35) — from the 2002 English chapter, snippets only

## Site and chronology (evidence)
- "The fortress of Hyrcania is located in the northern part of the Judean Desert, on the western edge of a valley named after it (in Arabic, el Buqeia)" (p. 336).
- Founded "during the Hasmonean period by Alexander Jannaeus (103-74 B.C.), or possibly by his father John Hyrcanus (135-103)" (p. 336).
- "destroyed in 57 by Gabinius", then "recaptured after a protracted siege in 31 by Herod, who rebuilt it at great expense" (p. 336).
- "Hyrcania is not mentioned again after the death of Herod. It is not mentioned among the desert fortresses nor was it used by the Zealots during the Great Revolt and thus seems to have been abandoned. In 492, St Sabas founded a monastery in the ruins" (p. 336).
- The aqueduct remains "represent these three phases of activity on the site — Hasmonean, Herodian, and Byzantine" (p. 336). The phrase just before, "the middle of the 14th c.", ends a sentence not seen in full (p. 336).

## Research history (evidence, p. 336)
- Conder and Kitchener "suggested that its source was Bir el Amara, east of Jebel Muntar"; Mader "briefly mentioned two channels"; "Wright thought that there was only one aqueduct, coming from the direction of Jebel Muntar".
- Feldman dug *sondages*: "Y. Feldman, 'The water system at Hyrcania fortress,' in Z. Ilan (ed.), *The Judean desert and the Dead Sea* (… Tel-Aviv 1974) 316-35 [Hebrew]" (n. 5).
- "in 1983 the present writer re-examined the aqueducts and cisterns as part of the survey of the Mar Saba map" (p. 336). Note 6: "J. Patrich, *Archaeological survey in Judea and Samaria. Map of Deir Mar Saba (109/7)* (Jerusalem 1994) 13* and sites nos. 48, 62, 67-69, 83, 86, 87. The cross-sections and plans … were prepared by B. Levenstein".

## The aqueducts

**Two aqueducts (evidence, p. 336):** "Two aqueducts brought water to the fortress during the Second Temple period (fig. 1). The N aqueduct is the shorter and almost certainly the earlier. The N aqueduct is 1950 m long, the S …". The S aqueduct's length is cut off in the snippet.

Between these two sections, the aqueduct ran parallel to but lower than and more to the south of the earlier aqueduct" (p. 338).

**Bridges (evidence, p. 352):** "three bridges span deep streambeds along the course of the S aqueduct, and three more bridges were built at three saddles along the shared course of the N and S aqueducts". Patrich calls these the "'Achilles' heel' of the system". "It is possible that the difficulties in maintaining the water-supply system were one factor in the abandonment of the fortress after …" (sentence cut off).

**Near the fortress (evidence, p. 351):** an area "with the N pools served as a kind of moat that cut off the fortress from the ridge that approached the site from the west".

## Cisterns (evidence, p. 351, "The cisterns")
- "After it crossed the bridge, the aqueduct divided into two branches (N and S) that encompassed the hill and supplied water to the rock-cut cisterns on its slopes. The N branch supplied the east cisterns."
- "On the S side, Feldman found remains of two aqueducts on two different levels … The lower aqueduct is to be associated with the Hasmonean bridge (the lower of the two), while the upper course was in use during the Herodian period, when the bridge was rebuilt at a greater height to permit the installation of the row of upper cisterns."
- "It may be assumed that a secondary channel (not yet located) descended from the upper southern branch to the lower aqueduct to supply water to the lower cisterns again during the Herodian period."
- "A total of 20 rock-cut cisterns and 2 built pools exist, and he [Feldman] estimated their total capacity at 20,000 m³. The cisterns are plastered with a thick layer of hydraulic plaster composed of …" (cut off).
- p. 352: pools "north of the E bridge beneath the fortress to function as swimming pools, with seats for spectators nearby".
- A caption fragment on p. 352 reads "Cistern [?], … plan and cross-section". The figure number cannot be read.
