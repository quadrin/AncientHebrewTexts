## 1. Eisenberg, "The Excavation of Cave V/49" (file 09, pp. 105–121)

### 1.1 The neighbouring cave: p. 120 (PDF idx 16), right-hand text column [R]

The neighbour is named explicitly as **Cave V/48**. It is not Cave 38, and no Cave 42 is mentioned.

> "…כמו מערה V/48, שנחצבה בימי הבית השני." [R]
> "…such as Cave V/48, which was hewn in the Second Temple period."

> "מערת מגורים זו (מס׳ V/48), הסמוכה למערה V/49, נחצבה לצד שביל, שחיבר בין שתי המערות." [R]
> "This dwelling cave (No. V/48), adjacent to Cave V/49, was hewn beside a path that connected the two caves."

> "סביר להניח שמערה V/49 שירתה את יושבי מערה V/48." [R]
> "It is reasonable to assume that Cave V/49 served the occupants of Cave V/48."

Context from the same paragraph [R]:

> "נושבה המערה שוב בתקופה ההלניסטית (המאה הא׳ לפסה״נ) ונעשה בה שימוש עד לימי המרד הגדול"
> "The cave was settled again in the Hellenistic period (1st c. BCE) and was used until the days of the Great Revolt."

The paragraph then says that the ceramic and organic finds do not establish the character of the stay, but the evidence for storing cereals, olives, almonds, dates and garlic hints at it. In V/49 this period used the **front of the cave**, where the Chalcolithic remains were cleared.

**Spatial relation between V/48 and V/49, as stated.** The two caves are:

- "adjacent" (הסמוכה);
- linked by a **path** (שביל) that connected them, with V/48 hewn beside it.

No distance, shared terrace, bearing or physical connection between the voids is given [R]. Eisenberg gives no evidence for his Second Temple date for V/48. Aronshtam records only two non-indicative sherds there (see §2.2).

### 1.2 Location, access and a second path reference: p. 105 (PDF idx 1) [R]

> "מערה V/49 היא מערה טבעית הפעורה במצוק הצפוני של ג׳בל קרנטל (נ״צ 19035/14370"
> "Cave V/49 is a natural cave opening in the northern cliff of Jebel Quruntul (grid ref. 19035/14370"

The cited figure is Fig. 1. The same sentence places the cave east of ʿEin Duyuk (footnote 1).

> "סמוך למערה, ממערב, מתחתר ערוץ, המסתיים במפל יבש, שגובהו כ־30 מ׳."
> "Close to the cave, on the west, a gully cuts in, ending in a dry waterfall c. 30 m high."

> "אך אפשרי היה להגיע אליה ממזרח (המשכו של השביל, שהוכשר על ידי דיירי מערה V/50)."
> "…but it was possible to reach it from the east (the continuation of the path prepared by the occupants of Cave V/50)."

- **Read.** Zoomed at scale 5, the number is clearly **50/V** on p. 105 and **48/V** (twice) on p. 120 [R].
- **Two readings [I].** Both statements can describe one path running along the cliff from the south-east (the V/50 side) to V/49, with V/48 cut beside it. Alternatively, the p. 105 "V/50" is a slip for V/48. Neither report resolves which.
- **Where Aronshtam puts V/50.** V/50 is on the north bank of Wadi Abu Saraj at 19060/14350, about 320 m south-east of V/49's printed point [I, computed]. Its entrance faces east (Aronshtam p. 97).

### 1.4 Entrance orientation and dimensions

- **Orientation: north [R and I].** Both mouths (W1 for the eastern hall, W2 for the western hall) lie on the page-left edge of Plan 1, and the north arrow points left. The text also says the floor of the eastern hall "slopes gently from the southern niche to the opening" (p. 105) [R].
- **Openings described [R, p. 105]:**
  > "פתח אחד, נוח, שימש ככניסה, למרות שלמערה היו פתחים נוספים"
  > "One convenient opening served as the entrance, although the cave had additional openings"

  > "לאולם זה שני פתחים: פתח הקרוע בקיר המצוק וחלון שבדופנו של הכוך המערבי."
  > "This [western] hall has two openings: an opening broken through the cliff wall and a window in the side of the western niche."
- **Wall 1 [R, p. 107].** 0.3 m wide; only the foundation stones survive. It is curved and follows the line of the cliff, closing L3 on the north.
- **Wall 2 [R, p. 109].** Only the inner face's foundation course survives; the upper courses were laid with mortar. Eisenberg suggests it did not close the opening to full height but served as a **window parapet**.
- **Entrance dimensions.** No numerical widths or heights are printed [R]. Scaled from Plan 1 [I, ±0.5 m]:
  - eastern-hall mouth: about 5.5–6 m, of which W1 closes about 2 m and about 3.6 m stays open;
  - western-hall mouth: about 3.5 m, with W2 across about 1.8 m of it.
- **Cave dimensions [R].**
  - Average height 4 m (p. 105).
  - The eastern hall (L3, L4, L6, L7) is 15 m long and about 4 m high; niche L1 is 3 m long; the western hall L8 is 5 × 8 m (p. 105).
  - Aronshtam gives about 11 × 14 m and about 4 m high (p. 94).

### 1.5 Periods of use [R]

- **Chalcolithic.** Main assemblage (pp. 109–116). A palm-fibre mat from L6 is radiocarbon-dated to 4206–4213 BCE (5%) and 3976–4133 BCE (95%), as printed (p. 116). There is also a copper axe (p. 116).
- **EB IB.** Three hand-made sherds (p. 116; Fig. 11).
- **Second Temple.**
  - p. 108: a hearth with Second Temple sherds in L5, outside the entrance and north of W1.
  - p. 109: most sherds on the bedrock of L8 (western hall), up to the soot layer, are from jars of the 1st century BCE. That part of the cave "was well cleared of earlier occupation remains when it was prepared for dwelling in the Second Temple period".
  - p. 117: the Second Temple material came from the front of the cave, near the surface, mixed with later material.
  - pp. 117–119: pottery (Fig. 12) including lamps of the 1st century CE; a chalk stone vessel; uninscribed papyrus pieces (Fig. 5; p. 119).
  - p. 120: Eisenberg's summary is "1st c. BCE to the Great Revolt".
- **Medieval.** Two sherds and textiles, interpreted as temporary shepherds' stays (p. 119).
- **Undated.** A basalt quern fragment (p. 119, Fig. 13).
- **Excavation.** Licence L-604, November–December 1993 (p. 120, note 1).

## 2. Aronshtam, "Region V" (file 08, pp. 91–104)

### 2.1 Region-level statements [R]

- **Survey area (p. 91).** About 650 m of the Jebel Quruntul cliff, trending south-east to north-west from the mouth of Wadi Abu Saraj to the mouth of Wadi Ḥalq er-Rummana. The location map cited is "מפה IV–VI, עמ׳ 43" (Map IV–VI, p. 43), with Fig. 1.
- **Valley (p. 91).** "לרגלי המצוק משתרעת בקעת נעימה" ("at the foot of the cliff extends the Naʿima valley"). Wadi el-Makkuk, Wadi Abu Saraj and Wadi Ḥalq er-Rummana drain into it, and it is laced with man-made water channels. This is relevant to Achor = Wadi Nuweiʿimeh.
- **Excavations (p. 91).** Four caves were excavated after the survey: V/38 and V/50 by Aronshtam, V/59 by Y. Peleg, and V/49 by Eisenberg.
  > "תוצאות החפירה במערה V/38 יפורסמו בנפרד."
  > "The results of the excavation in Cave V/38 will be published separately."
- **Permits.** Survey permit **L-588.5** (p. 103, note 1; the Index prints L-588/5). V/50 excavation L-596 (note 2); V/59 L-599 (note 3).
- **No regional plan.** Aronshtam's article has no location plan. Its plans are Plan 1 (V/64, p. 96), Plan 2 (V/50, p. 97) and Plan 3 (V/59, p. 100). The only positional graphics for V/38, V/48 and V/49 are Figs. 1–2 and Map IV–VI.

> "A hewn cave whose façade collapsed, leaving it cut in half on the cliff."

> "החדרים ערוכים בטור, שכיוונו מקביל לקו המצוק"
> "The rooms are arranged in a row parallel to the cliff line"

The rest of the V/48 row [R]:

- The two side rooms survive almost intact. The **eastern part of the central room collapsed**, so its size is unclear (long wall 3 m; short walls preserved to 1.8 m).
- In the room's north-west corner is a round cistern, 1 m in diameter and 1 m deep. It is hewn below and built above (lining 0.15 m thick), with white plaster.
- The **NE room** is 1.2 × 2.0 m and 1.5 m high. It opens into the central room through a 0.65 m doorway in the middle of the short wall.
- The **SW room** is 1.5 × 2.5 m and 1.35 m high. Its 0.9 m doorway is in the SW corner of the central room.

**V/49 description:**

> "בחזית המערה שרד קיר בנוי, שחסם את פתחה."
> "In front of the cave survived a built wall that blocked its opening."

The row continues: a cave space of about 11 × 14 m, about 4 m high, divided in the centre by a wide pillar into two halls; robbed in the past [R].

- **V/48 entrance and orientation.** Not stated [R]. The collapsed eastern part of the central room and the NE–SW row suggest that the open face lies roughly east or south-east [I, weak].
- **No plan or section** is published for V/38 or V/48 in this part [R].
- **V/38's period** is not stated. Indirect evidence: on p. 101 [R], textiles from V/59 "were dated to the Middle Ages on the basis of spin direction and parallels to Cave V/38". So V/38 yielded **medieval** textiles [I from wording].

## 5. Brief inventory: Regions VII, XI and XIV (only items bearing on entries 31, 20–23 and 35)

### Region VII (Abeles; file 10): eastern escarpment of Jebel Quruntul (entry 31, Dok)

- **p. 123 (idx 1), Map VII.** Grid E 190500–191500, N 141500–143000; 400 m bar. Cave dots VII/1–16 at about N 142.1–142.5. **The summit fortress, the monastery and any waterworks are not marked** [R].
- **p. 125 (idx 2).** The region covers the north bank of Wadi el-Mafjar near its outlet, "the eastern cliff of Jebel Quruntul, facing the city of Jericho", and a small stream between Jebel Quruntul and Jebel Abu Saraj. 31 caves were surveyed and 16 are described [R]. **The Dok/Doq fortress, its aqueduct and its cisterns are not mentioned anywhere in the article** [T, keyword search checked].
- **p. 135 (idx 12), VII/3 "Cave of the Sack" (19075/14215).** Roman sherds; a coin of Valerius Gratus under Tiberius (17–24 CE) and a Nabataean coin. Also "two pieces of metal (Fig. 8:2), probably scales of a soldier's armour" [T]. This shows Early Roman presence, possibly military, on the escarpment; it has no bearing on the summit plan.
- **p. 136 (idx 13), near VII/11 (19090/14240).** "A wall supporting the slope that was probably connected to the Deir Quruntul monastery at the top of the cliff." VII/12 lies 200 m above the monastery; VII/15–16 were used by its monks until recently [T]. These are monastery-related structures, **not** the Hasmonean fortress.
- **p. 137 (idx 14).** Survey permit L-588.3; VII/1 excavation L-611; VII/3 L-624. Note 1 on renumbering is quoted in §4.

### Region XI (Baruch, Mazor and Sandhaus; file 16): escarpment above Ḥorbat Qumran (entries 20–23, aqueduct)

- **p. 187 (idx 1), Map XI.** 1 km grid, E 193–195, N 128–129+; 500 m bar. Dots XI/1–22. **Kh. Qumran, the aqueduct, the Naḥal Qumran dam and Naḥal Qumran itself are not drawn or labelled.** The lower edge is at about N 128 [R].
- **p. 189 (idx 2).** 1.4 km of the fault escarpment, bounded on the north by a stream from Jaufat Zabin (near grid line 129) and on the south by Naḥal Qumran [T]. The authors could not re-identify de Vaux's and Patrich's caves because the earlier grids and photographs were imprecise [T]. **No mention of the aqueduct** [T, keyword search].
- **pp. 195–196 (idx 8–9).**
  - XI/19 is identified from old photographs as Baillet–Milik–de Vaux Cave 19, "the 2Q scroll cave". Its grid is printed as **19343/12391**, which is evidently a misprint: Map XI plots cave 19 at about N 128.9 [R; I for the misprint].
  - XI/20 is identified with Baillet–Milik–de Vaux Cave 20; it has Early Roman sherds and a 14th-century Mamluk jug [T].
- **p. 198 (idx 11), XI/22.** A rectangular enclosure (30 × 41 m) at the foot of the cliff at 19350/12850, with a stone-lined pit in the centre:
  > "במרכז המתחם נמצא בור (לאיסוף מים?)"
  > "In the centre of the enclosure is a pit (for collecting water?)"

  No finds were recovered, so it is undated [R/T]. This is the only water-related feature in the region; it has no stated link to the aqueduct.
- **p. 198, summary.** Most pottery dates from the 1st century BCE to the 1st century CE [T].

### Region XIV (Dahari; file 22): Naḥal Kidron to Naḥal Deragot (entry 35, "mouth of the Kidron gorge")

- **p. 229 (idx 1), Map XIV.** 1 km grid, E 187–191, N 110–120.5; 1 km bar. "Naḥal Qidron" is labelled at the top edge, and dots XIV/1–4 cluster where the Kidron leaves the escarpment (about E 190.6–191.0, N 120.2–120.6) [R].
- **p. 231 (idx 2).** The survey ran "from the south bank of Naḥal Kidron in the north to the north bank of Naḥal Deragot in the south" in November 1993 [R]. It also surveyed three caves in the marl near Kh. Qumran, one at 19340/12765 next to de Vaux's Cave 4; all were empty [R].
- **p. 231, XIV/1 (19060/12070).** A cave almost at the top of the southern cliff of Naḥal Kidron, about 170 m below sea level; empty [R].
- **p. 231, XIV/2 "Kidron (South) site" (centre 1909/1205; site map Fig. 2, p. 232).**
  - 17 niches and structures on four levels, built against the fault escarpment at mid-height [R].
  - Access is by a winding path from south of the Kidron across the Lisan marl, with retaining walls [R].
  - "The site is built mostly in one geological layer (Avnon member), exposed at the bend [lit. 'knee'] of Naḥal Kidron." Most of it faces east and a small part faces north [R].
  - Kh. Mazin (Qasr el-Yahud) lies about 1300 m away and is visible from the site [R].
- **p. 233 (idx 4), niche XIV/2/11.**
  > "זהו הכוך היחיד הפונה לצפון, אל הקניון של נחל קדרון"
  > "This is the only niche facing north, toward the canyon of Naḥal Kidron"

  It yielded Early Roman sherds (Fig. 4) [R].
- **p. 234 (idx 5).** From courtyard XIV/2/7 there are views to the Dead Sea, Kh. Mazin and Naḥal Kidron [T].
- **p. 239 (idx 10), summary.**
  > "האתר נבנה במאה הא׳ לסה״נ, הגיע לשיאו בתקופת המרד הגדול (לפחות עד שנה ג׳), ונעזב מיד לאחריו."
  > "The site was built in the 1st c. CE, peaked in the Great Revolt (at least until year 3), and was abandoned immediately afterwards."

  The author favours a hiding place for the people of Meṣad Mazin over a sectarian settlement. Coins run from Agrippa I (41/42 CE) through Revolt years 2–3, plus a Byzantine hoard [T].
- **p. 241 (idx 12).** XIV/3 (1911/1204): 15 natural caves in small channels in the Lisan marl parallel to and south of the Kidron, with no remains. XIV/4 (1908/1203): 20 small caves; no finds [T].

## 6. Computations [I]

- **Grid resolution.** Every Region V grid reference steps in units of 50 m (for example 19035 = E 190350 m). I assume each is rounded to within ±25 m.
- **V/49 → V/48, printed points.** Δ = +50 m E, −50 m N, so **about 71 m to the south-east**. Allowing ±25 m rounding per point, the separation could be **0 to about 141 m**. This is still not an entrance-to-entrance distance.
- **V/49 → V/28–29, printed points.** 50 m north. This contradicts Fig. 2, where V/28–29 lie east of and below V/49, which shows the grid is too coarse to fix the order.
- **V/49 → V/50, printed points.** About 320 m, bearing about 129°.
- **Gazetteer "Cave 38".** Its centre (190325, 143625) is the centre of the V/30–47 group box. The 79 m separation reported in the project is not meaningful for V/38.
- **Midpoint.** No midpoint is justified. Neither V/48 nor V/38 has a published plan or measured entrance position, and V/48's relation to V/49 is known only as "adjacent, linked by a path".
