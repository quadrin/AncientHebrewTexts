# Entry 40 — Jeremias 1958, Benjamin survey site 143, Milik DJD III

## 1. Jeremias, *Heiligengräber*, pp. 82–86: §32 "Der Prophet Micha (chirbet el-basal)"

**What the pages discuss.** They cover the tomb of the prophet **Micah** and the **"cemetery of the Anakim"** near Beit Guvrin/Eleutheropolis. They are not about a tomb *in* Beit Guvrin itself. (evidence)

- p. 82 (leaf 84): section heading "32. Der Prophet Micha (chirbet el-basal)". Micah came from Moreseth-Gath. The *Vitae prophetarum* place his grave near "the polyandrion of the Anakim". Jeremias calls this last detail "aus konkreter Ortskenntnis erwachsen" (drawn from concrete knowledge of the place) (p. 82). (evidence)
- pp. 83–84 (leaves 85–86): the Anakim/giants tradition is tied to the Eleutheropolis area. Jeremias gives the etymology of beth gubhrin as "Riesenhaus" (house of giants), and cites "die außerordentlich große Zahl mächtiger Höhlen, die sich rings um bet dschibrin finden" (p. 84). Historical dates given: Maresha destroyed by the Parthians "40 v.Chr."; the era of Eleutheropolis begins "199/200" (p. 83, with n. 1). (evidence)
- p. 84: he concludes that late-Jewish tradition looked for Moreseth-Gath "in der nächsten Umgebung von bêt dschibrin" (p. 84), supported by Eusebius (Moresheth east of Eleutheropolis). (evidence)
- p. 85 (leaf 87): Sozomenos (*HE* VII 29,2) reports that Bishop Zebennos found Micah's bones **"im Jahre 385"** at Berathsatia, "etwa zehn Stadien von der Stadt Eleutheropolis". The monument was locally called the "Grabmal des Getreuen", Aramaic "nephscha [di]mehemena", which Jeremias thinks "eine besonders prächtige Grabanlage gewesen zu sein" (p. 85). He suggests the monument already existed in Jewish times, linked to Micah's treatment as a martyr-prophet and to Mt 23,29 / Lk 11,47. (evidence)
- **p. 86 (leaf 88): the necropolis.** On **4 Oct 1932**, with A. Alt, 2 km north of Beit Jibrin, just west of the Roman road Eleutheropolis–Jerusalem, they found "eine ausgedehnte Nekropole von seleu-kidischen Schachtgräbern" (an extensive necropolis of **Seleucid shaft graves**) (p. 86). (evidence)
  - Size and layout: "Etwa hundert Gräber … in unregelmäßigen Reihen … ein großes Viereck" (about 100 graves in irregular rows forming a large rectangle) (p. 86).
  - One tomb they measured: depth "4,89 m" from the surface to the floor; two troughs "0,60 (0,70) m Breite und 1,80 m Länge"; a side chamber "2,10 zu 1,84 m" (p. 86).
  - He calls it without equal in size in inland Palestine and identifies it with the "Friedhof der Enakiter" (p. 86).
  - Church: "15,70 m ostwestlicher Länge zu 10,30 m nordsüdlicher Breite", 350 m away on **chirbet el-basal**, with pottery showing settlement "in römischer und byzantinischer Zeit". He identifies it as the Micah church. Tell ed-dschdêde is identified as Moreseth-Gath, with an "früh-römischer" fort = Berathsatia (pp. 86–87). (evidence)
- **Date Jeremias gives for the necropolis:** "seleukidisch" only (i.e. Hellenistic, BK: 312–c. 63 BCE for the Seleucid era in general). There is no finer date and no finds are listed. (evidence for the word; the BK range is not from Jeremias)

**Not in these pages (as far as the snippets show):** no tomb plans or figure numbers, no orientation of the tomb openings, no pottery or coins from the shaft graves, and nothing about Beth-Horon or Naṭuf. The Habakkuk section (§31, pp. 81–82) mentions Kh. Kila, "12 km östlich bêt dschibrin" (p. 81), but that is not a Beit Guvrin tomb.

## 3. Milik, DJD III — commentary on D 32 (p. 268) and Addenda

**p. 268 (leaf 288), item "32".** Milik reads the place as החורון and takes it as Beth-Horon, the tomb as "orienté vers la Mer (Méditerranée)" (from הצופא, ix 7), and the site as "sans doute de Bethoron-le-Haut, Beit ʿÛr el-Fôqā … 617 m. d'altitude". The sentence citing Jeremias:

> "Une localisation alternative, assez attrayante: J. Jeremias, Revue Biblique, lxvii, 1960, pp. 220–2." (p. 268)

So **Milik cites Jeremias's 1960 *Revue Biblique* article, not *Heiligengräber*, at D 32.** (evidence) What that alternative location is cannot be read from p. 268. The RB article was not seen.

Other Jeremias citations in DJD III (full list of "Jeremias" hits: leaves 288, 291, 292 = pp. 268, 271, 272):
- p. 271, no. 40 (tomb of Zadok): "J. Jeremias, Heiligengräber in Jesu Umwelt, pp. 62–64", on the Tomb of the (High) Priests in the Kidron. Milik adds that it "n'est pas à identifier avec ceux-ci, comme le voudrait Jeremias" (p. 271).
- p. 271, no. 42 (Huldah): "Jeremias, l.c., pp. 51–53".
- p. 272: "J. Jeremias, Die Wiederentdeckung von Bethesda", and "Pour les traditions attachées à Beit Ummar, voir Jeremias, l.c., p. 88".
