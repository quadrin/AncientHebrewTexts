# Garbrecht & Peleg 1994: water supply of the desert fortresses

## General statements (evidence)
- Rainfall: "The mean annual precipitation on the western slopes of the Jordan Valley is 50 to 200 mm" (p. 162). Near Jericho it is "140 mm" (p. 166).
- Storage: "Water collected in the winter had to be stored for use during at least 12 and up to 24 months … only large underground cisterns were suitable" (p. 163).
- Demand: "A minimum of 10-20 liters a day for every inhabitant can be assumed under siege conditions. At Herod's fortress-palaces, daily water-use per person may have reached 200-300 liters and more" (p. 163).
- Layout: the cisterns "had to be situated outside the fortress on the slopes of the hill". Water went up to the fort "in jars or water skins by porters or pack animals" (p. 164).
- The four steps of the general scheme (p. 164): (1) catchment "at springs, wadis, or collecting channels"; (2) conveyance "to the foot of the hill or, when a bridge or pressure conduit was installed, to a point somewhere on its slope"; (3) "Intermediate storage on the slope"; (4) "Transportation by pack animals into cisterns in the fort".
- Foundation dates (p. 170): "The oldest fortified vantage point was probably Dok (about 167 BCE), followed by Hyrcania (134-104 BCE). Alexander Janneus (134-107 BCE) probably founded Masada, Alexandrion, and Machaerus. Cypros, rebuilt by Herod, was most likely built by the Hasmoneans. Herodion was founded by Herod." The Jannaeus regnal dates are printed like this, and they are wrong (BK: Jannaeus reigned 103–76 BCE).

## Fortress by fortress

### Dok (Dagon), Gebel Karantal (pp. 164–165) ⚑ Jericho
- History (evidence): "probably founded during the Maccabaean uprising by Ptolemy, the son of Abubus". Simon "was murdered there in 134 BCE". "Dok is apparently the only fort that was not later rebuilt by Herod" (p. 164). So the site is **Hasmonean only**, and the evidence given is historical (Josephus/1 Macc tradition), not stratigraphic (inference).
- Aqueduct (evidence): water "diverted from a wadi on the west to nine cisterns. One other cistern was situated somewhat higher" (p. 164).
  - The channel began at "a small diverting weir … Only traces of masonry on the cliff show the position of the weir" (p. 164).
  - "The first cistern is 500 meters away" (p. 165).
  - The channel is "partly built of field stones and partly hewn in the rock, is 0.50 to 0.70 m broad, with a fall of about 1%". Flowing at "a depth of 0.30 m, it could deliver up to 1.2 cubic meters per second" (p. 165).
- Cisterns (evidence): "roughly hewn in the hard limestone bedrock … approximately rectangular in plan (L[ength] 7–11 m, W[idth] 3–5 m, H[eight] 5–7 m)". Capacities "range from 190–270 cubic meters (m³); the total capacity of the nine cisterns is 2100 (m³)". The openings were made high "to facilitate the removal of the construction spoil, as later blocked by masonry to enlarge the cistern capacity". "All the cisterns were lined with several layers of plaster" (p. 165).
- Figure (p. 165): plan of "Gebel Karantal and fortress Dok, diversion weir, channel and cisterns according to Amit (1989)". It **has a north arrow and a 0–100 m scale**, plus contours at 10 m intervals and the Monastery of Temptation (evidence; not copied).

### Cypros / Kypros, Tel el Akaba (pp. 165–167) ⚑ Jericho
- Site (evidence): "3 km south of Dok … 250 m above the Jericho plain". "a 1800 m² acropolis and a lower terrace of about 4500 m²". "The ruins are from the Hasmonean and Herodian periods." There is "no literary evidence of a Hasmonean fort here, but it is assumed" that Thrax or Taurus occupied it (pp. 165–166).
- **Hasmonean phase** (evidence): "The older Hasmonean fortress was supplied by a collecting channel."
  - A channel "at the height of the col (saddle) between the two peaks, 55 m below their summits, encircles the lower hill, an area of about 43,000 m²". Total catchment "about 100,000 m²". Estimated yield "approximately 3,500 m³ … 'harvested' yearly".
  - Cisterns: "the four cisterns on the eastern slope is more than 2000 m³", built "approximately 40 m below sea level, 5 m below the saddle, and approximately 55 m below the acropolis" (p. 166).
  - The attribution of the collecting system to the Hasmonean fort follows Meshel & Amit 1979 (figure caption, p. 166).
- **Herodian phase** (evidence): after Herod rebuilt the fort, "the former reliable but frugal water supply was considered to be insufficient" (p. 166).
  - Spring water from Wadi Kelt: "Ein Fara … by a 6 km long channel to the lower spring (Ein el Fawar)". Then "The channel from Ein el Fawar to Cypros was 14 km long and incorporated ten bridges and five tunnels" (p. 167).
  - Longest bridge "(L 135 m)", "maximum height … about 14 m", widest arch span "12.60 m" (p. 167).
  - Flow: Ein el Fawar maximum "0.83 cubic meters per second"; median "0.133 m³/s", about "4 million m³" a year; "a yearly supply of 3 million m³ can be expected at the end of the conduit" (p. 167). The conduit "continued on downhill … to supply the winter palace of Herod on the south bank of Wadi Kelt" (p. 167).
- Figure (p. 166): "Water supply system of Cypros according to Meshel-Amit (1979)", with a north arrow and a 0–50 m scale (evidence).

### Hyrcania (p. 168) ⚑ Hyrcania
- Conveyance (evidence): per the table, "Diversion from two Wadis", ~2 km and ~9 km (p. 169). Text: Hyrcania "received its water from the quite frequent winter floods in the Kidron (Wadi Nur)" (p. 168).
- Saddle bridge (evidence): "The highest saddle crossing is found at Hyrcania. Here, the natural saddle was deepened for defense purposes. Only a narrow path was left on which the foundation of the water-supply channel was laid, a substructure 112 m long and 16.80 m high" (p. 168).
- **Two phases** (evidence): "Two phases are discernible in the masonry. During the first phase, possibly of the Hasmonean period, only the four lower cisterns on the south slope could be supplied. During the second, possibly Herodian, phase twelve more cisterns higher up the slope could be filled" (p. 168). The dating evidence is the masonry phasing only, and it is hedged "possibly".
- Pools (evidence): "Three unroofed pools measuring about 18 x 15 x 5 m deep were quarried in the rock on both sides of the path and plastered on the inside (total capacity about 4000 cubic meters)". They "were filled after the cisterns on the slope of the fortress were already full". The authors think reservoir water "was used first and the content of the cisterns saved for later use or during a siege" (p. 168).
  - Excavating the reservoirs served for "the creation of a moat, the provision of quarried stone … and water storage" (p. 168).
- Cisterns: 16 in total (4 lower + 12 upper), about 16,000 m³ (table, p. 169). No dimensions for single cisterns.
- Photo caption (p. 168): "Hyrcania, channel bridge across saddle between the fortress and the Judean mountains. In foreground one of the rock cut pools." No plan of Hyrcania.

### Alexandrion / Sartaba (pp. 168–170)
- Collection (evidence): "Run-off from the nearby mountain Ras Kuneitra was collected by a circumferential collecting channel" (p. 168).
- Inverted siphon (evidence):
  - "a masonry channel (W 0.60 m, H 0.80 m) covered by 0.30 m high stone slabs and plastered on all its interior surfaces" (p. 168).
  - "The discharge end of the siphon lies 2.51 m lower than its intake, giving a fall of 1.3% along its 190 m length". Pressure was "at least 5 atmospheres and perhaps reached 10".
  - "the siphon theoretically could not have functioned. After failure of the siphon, most likely a higher crossing was built" (p. 169).
  - "a 2.20 m wide masonry wall alongside the siphon", possibly "intended to carry a second inverted siphon" (pp. 169–170).
- Cisterns: 14, about 4,700 m³; lift about 110 m (table, p. 169).
- Phase: no Hasmonean vs Herodian split is given for the water system. Foundation "probably" by Jannaeus (p. 170).
- Figure (p. 169): "Inverted siphon at Alexandrion according to Amit (1989)", a cross-section with a 0–1 m scale.

### Herodion, Masada, Machaerus (table only, p. 169; plus p. 170)
- **Herodion:** "Pools of Solomon", ~7 km; 3 cisterns ~2,500 m³; lift ~25 m; ~200 m³ inside. Supplied "by spring water from the so-called 'Solomonic Pools'" (p. 166).
- **Masada:** diversion from two wadis, ~350 m and ~200 m; 12 cisterns ~38,500 m³; lift ~40 and ~80 m; ~8,000 m³ inside. The text has no separate section on Masada.
- **Machaerus:** diversion from two wadis; "Wall"; 6 cisterns; everything else "?". "at Machaerus a wall carried the supply channel to the fort" (pp. 167–168).

## Summary claims (evidence, p. 170)
- Every fortress had "'consumption cisterns' inside the fort" and "'storage cisterns' on the slope". Storage cisterns were filled by "diversion of run-off water from wadis (Dok, Hyrcania, Masada, probably Machaerus), the collection of runoff water from an adjacent hilltop (Alexandrion, Cypros), or the capture and conveyance of spring water (Cypros, Herodion)".
- "circumferential collecting channels" are called "a seemingly local invention"; all other techniques "were well known in the eastern Mediterranean".

## Comparison with Qumran
**None.** The word "Qumran" does not appear in the article (text search, 0 hits). Hirschfeld (*JNES* 1998) uses this table for his own Qumran comparison; that comparison is his, not the authors' (BK / inference).

## What the source does NOT say about the question
- Anything about Qumran.
- Excavation-based dating (pottery, coins, stratigraphy) for any water system. Phases rest on masonry (Hyrcania, "possibly"), historical sources (Dok, Cypros), or Meshel & Amit 1979 (Cypros).
- Hyrcania: the separate lengths and routes of the two aqueducts, which wadis, individual cistern sizes or positions beyond "four lower … on the south slope" and "twelve more … higher up", storage inside the fort, and Byzantine reuse.
- Alexandrion and Masada: individual cistern dimensions. Machaerus: nearly all figures.
- Kypros: whether the four eastern-slope cisterns (> 2,000 m³) were enlarged in the Herodian phase. The table's "~4,000 m³" storage inside the fortress is not explained in the text.
- Doq: dates for the cisterns themselves; they are assumed contemporary with the Hasmonean fort (inference).
