## Part 2. Sources read (user uploads, 29 September 2026)

### D. Stacey, "Some Archaeological Observations on the Aqueducts of Qumran", *DSD* 14.2 (2007), pp. 222–243 — read in full

Evidence:

- **Date of the main aqueduct.** Stacey dates it no earlier than Herod, after the earthquake of 31 BCE, against de Vaux's date before 31 BCE. The channel is sunk into earthquake debris (pp. 222–226, 239).
- **Two construction stages upstream** (p. 228):
  1. *Earlier:* a simple, wide run-off channel "running down from below the cliffs to the site". Stacey cites Ilan–Amit 2002 Fig. 1, "no. 16 eastward" (n. 28). It had a low wall to catch run-off; Stacey quotes Ilan–Amit 2002 p. 385 (n. 29).
  2. *Later:* the dam at the foot of the waterfall, and the tunnelled cliff aqueduct that carried water "from the dam to the beginning of the earlier aqueduct" (Fig. 1, sections D-D and E-E; n. 30).
- **Other observations.** Qumran had no rock-hewn underground cisterns, unlike the Hasmonean desert fortresses (p. 237). The tower watched the path up to Hyrcania (p. 236).
- **Secondhand from the unread 2002 chapter.** Ilan–Amit 2002 p. 385 assigns the aqueduct to Herod Archelaus (Stacey n. 3). This date differs from the 1989 Hebrew discussion on p. 286.

Comparison with the 1989 plan:

- In the 1989 Hebrew numbering, point 16 is the right-angle bend where the channel leaves the cliff for the plain ([review](ilan_amit_1989_plan_review.md)). This matches Stacey's description.
- **Assumption, not checked:** that the 2002 figure numbers equal the 1989 numbers. The 2002 figure has not been seen.

### Zertal and Mirkam, *Manasseh Hill Country Survey* vol. 3 (From Nahal ʿIron to Nahal Shechem), 7 uploaded parts — searched

### Zertal and Bar, *Manasseh Hill Country Survey* vol. 4 (From Nahal Bezeq to the Sartaba, 2019), 8 uploaded parts — searched

- **Ibziq is not in vol. 4 either.** Vol. 4 covers the Jordan Valley strip south of Nahal Bezeq. Its western boundary is the eastern boundary of vol. 2 (p. 1, "Chapter One").
- North of grid N 1950, all 36 sites lie at E ≥ 1923. The nearest to Kh. Ibziq (about 1876/1972) is at 1923/2001, about 5.5 km away. The word "Ibziq" does not occur in the text.
- The "Bezeq Canal" (site 23(1)) is a Chalcolithic site named after a modern water channel. It is **not** a candidate for the scroll's "great conduit".
- **Roads.** Road B7 runs from Shechem to Beit Sheʾan through the Bezeq Pass and the Tubas Valley. It is the same as the Neapolis–Scythopolis Roman road (Ch. 1, citing Zertal 2008 p. 28).
  - *Inference, low:* Gerizim/Shechem, Bezek and Beth Shean lie on one route. But the scroll's order (Gerizim → Beth Sham → Bezek, entries 57–59) does not follow the road, because Bezek lies between the other two.

### Zertal, *Manasseh Hill Country Survey* vol. 2 (The Eastern Valleys and the Fringes of the Desert, Brill 2008), 9 uploaded parts — Ibziq entries read, page images checked

**Site 44, Kh. Ibziq (Upper)** = SWP "Kh. Ibzik", the atlas candidate (pp. 195–198):

- Grid 1878/1971. A tell and a large ruin of 32 dunams.
- Features: 45 cisterns; walls, quarried places, doorposts and lintels; caves reused as dwellings; a built pass (*naqb*) nearby; "a paved Roman road runs east of the site" (quoted).
- Pottery (102 sherds): Iron Age II 5%, Iron Age III 2%, Persian 3%, Late Roman 20%, Byzantine 30%, Early Muslim 10%, Mamluk 30%. There is no Hellenistic and no Early Roman in the percentages. Fig. 122 does show two Early Roman vessels (nos. 8 and 10), so there is a small Early Roman presence that is not quantified.
- No conduit, drain or channel is described.
- Zertal: "The identification with Biblical Bezeq (Judges 1:4–5) is unfounded according to the results of the survey" (p. 197).

**Site 42, Kh. Ibziq (Lower)** (pp. 191–193):

- 1 km NE of the upper site. 38 dunams.
- "About 70 burial caves, mostly containing a hewn opening, vault and internal building" (quoted); 45 cisterns; remains of a Roman road; a winepress with Greek names (Jews or Samaritans).
- Pottery: Byzantine 80%, Early Muslim 20%. The settlement "apparently reached this site during the Byzantine Period" (quoted).

**The name** (Ch. 3, pp. 104–107):

- Eusebius's "two neighbouring villages called Bezeq 17 milestones from Neapolis on the way down to Scythopolis" are sites 42 and 44 "with high probability" (quoted).
- Zertal's model moves the name: Canaanite Bezeq at Kh. esh-Sheikh Safiriyan (site 2), then Kh. Salhab (site 23), then Ibziq at the end of the Iron Age. The name is attested at Ibziq only from the Byzantine period on.

**Site 23, Kh. Salhab** (pp. 151–153): Zertal's biblical Bezeq (1 Sam 11:8).

- A tell of 15 dunams on road B7.
- Pottery includes Early Roman 15% and Late Roman 15%; 30 cisterns; Roman–Byzantine walls.
- No conduit is reported.

**Site 43, el-Quleh** (pp. 193–194): a small fortress with Hellenistic–Herodian drafted masonry on the Neapolis–Scythopolis road. Pottery "Hel (?) 100%", 4 sherds.

## Part 3. Effect on the entries

### Entry 59, Kh. Ibziq: lowered to possible, low (applied)

Inference, medium confidence:

- The landmark, the "great conduit", is still not reported at either Ibziq site.
- The fit to the period is weak. Lower Ibziq starts in the Byzantine period. The 1st-century presence at Upper Ibziq rests on two illustrated sherds.
- The name evidence (Eusebius) is 4th century and fits the Byzantine villages. It does not show which site had the name in the scroll's period; Zertal's own model allows Kh. Salhab.
- The burial caves at Lower Ibziq match Puech's alternative reading "burial chamber" only as a type, and in an undated (probably Byzantine) setting.

Result: keep "possible"; lower the confidence from medium to low; record Kh. Salhab as an alternative site that could have had the name. Salhab is not mapped, because this record does not give its grid reference.

Applied in `tables/phase5_archaeology_index.csv`, `tables/phase5_assessments.csv` and the atlas (`atlas/research/build_atlas.py`, `atlas/app/atlas-data.json`). The Phase 3 tables keep their original assessment. Elitzur still counts Bezek = [bzīq] as positive (2004 p. 16, [F8.5](findings_log.md#entry-40-elitzur-and-outreach--2026-09-29)). That is a test of the name, not of the site that carried it in the 1st century.

### Entry 21, the Qumran aqueduct head: no change in confidence

Inference, medium confidence:

- If the scroll postdates the dam stage, the head of the whole system is still the dam intake (Ilan–Amit 1989 points 3 and 4). This is the current lead.
- Stacey's sequence makes point 16 a documented former head of the earlier line. So "head of a subsection" is now a concrete alternative, not an abstract one.
- Both statements depend on Stacey's phasing, which is a hypothesis. The equivalence of the 2002 and 1989 figure numbers is not checked.

Recorded as a constraint in `tables/feature_constraints.csv` and in the atlas text for entry 21.

### Entry 57, Gerizim: no change

Magen's NEAEHL entry reports Hellenistic staircases and a courtyard cistern. If the Hellenistic city ended about 111 BCE, these may be earlier than the scroll's period only. The entry has not been read in full, so the Phase 5 verdict (best-supported, medium; the feature not verified) stays.

Only leads were found. No verdict changes.
