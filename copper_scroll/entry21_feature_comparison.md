# Entry 21: ranked Qumran feature comparison

Reviewed 28 September 2026. This comparison assumes, provisionally, that Sekakah refers to Qumran or its wadi. It ranks **research candidates within that hypothesis**, not probabilities that the Copper Scroll describes Qumran. No feature is securely identified with the entry.

## Result

The visible channel start at **Ilan–Amit Figure 1, point 3** is the strongest functional candidate for the head of the whole conduit. **Reeder–Jol's upstream collection area and large rock** provide the strongest specific rock candidate. These are two related investigative leads; their precise correspondence has not been established. Do not combine them into a single confirmed point.

The long eastern tunnel is a better cross-survey landmark than an entry 21 candidate. Its downstream position weakens the whole-conduit-head interpretation. Its two openings may be recognizable across three publications, making it useful for aligning the plans.

The [interactive comparison](https://github.com/quadrin/AncientHebrewTexts/blob/main/copper_scroll/atlas/public/research/entry21-comparison.html) shows separately digitized portions of the two plans. Select a candidate to highlight its source-relative position. Coordinates in the companion [register](entry21_candidates.json) are source-image pixels, not latitude/longitude. Highlight sizes are visual selection aids, not archaeological boundaries or confidence radii.

## Ranking and tests

| Priority | Specific candidate | What supports it | What weakens it / decisive test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Ilan–Amit point 3**, visible channel start at the small ravine on the northern bank, beside basin 2 | Directly described channel beginning; strongest fit if “head” means the entire aqueduct's upstream start. | The northern bank does not by itself satisfy every grammatical reading of “north.” No particular great stone is identified there. Match the ravine and its channel levels to the later survey. |
| 2 | **Reeder–Jol large rock and adjacent upstream channel**, below the pothole and stone wall | A reported approximately 3 m-high boulder incorporated into the collection works, beside an upstream channel. More specific than a generic tunnel-mouth rock. | “Stone” is restored; the rock's required northern relationship and ancient phase are unproved. Determine whether the rock adjoins the same intake as Ilan point 3, using the pothole, wall and ravine as intermediate landmarks. |
| 3 | **Ilan points 7–10**, branch junction and short western tunnel | A possible start of a collecting branch or subsection. | Requires a narrower sense of “head.” Published interpretations disagree about branch function and flow. Compare channel-floor elevations and the two branch connections; a plan alone cannot resolve flow direction. |
| 4 | **Ilan point 11 / long eastern tunnel's western mouth** | A conspicuous rock-cut entrance; probable correspondence across several sources. | Already downstream of an intake and channels. No independently identified great stone or reason to call this the whole conduit's head. Retain only under a subsection-head model. |

All four remain **low confidence as the exact scroll feature**. The ranking expresses relative investigative priority. The existence of channels, tunnels and a boulder is much better established than their proposed scroll identities.

The settlement inlet remains a secondary semantic alternative if “head” denotes the beginning of an internal distribution channel. Humbert's English volume III A, Fig. 174 (printed p. 435), distinguishes inlet phases. It does not identify the required rock. Channel 500's later restoration cannot simply substitute for the earlier channel 673. The Ground-view cistern photograph remains contextual and is not an upstream intake photograph.

## Reading sensitivity

Puech 2006, p. 188, and Lefkovits 2000, pp. 185–189, were checked in the local edition page images. These are editorial alternatives, not fresh readings of the metal.

| Interpretation | Consequence for the candidates |
| --- | --- |
| Head of a conduit supplying Sekakah; “north” describes the cache's side of the channel | Points 3 and the upstream rock remain first comparisons. The local channel orientation and rock position must establish the north-side relation. |
| Conduit approaches Sekakah **from the north** | A northern-bank intake alone is insufficient. The system's approach to the settlement becomes the relevant directional test. No candidate is promoted by this reading here. |
| Conduit **west of** Sekakah | Compatible with the upstream Qumran works, conditional on the restoration and site identification. Puech's discussion invokes Qumran in weighing the restoration, so this cannot count as independent geographic confirmation. |
| Head of a branch/subsection | Raises points 7–10 relative to point 3. The long tunnel remains possible only with an additional explanation for treating its entrance as a beginning. |
| Landmark noun restored as a monument or another object rather than a natural stone | Removes the large boulder's distinctive textual advantage. The conduit-head evidence remains. |

Puech prefers a restored depth of three cubits on spacing grounds. Lefkovits allows three, five or six. No depth model here selects one restoration or uses it to define a modern excavation coordinate.

## Tunnel correspondence: a probable alignment anchor

The tunnel numbers run in opposite spatial order in the two plans. This is an inference from the drawings and route sequence, not a published concordance.

| Physical feature | Ilan–Amit 1989 | Reeder–Jol 2006 Fig. 4 | Magen–Peleg 2018 | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Western short tunnel | Point 10; 5.5 m (p. 284) | **Tunnel 2**, western of the pair | 4.90 m (p. 78) | Probable match from order, branch setting and relative length. Dimensions differ. |
| Eastern long tunnel | Point 11; 13.5 m (p. 285); inset shows two breaches | **Tunnel 1**, eastern of the pair | 12.40 m (p. 79) | Probable match from order, projecting crag and downstream turn. |

Reeder's labels were read on the figure itself. His prose follows the system upstream, so prose references to a “first” tunnel should not be joined to Ilan's numbers without the map.

Masterman's accessible tunnel is a further probable match to the long tunnel:

| Measurement | Masterman 1903, p. 267 | Magen–Peleg 2018, p. 79 | Comparison |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Accessible/long tunnel length | 43 ft ≈ **13.1 m** | **12.40 m** | Similar scale; not identical measurements. |
| First break from entrance | 11 ft ≈ **3.4 m** | **3.10 m**, explicitly from western entrance | Consistent sequence; Masterman's entrance orientation follows his route account rather than an explicit compass label. |
| Further interval to second opening | 12 ft ≈ **3.7 m** | **4 m** | Similar spacing. Masterman distinguishes a floor break and a higher south-facing window. |

Converted values use exactly 0.3048 m per foot and are rounded to one decimal; this does not improve the original measurements' precision. Quotations and page-level extraction are retained in [Masterman](registration/masterman1903_extracted.md) and [Magen–Peleg](registration/magen2018_extracted.md). Ilan–Amit's readings are documented in the [plan review](ilan_amit_1989_plan_review.md). Reeder supplies relative plan positions here, not another numerical tunnel-length measurement.

Masterman's separate blocked tunnel, reported as about 40 ft, is a mismatch with a roughly 5 m short tunnel. This unresolved discrepancy prevents treating his entire route as a solved one-to-one correspondence. His pre-1952 description supplies valuable historical observations, but later authors' use of Masterman limits the independence of repeated details.

## Entry 22: a testable extension

Under a **basin 2 = named reservoir** hypothesis, the long tunnel's two openings lie generally east of the basin on Ilan's plan. That makes them specific fissure candidates to compare, rather than an unspecified fissure somewhere at Qumran. Magen–Peleg locates the first opening about 3.10 m along the long tunnel from its western entrance and the second about 4 m farther on.

The match remains low confidence: no source independently names basin 2 as Solomon's reservoir; its impoundment depends on a lost, proposed dam; and repaired openings in an aqueduct need not be the scroll's fissure. Entry 22 supplies an eastern relation, not the tunnel offsets. This hypothesis should be tested separately from entry 21 and from the alternative settlement-reservoir model.

## Geometry and next discriminating work

The comparison uses manually sampled linework from Ilan–Amit's p. 283 image and Reeder–Jol's 1380 × 1044 Figure 4. Each drawing keeps its own coordinate frame. Reeder's main scale is approximately 173 image pixels per 50 m; Ilan's is approximately 180 pixels per 50 m in the inspected page image. These scales describe the reproductions and are not survey-accuracy estimates. The interactive crops do not use Ilan's compressed section around point 18 or Reeder's separately scaled settlement inset.

No affine transformation, GPS point, geographic footprint or inter-plan distance is asserted. The preceding aerial registration trial remains rejected. Plan-relative geometry is usable now for feature selection and comparison while preserving the unresolved ground position.

The next useful digital work is a **photo-to-plan correspondence for the two tunnel mouths and their openings**: Ilan Figs. 2–4, Magen–Peleg Figs. 86–87, and Masterman's Fig. 2 as upstream context. Once the tunnel pair is matched, trace back through the split to the intake and rock. This offers a discriminating route to reconciling the upstream features without requiring the missing survey files. Then check the construction sequence using the surviving channel-floor and repair evidence. The missing 2002 station data would improve geographic registration, but is not a prerequisite for this comparison.
