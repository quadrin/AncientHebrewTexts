# Entry 40 (IX 7–9): Beth-Horon, the Horites, and Elitzur's name test

29 September 2026. The [plate check](plate_check.md) leans to ים ("facing the Sea", west) at IX 7. That restores the textual premise of Milik's Upper Beth-Horon placement, so this review tests it again. Extraction notes, quotations and URLs are in [registration/entry40_elitzur_extracted.md](registration/entry40_elitzur_extracted.md).

**Result.**

- **Beth-Horon becomes a possible candidate, low confidence.** It is conditional on ים and on reading the article as standing for "Beth". Entry 40 stays **possible only**.
- **Naṭuf stays possible and low,** on the sequence of entries alone.
- **Jeremias's Horite tombs at Beit Guvrin are recorded as possible-low.** This is the new point: Milik himself judged this reading "slightly more likely" in December 1959. The project's files had not recorded it.
- **No individual feature is identified anywhere.** No report gives a chamber facing west, a channel (זרב) or a depth of 16 cubits.

## What changed the question

**Milik changed his mind before DJD III appeared.** In *RB* 67 (1960) pp. 222–223 he quotes his own manuscript notice for DJD III:

- The notice places the entry at Upper Beth-Horon. It says the word ים was chosen "intentionally" instead of the usual word for west.
- After reading Jeremias he says he "would gladly return" to "Tombs of the Horites" at Beit Guvrin.
- He calls the two identifications "plausible to about the same degree", with the Horites more likely "by a few details".

DJD III (1962) still prints Beth-Horon (Puech 2006 p. 196; Lefkovits p. 288). Puech reports the Horite reading as the one "that now has the editor's favour". This review did not re-read DJD III D32 p. 268; the Internet Archive copy is restricted.

**The yod/waw question does not decide the place.** Puech reads החורין (Horites), Milik prints החורון (Horon), and Milik 1960 calls the two "materially equally good". The Tosefta spells the town בית חורין (t. Niddah 8), where b. Niddah 61a has בית חורון. Both passages tell of a burial pit at "the rock of Beth-Horon". A yod therefore does not exclude Beth-Horon.

**Jeremias's proposal was made under ים** (*RB* 67, 1960, pp. 220–222):

- Genesis Rabbah and Jerome tie the Horites to Eleutheropolis (Beit Guvrin).
- A large shaft-tomb necropolis lies about 2 km north of Beit Guvrin, west of the Roman road. He takes that position as what "facing the sea" means.

Kokhim tombs are the ordinary Jewish tomb type of the period in Judaea and Benjamin. Their presence only shows compatibility at the level of type. It does not discriminate between the candidates.

**Sequence.** Puech's cluster for entries 39–45 contains no identified place name. It is anchored by Naṭuf (38; the feature is not attested) and Ramat Raḥel (46; low). Both Horon candidates lie 27–34 km outside it, and Beth-Horon is the nearer. The project's rule is that sequence alone cannot make a site best-supported. It is treated here as a moderate argument against Beth-Horon: enough to block "best-supported", not enough to drop it.

## Confidence by axis (Beth-Horon)

- **Reading: low–medium.**
  - ים leans on the plates, but Puech reports traces of דרום.
  - The toponym needs Milik's article-for-"Beth" rule.
  - Milik himself rated the Horites slightly higher.
- **Site: low.**
  - The name test passes for the place.
  - Upper and Lower Beth-Horon cannot be distinguished. The only tomb with pottery of the period is at the lower village.
- **Feature: low.** The fit is at the level of type only.

The atlas marks the upper village, following Milik (IAA survey point, ~300 m). This is a village anchor, not a feature.

## Elitzur 2004: the name test re-checked

Elitzur's method pages were read in session 2 (F2.21). This review checked his corpus and index against the project's Phase 3 results:

- **Doq (31): consistent.** He records the living names *dūk / dyūk* (p. 139 n. 2; p. 358). The note belongs to entry 27 (Adullam), not entry 28 as the project recorded. Dok is not in his 177-name corpus, so the "pass" is the project's application of his test.
- **Siloam (49): consistent, but only as phonology.** He treats Silwān as continuing שילוח (pp. 108, 298, 303). He does not discuss the identification, and Siloam is not in his corpus.
- **Bezek (59): stronger than recorded.** Bezek = [bzīq] is no. 62 of his 177 (p. 16). The "possible" cap comes rightly from the scroll's reading, not from the name.
- **Beth-shean (58): not consistent.** Bēsān is no. 63 of his 177. The project's "partial" on condition (ii) really reflects the scroll's spelling בית שם, which is a reading question. Record it as yes / yes with a separate reading caveat.
- **Achor, Secacah, Kozeba, Naʿaran: he says nothing.** The project's results stand.
- **Beth ha-Kerem:** the note is p. 185 n. 2.
