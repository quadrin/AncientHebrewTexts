# Stacey 2007: phases of the Qumran aqueduct (entries 20–22)

29 September 2026. The user supplied the article. It was read in full; no scan is added to the repository.

**Source:** David Stacey, "Some Archaeological Observations on the Aqueducts of Qumran," *Dead Sea Discoveries* 14.2 (2007), pp. 222–243. Stacey visited the site on 27 May 2005. His evidence is:

- de Vaux's field notes (Pfann's translation);
- Humbert and Chambon's plates;
- his own observations in the field (p. 222 nn. 5–6).

This is a different article from his 2009 *Bible and Interpretation* note "Three Notes on Qumran", which the repository already cites (F6.3).

**Result.** The article adds a construction sequence to the upstream system. That gives entry 21's "head of the conduit" a second concrete candidate, the head of the earlier run-off channel, alongside the dam intake. Site confidence is unchanged. No feature is identified, and no geometry is changed. The same result is recorded in [source_leads_2026-09-29.md](source_leads_2026-09-29.md), `tables/feature_constraints.csv` and F9.1, F9.7 and F9.10.

## What Stacey argues

| Claim | Pages | Kind |
|---|---|---|
| The main aqueduct is no earlier than Herod's reign after the 31 BCE earthquake. De Vaux dated it before the earthquake. | 222, 226–227, 238–239 | Stacey's argument from the field notes and plates |
| Inside the settlement the main channel was sunk into earthquake debris 75 cm or more deep. It cut the "Period Ia" floors in loci 100, 106, 114 and 115, and was never free-standing. | 223–226, 228–230 | Stacey's reading of the stratigraphy |
| Upstream, two building techniques mark two stages. (1) Earlier: "a simple, wide channel running down from below the cliffs to the site", a run-off aqueduct for rain on the slopes north-west of Qumran. (2) Later: a dam "across the foot of a waterfall in Nahal Qumran", plus a "technically more challenging" aqueduct, tunnelled in places, carrying water "from the dam to the beginning of the earlier aqueduct". | 228 | Stacey's interpretation |
| Stage 1 is located as Ilan–Amit 2002 "Fig. 1 no. 16 eastward". The run-off wall is quoted from Ilan–Amit 2002 p. 385: "A wall serving as a kind of low dam was built attached to the aqueduct, evidently in order to catch run-off." Stage 2 is shown by Fig. 1 and sections D-D and E-E. | 228 nn. 28–30 | Citations of the unread 2002 chapter |
| Ilan–Amit 2002 p. 385 assigns the aqueduct "without discussion, to the time of Herod Archelaus". | 222 n. 3 | Secondhand report of the 2002 chapter |
| Pool L 91 was dug with the main aqueduct and may have been its first end point. The pools east of it came a winter or two after the dam began working. | 233, 239 | Inference |
| No large rock-hewn underground cistern was built at Qumran, unlike the Hasmonean desert forts. The tower watched the path up to Hyrcania. | 236–237 | Stacey's observation and inference |

## Comparison with the Ilan–Amit 1989 plan

In the Hebrew plan already reviewed ([ilan_amit_1989_plan_review.md](ilan_amit_1989_plan_review.md)):

- **point 16** is the right-angle bend where the channel leaves the cliff slope for the plain;
- **point 19** is the wall joining from the north, which the authors propose was a low run-off barrier;
- **points 3–15** are the intake, the tunnels and the cliff-foot channel.

Stacey's two stages map onto this plan without strain:

- **Stage 1** is the plain section from 16 eastward, with the barrier at 19.
- **Stage 2** is the upstream section from the basin and dam (2–4) through the tunnels (10–11) to 16.

This mapping assumes that the 2002 English figure uses the same numbers as the 1989 Hebrew one. That has not been checked.

## Effect on the Copper Scroll entries

- **Entry 21 (head of the conduit), inference, medium.**
  - If the scroll postdates the dam stage, the head of the whole system is still the intake at 3/4, the current first comparison.
  - Under Stacey's phasing, point 16 was itself a head: the start of the earlier run-off line, and later the junction where the tunnelled channel fed it.
  - "Head of a subsection" (Q38) is therefore a concrete alternative with a place on the plan. Either reading depends on Stacey's unexcavated phasing.
  - The 1989 plan gives no dating evidence for points 3, 4, 10, 11 or 16.
- **Entry 20 (cairn or dam).** Stacey accepts a dam at the waterfall but, like Ilan–Amit, cites no surviving masonry. This adds no evidence on whether the scroll's word means a dam.
- **Entry 22 (fissure east of the reservoir).** L 91 is the largest pool and the likely first terminus. The article names no reservoir as Solomon's and reports no fissure.

## Check against the 2002 book

On 29 September 2026 the Google Books full-text search of *The Aqueducts of Israel* (no preview; snippets only) was used on Stacey's two citations of p. 385.

- **"Archelaus"** occurs only on p. 37, in a list of Herod's and Archelaus's aqueducts including the branch to Archelais, and on p. 53. Both pages are in the introduction.
- **"Low dam"** occurs only on p. 332, in a passage noting that at Hyrcania the aqueduct begins at a low dam that diverts part of the floodwater.

Neither phrase was found on pp. 380–386. The index may not cover every page, so Stacey's citations remain unconfirmed rather than refuted.

*Later the same day* a HathiTrust page-level search found "Archelaus", "Herod Archelaus" and "low dam" on p. 385 ([registration/ilan_amit2002_p385_check.md](registration/ilan_amit2002_p385_check.md), F10.8). The citations are supported at word level; their wording still needs a scan of the page.
