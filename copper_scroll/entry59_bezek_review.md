# Entry 59 (XII 8–9): Bezek, Kh. Ibziq and Zertal's survey

29 September 2026. This review closes the Phase 5 access gap for Zertal's Manasseh survey (Q25 limit; [phase5_summary.md](phase5_summary.md) §6). The user supplied Volumes 2, 3 and 4 as PDFs. The Kh. Ibziq pages of Volume 2 were checked on the page images; the other volumes were searched in their text layers. No scans are added to the repository.

**Result** (revised the same day after *HA* 40 was read; see the section below).

- **Kh. Ibziq stays "possible", at medium confidence.** An earlier draft of this review lowered it to low. The 1971 excavation report restores the period evidence.
- Zertal reports **no conduit, drain or channel** at either Ibziq site, so the entry's "great conduit" (ביבא הגדולא) is still unreported.
- **The period is now attested.** *HA* 40 (1971) p. 22 reports a robbed kokhim burial cave at Kh. Ibziq, with lamps and other pottery of the Roman period (1st–2nd centuries CE).
  - Zertal's surface counts show no Early Roman share at Upper Ibziq, apart from two illustrated sherds.
  - Lower Ibziq has no Early Roman pottery at all.
- **The tomb type matches Puech's alternative reading.** Puech reads the second word as בזך or כוך ("burial chamber"), and the excavated cave is a kokhim tomb. This is a type-level fit. It does not identify the entry's chamber, and it does not decide the reading.
- **The name evidence is late.** It is 4th-century (Eusebius). Zertal places biblical Bezeq at **Kh. Salhab** and has the name move to Ibziq at the end of the Iron Age. Salhab is recorded as an alternative, at low confidence.
- **No individual feature is identified.**

## Zertal's history of the name (pp. 104–107)

- **Canaanite Bezeq** (Judges 1:4–5) is placed at **Kh. esh-Sheikh Safiriyan** (site 2).
- **Biblical Bezeq of 1 Samuel 11:8**, where Saul mustered for Jabesh-Gilead, is placed at **Kh. Salhab** (site 23), "instead of the identification at nearby Kh. Ibziq".
- **The name then moved.** It "was transferred to this place during a later period (apparently at the end of the Iron Age) from the original site at Kh. Salhab. Then, it was preserved from the Byzantine period onwards."
- **Eusebius:** "two neighboring villages called Bezeq 17 milestones away from Neapolis on the way down to Scythopolis". Zertal takes these "with high probability" to be sites 42 and 44. He rejects Kh. Jabaris (site 69) as the second village.

**Kh. Salhab** (site 23, pp. 151–153):

- grid 1853/1957, about 3 km SW of Upper Ibziq;
- a 15-dunam tell on the Salhab–Beth Shean road (B7), with 30 cisterns and Roman–Byzantine walls;
- pottery: IrA I 3%, IrA II 2%, Per 5%, **ER 15%**, LR 15%, Byz 20%, EM 10%, MA 15%, Ott 15%;
- no conduit reported.

**el-Quleh** (site 43, pp. 193–194) is a hilltop fortress on the same road with "Hellenistic-Herodian" drafted masonry and a cistern. It yielded four sherds, "Hel (?) 100%". It is recorded for completeness; no edition connects it with the entry.

## Assessment against the entry

Reading dependencies are unchanged (Puech 2015 pp. 110–111; see `phase5_assessments.csv`):

- The toponym is הבזך (Allegro, Puech, Lefkovits), against הברך (Milik), הכוך (Wolters), הכרך (Beyer) and הבור (Wilmot, Wise).
- The second instance may be בזך or כוך ("burial chamber").

**Verdict.** Entry 59 stays "possible only".

- **Kh. Ibziq: possible, medium** (unchanged from Phase 5). The period is attested by the tomb. The landmark is not reported.
- **Kh. Salhab: possible, low** (new, post-Phase 3 revision), for the case where the scroll's Bezek names the older site.
- Both depend on reading הבזך.

The Phase 3 name test (Elitzur) is unaffected: Bezek (Elitzur no. 62) = Ibziq.

## The 1971 excavation (*HA* 40, p. 22)

*Hadashot Arkheologiyot* 40 (1971), p. 22, entry "ח' אבזיק", read on 29 September 2026. The PDF is at <https://publications.iaa.org.il/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1016&context=ha_hebrew_series>; the item page at `/ha_hebrew_series/1016` returns 403. The text was extracted through an automated reader and checked twice for wording. A direct look at the page image remains desirable.

What the report says:

- **Excavator:** ʿAbd el-Raʾuf Majed, an employee of the Staff Officer for Archaeology, excavated a burial cave at "Kh. Ibziq, identified with biblical Bezeq".
- **The tomb:** a hewn façade with a barrel-vaulted ceiling, whose remains survive at the corners of the façade. A small opening leads into a burial chamber of 5 × 4 m. Spacious kokhim are cut into its walls.
- **Finds:** square closing stones (אבני גולל מרובעות) by two of the kokh openings. Many fragments of lamps and other vessels "of the Roman period (1st–2nd centuries CE)".
- **Condition:** "It seems that the tomb was robbed already in antiquity."

Greenberg and Keinan 2009 (licence L-52/1971) list the site's major periods as Rom2, Byz, Med and (Ott), and its other periods as IA2, Per, (Hel) and EIs. Their grid reference is 187800/197100, the Upper Ibziq point.

## What would discriminate

1. A dated drain or channel at Upper Ibziq or Salhab. Gophna and Porath 1972 (nos. 51–53, 65) and Zori 1977 (nos. 50–51) are the earlier surveys Zertal cites.
2. The tomb's location relative to the two Ibziq sites, and whether any channel runs near it. *HA* 40 gives no plan; the SOA licence file L-52/1971 may.
3. An independent reading of XII 8–9, first letter by letter for הבזך against הבור.
