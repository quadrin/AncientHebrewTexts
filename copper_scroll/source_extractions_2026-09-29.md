## Results by entry

**Gerizim (57): medium, unchanged.**
- Magen's NEAEHL entry describes three Hellenistic staircases:
  - E1 ("The Bakers' Fort"): 15 steps, 8 × 2 m (p. 1745);
  - the southern wing of the sacred precinct: 9 m wide, seven steps preserved (pp. 1746–1747);
  - the eastern approach: up to 23 m wide and 34 m long, about 57 steps rising 15.5 m (p. 1747).
- The mansion (Area P), building IV, has "a stone-paved central courtyard with a cistern" (p. 1745).
- The city and temple "stood abandoned through the Hasmonean and Roman periods" (p. 1742). The coins break "from after Jannaeus until the beginning of the fourth century CE" (p. 1747).
- **No use or repair** of any staircase or of the cistern is reported after about 110 BCE.
- According to Magen 2021 (JSP 19 p. 58), the sacred precinct stood almost in its Hellenistic form until the 4th century CE. So steps and cisterns of the right *types* were present on the summit as ruins in the scroll's period (inference). They were not in use.
- One thin signal: three coins of Festus (58/59 CE) from a single locus in Area P, the mansion (JSP 19 p. 159). Magen treats all seven 1st-c. BCE–1st-c. CE coins as chance finds (p. 59). New question Q48.

**Hyrcania (16, 29, 35): possible, low, unchanged.** The link to these entries still rests on restorations and a disputed word.
- *Cisterns and pools.* Garbrecht & Peleg count 16 cisterns (about 16,000 m³). They add three rock-cut pools of about 18 × 15 × 5 m (about 4,000 m³), filled after the cisterns (pp. 168–169).
- *Bridge phases.* They date two phases of the 112 m saddle bridge "possibly" Hasmonean (four lower cisterns) and "possibly" Herodian (twelve upper cisterns) from the masonry (p. 168).
- *Two aqueducts.* Patrich 2002 describes two aqueducts: the N one is "1950 m long" and "almost certainly the earlier" (p. 336).
- *The N pools.* He also describes "N pools" that served "as a kind of moat" on the western approach (p. 351).
- *Capacity.* He cites Feldman's count of "20 rock-cut cisterns and 2 built pools" (about 20,000 m³) (p. 351).
- *Byzantine phase.* Byzantine repairs and a Byzantine channel are reported (p. 338).
- *Entry 29.* The "N pools" are a period feature of the kind entry 29's "northern reservoir" needs (inference). The pool count differs between the sources (new question Q49).

**Doq (31): medium, unchanged.**
- The water system (Garbrecht & Peleg pp. 164–165):
  - a diversion weir in a wadi;
  - a channel 500 m long;
  - nine cisterns, each 7–11 × 3–5 × 5–7 m, about 2,100 m³ in all.
- They add that Dok "is apparently the only fort that was not later rebuilt by Herod". This is a historical inference, not stratigraphy. It does not show whether the summit was in use in the 1st century CE; see the Q24 update.

**Ibziq / Salhab (59): possible, low, unchanged.**
- No source seen reports a conduit, drain or channel at either site. Only cisterns are reported: "numerous cisterns" at Salhab (Guérin p. 355; SWP II p. 240), and cisterns and caves at Ibziq (SWP II p. 237).
- *HA* 40 (1971) p. 22 reports a burial cave at Kh. Ibziq with lamps and vessels of the 1st–2nd c. CE. The report does not say whether this is the upper or lower site.
- Gaß gives Salhab at N 32°21′15″, E 35°22′25″ (Zertal 1853.1957). It is still not mapped.
- *On merge:* the parallel [entry 59 review](entry59_bezek_review.md) restored Kh. Ibziq to possible, medium after *HA* 40 and mapped Salhab at possible, low from Zertal's grid (F9.8). Gaß's coordinate lies about 120 m from that point. This run did not revisit the confidence, and its sources add no evidence against the period fit.

**Qumran aqueduct (21): medium, unchanged.**
- HathiTrust's OCR puts "Archelaus", the phrase "Herod Archelaus" and the phrase "low dam" on p. 385 of Ilan & Amit 2002. The chapter runs pp. 380–386 (inference).
- Stacey's two citations are therefore supported at word level; their wording is still unverified.
- "Point 16" does not occur in the chapter.

**Entry 40: possible only, unchanged.**
- *Jeremias 1958, pp. 82–86 (§32, on Micah's tomb).* The new point is the date. He dates the necropolis 2 km north of Beit Guvrin as "seleukidischen Schachtgräbern", i.e. Hellenistic (BK), so the tombs existed by the scroll's period.
  - He found it in October 1932 with Alt. It has about 100 graves, and he identified it with the "cemetery of the Anakim" of the *Vitae prophetarum* (p. 86).
  - His *RB* 1960 note, already read (F8.1), gave the place and a depth of 4.89 m but no date.
- *DJD III D32 (p. 268).* Milik prints Beth-Horon and adds "Une localisation alternative, assez attrayante: J. Jeremias, Revue Biblique, lxvii, 1960, pp. 220-2". So DJD III does acknowledge Jeremias, answering the first "Next" item of the entry 40 review.
  - The Addenda (pp. 300–301) do not return to it.
  - DJD III cites *Heiligengräber* only on pp. 271–272, for other entries.
- *Benjamin survey site 143.* The English online record (Beit Ur el-Fauqa [143], map 83/1) lists "burial caves" but gives no plans or orientation. The Hebrew record, read earlier ([registration/entry40_elitzur_extracted.md](registration/entry40_elitzur_extracted.md)), gives four undated bench tombs, also without orientation. The printed volume is still needed.

**Zissu 2023.** A synthesis with no coordinates or cave plans.
- It flags five Jericho / Wadi Qelt cave groups. One group has cisterns cut in hiding caves "first hewn out in the Second Temple period" (p. 203).
- It says nothing about the Buqeia or Naḥal Kidron / Mar Saba.
