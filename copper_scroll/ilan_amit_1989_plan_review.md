# Ilan–Amit 1989: direct review of the Qumran aqueduct plan

Reviewed 28 September 2026 from the user-supplied `trimmed aquaduct.pdf` (attachment `file_000000009e2081f891db5fde8f5b2027`). PDF pages 1–6 are printed pages 283–288. Page 7 starts the separate Ein Boqeq chapter; it is not part of the Qumran article. The six Qumran pages and their figures were read visually; the PDF has no extractable text layer.

Source: צבי אילן ודוד עמית, “אמת המים של קומראן,” in דוד עמית, יזהר הירשפלד ויוסף פטריך, eds., *אמות המים הקדומות בארץ־ישראל: קובץ מחקרים* (1989), pp. 283–288. The first-publication note cites *Teva va-Aretz* 24, Shevat–Adar 5742, pp. 118–122. Note 4 on p. 288 describes survey visits in December 1980 and January 1982.

**Result:** the original Hebrew plan is now available and checked. Figure 1 on p. 283 distinguishes the intake, reconstructed dam, branch, tunnels and downstream channel. It supports a more specific comparison for atlas entry 21, but does not establish a Copper Scroll identification. Neither site confidence nor geographic geometry has been changed. The 2002 English chapter has not been read directly, and complete equivalence between its plan and the Hebrew figure is not assumed.

## Effect on the Copper Scroll candidates

The atlas's numbering is retained here. The article uses a different paragraph numbering convention: its paragraph XXII in Figure 8 is the conduit-head passage treated as atlas entry 21. Compare the wording rather than joining records by the printed paragraph number alone.

| --- | --- | --- |

| 21: head of the conduit | Point 3, at basin 2 beside proposed dam 4, is the first functional comparison for the head of the entire aqueduct. The intake's location on the northern bank is a physical observation. Whether it satisfies the scroll's northern phrase depends on the phrase's referent and grammar. It cannot be equated automatically with water flowing north or an aqueduct approaching the settlement from the north.

## Source dependence and dating

On p. 287 the authors explicitly propose that this aqueduct, and perhaps the dam directing water into it, are the installations mentioned in the Copper Scroll. Their argument assumes Qumran's identification with Sekakah and cites earlier interpretation. It is part of the identification's source history, not independent confirmation of that identification.

Note 5 on p. 288 also invokes the proposed Copper Scroll mention in discussing the dam's substantial construction. The hydraulic argument from the differing levels should be evaluated separately from that textual argument. A hypothesized dam cannot be used to establish a scroll reading and then counted as corroborated because the reading mentions a dam.

Page 286 discusses a possible early channel associated with the Iron Age round pool, a presumed Hasmonean water supply, and the surviving aqueduct's attribution to the later occupation phase, with possible rebuilding of an earlier channel. These are the authors' chronological interpretations. This review does not convert them into independently measured dates for points 3, 4, 10 or 11, and does not resolve later disagreements over the system's chronology.

## Mapping consequence and next discriminating work

The source-access gap for the Hebrew chapter is closed. Geographic registration is not performed: a printed scale and north arrow do not supply the ground coordinates required to position a polygon reliably. There are no fitted transformation, error residuals or independent check points in this review.

The next useful comparison is to match the basin, northern intake cut and eastern tunnel to a georeferenced survey or reliably located photographs, keeping the lost dam as a reconstructed feature. Multiple distributed control points and an independent check are needed before the historical plan can support a map overlay. Point 19's barrier and point 4's dam must remain separate records.

For entry 21, retain point 3 as the first investigative comparison and label tunnel 11 as downstream. For entries 22–23, obtain evidence for the names and precise feature relations before drawing a reservoir–fissure or channel–landmark linkage. No deposit, hiding place, field observation or new geographic boundary is claimed.
