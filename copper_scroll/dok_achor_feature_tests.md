## Entry 31, VII 11–13: Dok

**Three spatial hypotheses must remain separate.** Lefkovits 2000, pp. 234–235, was checked against page images this review; Puech 2006, p. 192, was re-read against the earlier page check. This refines the previous generic “drying place versus guardhouse” constraint:

| Reading | Required feature | What would discriminate it |
|---|---|---|
| Spreading/drying place; eastern modifies corner | Eastern corner of an independently identified spreading area | A dated installation with evidence of that function, then its plan orientation |
| Guard post; eastern modifies corner | Eastern corner of a guard post | A dated guard-post plan and its eastern corner |
| Guard post; eastern modifies post | A corner of the eastern guard post | Evidence for distinct posts and identification of the eastern one; the corner itself is not fixed by “eastern” |

Puech argues for the consonants of *hmšṭḥ*, rejecting the guardhouse reading. Lefkovits discusses both the letter readings and the grammatical attachment. These are editorial assessments, not a new inspection of the metal. A military site cannot by itself settle either question. Seven cubits describes the vertical burial/digging instruction under these readings; it supplies no horizontal search radius. No conversion or ground polygon is justified before identifying a surface and feature.

**Source access corrected:** David Amit, “מפעל המים של מבצר דוק (דגון),” in *אמות המים הקדומות בארץ־ישראל* (1989), pp. 223–228, has a [public Kotar opening excerpt](https://kotar.cet.ac.il/KotarApp/Index/Chapter.aspx?nBookID=6765980&nTocEntryID=7085524). A table-of-contents entry is not full-text access. Water-system remains may help establish site layout and phases, but a cistern or aqueduct alone does not satisfy any of the three building tests.

**Candidate effect:** retain the broad Dok association and unresolved exact feature. The summit’s eastern extremity is not automatically the eastern corner of the required installation. Obtain Amit Fig. 1 with its phase discussion, and compare only after the reading review. Update the earlier specialist packet citation from Lefkovits p. 232 alone to pp. 232–235, especially 234–235.

## Entry 17, IV 6–8: Achor

Under Achor = Wadi Nuweiʿimeh and Puech’s cavity reading, the first concrete pair to test is **Cave V/49 and the neighbouring dwelling cave reported by Eisenberg**. This is a new investigative pairing drawn from an existing archaeological record; it is not independent new evidence or an established Copper Scroll identification.

Puech 2006, pp. 186–187, translates two cavities and a midpoint. His commentary argues for *kynyn*, retaining a cavity/chamber sense; competing trees and buildings readings remain in the project. Cavities need not mean two conspicuous natural cliff caves. The depth instruction gives no separation distance between the pair.

The existing Phase 5 extraction cites Eisenberg, “The Excavation of Cave V/49,” *ʿAtiqot* 41 (2002), p. 120: a northern-cliff cave used for dwelling and food storage in the first century BCE through the First Revolt, with a neighbouring dwelling cave hewn in the Second Temple period. This makes the pair worth examining. Food storage does not establish a drying installation for entry 31.

The [IAA article record](https://publications.iaa.org.il/atiqot/vol41/iss1/9/) was verified this review. Its PDF returned HTTP 403 through both the web reader and a normal direct request. [Aronshtam’s English Region V record](https://publications.iaa.org.il/atiqot/vol41/iss2/10/) is also available, but its PDF likewise returned 403. The Hebrew/English manifestations are one survey, not two independent confirmations. New plan inspection remains pending; archaeological details above come from the recovered Phase 5 extraction.

The discriminating extraction from the original publication is: the neighbouring cave’s identifier, both entrance locations, their contemporaneous use, connecting terrain and other nearby cavities. Test entrance-to-entrance, chamber-centre and accessible-route interpretations of “midway” separately. A pair selected solely because both caves are ancient would have many rivals. No midpoint coordinate is drawn until the pair and coordinate frame are verified.

**Candidate effect:** record V/49 plus its reported neighbour as an unranked feature-pair lead under the cavity reading. Retain the current valley-level candidate and confidence. Under a trees or buildings reading, this cave-pair test does not apply.
