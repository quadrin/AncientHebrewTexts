# Photographic reader: strip 13

The Scroll view includes a photograph of the original Copper Scroll in the Jordan Museum, photographed by Osama Shukir Muhammed Amin on 20 January 2020. It opens beside a modern Hebrew reading, morphology/glosses, the project translation and existing editorial notes.

## Coverage and evidence

Eight word regions currently cover VII 7–11: ככרין, במערא, בית, הקץ, כדין, של, בדוק and תחת. These are **provisional manual registrations and interpretive stroke tracings**, not a reviewed diplomatic transcription. The curved metal compresses the sides of the text, and corrosion interrupts strokes. The interface states this coverage and leaves the rest of the photograph unaligned. Do not extrapolate the overlays to the entire column or treat traced strokes as new evidence for a reading.

Line identification was compared with Puech 2006, vol. II, p. 413, pl. CCCLXXII (column VII), and p. 385, pl. CCCXLVI (strip 13). Those figures are not included in this feature. The Hebrew, glosses, translation and reading notes come from the existing atlas text data, with its existing source attribution.

The familiar copper-coloured Commons images “Part of Qumran Copper Scroll” depict a replica. They were not used as photographs of the original.

## Files and coordinate system

- `app/atlas-photo.tsx`: reader and controls.
- `app/atlas-photo-data.json`: source metadata, line/word references, boxes and manually drawn SVG paths.
- `public/scroll/strip13.webp`: photograph resized to 1600 × 2605, WebP quality 86; no retouching, synthesis or restoration.

The SVG uses a 1000 × 1628 coordinate system for the image, hit regions and tracings together. Zoom and pan change only the SVG viewBox. Mouse, touch and keyboard controls select words; dragging suppresses selection. The original-comparison control temporarily hides overlays and contrast adjustment. Reduced-motion preferences disable stroke animation.

To extend coverage, inspect the source photograph at full resolution, verify the line and word against the edition, draw only supported strokes and record uncertainty. Do not place a modern Hebrew font over the photograph and label it a tracing.
